# Tropical island flora cross-check: Tonga is a non-equivalent dispersal contrast

**18 November 2026 | Independent published source verification | NOT a replacement for the 22-region EuDiS analysis or global seed dispersal identification.**

## What was independently found

Fall & Drezner (2011), *Plant dispersal, introduced species, and vegetation change in the South Pacific Kingdom of Tonga*, **Pacific Science 65**, 143–156 (DOI [10.2984/65.2.143](https://doi.org/10.2984/65.2.143)), Table 1, reports the following numbers of **plant species coded for water dispersal** by source-defined plant origin:

| Origin on Tonga (2011 source) | Total plant species | Species coded for **water** dispersal | Descriptive proportion |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Indigenous + endemic | 364 | 137 | 37.64% |
| Polynesian introduction | 64 | 35 | 54.69% |
| Post-European-contact introduction | 309 | 137 | 44.34% |

Combining Polynesian and post-European introductions yields **172/373 = 46.11% broad water-dispersed**, compared with **137/364 = 37.64% native**, a descriptive native-minus-all-introduced **−8.48 pp**. The narrower native-minus-post-European comparison below is **−6.70 pp**; these are different source-defined denominators, neither a EuDiS seawater-dispersal contrast.\n\nThe same table explicitly allows **multiple dispersal mechanisms per plant**. In these source-defined classes the **native minus post-European-introduction broad water-dispersal proportion is −6.70 percentage points**. The published categories are NOT equivalent to EuDiS v5's **specific thalassochorous morphology**, which identifies traits such as air chambers/corky tissues favorable for seawater floatability. Broad water dispersal can include freshwater or coastal water dispersal and is not an observed ocean-current passage. The original 2011 species sets and origin categories do not match WCVP-v13 accepted-name botanical regions or the newer Tonga 2020 species-level source.

This **does not refute** the positive **native minus introduced +2.95 pp** in 22 EuDiS-covered WGSRPD3 island regions, because they measure different traits, ages of flora, origins, taxa and samples. It **does** warn against extending the 22-region result into a worldwide law before a trait-definition-harmonized tropical comparison. The prior 22-region result is preserved unchanged.

Original 2011 indexed full-text source: <https://pages.charlotte.edu/patricia-fall/wp-content/uploads/sites/697/2015/03/03-65.2_FallDrezner2011-143-156.pdf>. The article's Table 1 and denominators, not a new reconstructed WCVP analysis, are the complete basis of these descriptive proportions.

## Public tropical source for next taxonomically aligned sensitivity

Fall & Drezner (2020), *Vascular plant species of the Kingdom of Tonga by vegetation type, species origin, growth form, and dispersal mechanism*, *Ecology* **101** e02902 (DOI [10.1002/ecy.2902](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecy.2902)), describes **1,020 plant species** of which more than 450 are indigenous, covering **11 island groups**, with taxon identity, origin and dispersal modes; supplementary data named **`ecy2902-sup-0001-DataS1.zip`** (41.8 kB), metadata PDF. The published note says there are no copyright restrictions; nonetheless preserve author citations and site provenance. A full independent species-level 2020 supplement was **not obtained or validated in this turn**; do not report a new 2020 native-vs-introduced effect until its CSV schema and species identities are checked.

Candidate access and metadata: <https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ecy.2902>.

Other genuinely broader options:
- **GIFT** (Weigelt, König & Kreft 2020; Denelle et al. 2023) includes global regional floras and a dispersal-syndrome trait for ~9,600 species in the published 3.0 release; evaluate its **hydrochory/thalassochory category mapping and exact WCVP match**, rather than treating all dispersal syndromes as measured seawater transport. <https://gift.uni-goettingen.de/>; DOI <https://doi.org/10.1111/jbi.13623>.
- **PacIFlora** (DOI <https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.qfttdz0hd>) provides Pacific naturalized vascular-plant origin/distribution cross-checks but **not a prevalidated seawater dispersal trait**. It can test historical origin classification, not marine seed movement.
- **FIG 120 islands** (DOI <https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.v6wwpzh8f>) samples fleshy fruits and frugivores; useful as an alternate **bird/vertebrate dispersal pathway**, not a database of thalassochorous species.

## Required source-locked protocol before tropical extension enters the GEB main Results

1. Download the *actual* Tonga 2020 supplementary ZIP, preserve source URL, SHA256, decompressed member list, metadata, accepted plant names and origin codes. Do not invent a 1,020-row result from an abstract.
2. Specify **sea-current-adapted morphology** separately from broad **water-dispersal coding**, and permit multi-guild species without silently discarding them. Report matching rates per origin group; do not replace nonmatched species with zero sea dispersal.
3. Match accepted plant IDs to WCVP v13 and classify 11 Tonga island groups at their actual geographic grain. Avoid treating independent 11-island subgroups as eleven 126-region equivalents or assuming a single country is independent continental replication.
4. Recompute within-island origin contrasts, species/case recruitment uncertainty and alternative vertebrate-mediated guild prevalence. Declare that the 2011 published aggregate and 2020 species-level dataset overlap in author/source provenance.
5. Only after these source gates would a tropical matched comparison challenge or support a general **port–plant-origin–seawater-dispersal** rule; directional OSCAR+actual shipping OD remain independent, unobserved mechanisms.

**Interpretation:** The robust central story is a human-reassembled *potential resource layer* juxtaposed with historic/functional native flora. It is **premature** to claim that currents built all native floras, that their signal disappeared because of island speciation, or that shipping now utilizes the same sea-connectivity circuit causally.
