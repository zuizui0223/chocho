## Primary stricter geographic transfer
- For each of the original **eight** WGSRPD Level1 geographic groups (1–8), hold out **all dyads with either endpoint in that Level1 group** for testing. Train only on dyads whose two endpoints are outside the held-out group. Report training/test dyad counts, held region counts, the MSEs for M2 (original base plus unregistered-alien flora) and M3 (same model plus known HOSTS larval-host alien flora), and the signed percentage MSE change for H after O. Training and test pairs must have no shared island/region node from the held-out group.
- **Primary aggregate**: equal-weight each held-out Level1 group's MSE first; compute 100×(mean_world MSE_M2 − mean_world MSE_M3)/mean_world MSE_M2, once per draw. This gives a reproducible score but is **not** a cluster-robust independent effect estimator. Include the source raw original (unthinned) score and the **8 individual signed group effects**. Explicitly report Level1=6 (only two original regions) and Level1=8 (original geographic adverse test), rather than dropping them.
- Secondary: report the mean of the 8 signed relative gains, the number of groups with positive improvements, and the analogous MSE aggregate weighting each holdout by its test-pair count (not an iid pair confidence interval; cross-world dyads appear in more than one held-world test).
- For each proxy, report all 29 individual draws, their median and 2.5–97.5 conditional **Monte Carlo** percentiles, and the fraction of draws where the primary world-equal aggregate is positive; also report for each of the 8 groups median, range, MC central quantiles, and fraction positive. Do **not** treat 29 draws as 29 biological populations or regions as 8 independent trials.

## Decision and caveats
- If the original positive leave-one-region-out gain becomes adverse or unstable when entire world divisions are held out, limit the claim to local observational geography despite the previous 29/29 positive source simulations.
- If the whole-world aggregate remains positive, still **do not claim universality** if there are negative named divisions; an average is not guaranteed transferable to every new biogeographic region. Keep the original +54.9% potential-host resource and positive island/port/alien flora association separately, irrespective of this result.
- Modern HOSTS/GloNAF maps lack measured historical arrival dates. GBIF positive records are opportunistic and the thinning assumptions cannot identify true occupancy, population size, host quality, larvae survival, ecological homogenization or historical ship/cargo transport. Leave-late-effort/record-count original source covariates fixed while thinning synthetic responses is a deliberate retrospective model constraint and potential source of sensitivity.
