# Directional current progress and source-native plant dispersal traits

Exploratory follow-up to the integrated island butterfly resource paper (PR #179), frozen original 126-region dataset. The paper and source audit are unchanged. The earlier Atlantic pilot found no consistent positive within-family excess of thalassochory in recipient listing; the global pilot yielded only 12 nearshore-positive directions and four exact modeled coast contacts. Do not discard these results or lower their original event-model gate.

## Frozen new diagnostic (written before first result)

- Recover the exact 280 previously modeled 2005 NOAA OSCAR 15-m current trajectories, the original three release dates and valid-start preflight, the 126-region WCVP flora/geography and the original EuDiS v5 source-coverage tables. No new flow simulation. Verify the signed original artifacts and exact taxonomic source. Only paths with a valid day-30 endpoint contribute; censored tracks are excluded rather than assigned zero.
- Primary: original 22 paired trait-covered source regions, native source plants, known EuDiS trait 0/1. Require >=10 positive and >=10 negative plants in >=3 mixed-trait WCVP families per source. Recipient cohort is original geography- and coast-supported islands, not selected by positive current proximity or destination trait coverage. Release at most 50 km from the original GSHHG wet anchor. At least one valid 30-day release. Sensitivities: require two releases, 200 km original broad release support, and all 126 trait-supported source regions using the same thresholds.
- The predictor is **signed reduction in distance to a fixed recipient representative point at day 30**, averaged over available valid fixed releases. This is modeled current-assisted orientation/progress in km, not shoreline arrival or a biologically calibrated transport probability. Reverse-endpoint placebo uses the same net displacement at opposite bearing; it is not an independently integrated reversed ocean flow.
- For each source and recipient, use the *same* source-native accepted species pool for native and introduced destination listing. Within each mixed family, subtract the trait-0 share from the trait-1 share; weight families by their positive-trait source counts. The two responses and their paired difference are in percentage points. Missing trait is excluded; unlisted is not an observed biological absence.
- Minimum 10 independent source labels, 20 destination labels, 100 directed dyads for fitting (operational feasibility only, not power). If the primary cohort fails, explicitly report no fit; keep planned sensitivities separate. Source-equal weights, source and recipient fixed effects, cubic log initial distance, same TDWG Level1 and absolute latitude differences; approximate two-way node-cluster intervals; leave-one-source diagnostics. No claiming independent source replication from overlapping species/regions.
- Direction-specific hypothesis: greater modeled progress predicts larger family-matched positive-trait sharing excess in recipient native flora, and a positive native-minus-introduced contrast. This diagnostic can fail; a successful numerical fit does not prove the mechanism. All output directions and failed gates retained. No result-dependent threshold selection or automatic promotion to paper.

The analysis is exploratory because earlier data and adverse pilot results were examined. It cannot establish historical plant transfer dates, flotation/survival, actual ship or seed movement, or butterfly establishment. Fixed representative points, one 2005 current field, coarse 1-degree decimation and conditional valid-day30 tracks limit interpretation. Only aggregate result and coverage receipts are uploaded, not raw plant records or new detailed geography tables. Original source licences and manuscript access gates remain separate.
