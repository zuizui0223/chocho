# Original photo × historic butterfly evidence: Avalon / Santa Catalina

**14 November 2026 | Draft PR #159 | Source-locked observational evidence audit**

## Verdict

The best original-image case is a *Pieris rapae* larva **resting on a round, radially veined/peltate-looking leaf** in April 2022 in Avalon, Santa Catalina Island. The plant's leaf morphology is **consistent with *Tropaeolum* (garden nasturtium), tentative photo-only genus**, and this is independently a documented *P. rapae* host genus. However there is **NO textual foodplant ID or feeding observation** in original iNaturalist/GBIF fields. The April 2022 leaf is only partially photographed, so species *Tropaeolum majus* is NOT certified by this observation; a botanical check would be necessary before labeling it as a species-level observed host.

Crucially, this same butterfly was already catalogued from the island in **1927** and designated a **resident** in a 1984/85 published compilation. The later 2011–2017 initial *GBIF indexing* was **not a true historical colonization event**. This photographic case can illustrate a *port-nearby, possibly introduced garden plant × larval contact* but **not a newly enabled establishment, shipborne source, island relay, or actual long-distance crossing**.

### Photo inspection, separated by evidence grade

- **2020, original [observation 48671907](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/48671907):** a green caterpillar attached to a narrow/lanceolate leaf in a mixed planted-vegetation image. **Plant species unidentifiable from this image**, and mouthparts/feeding are not clearly documented.
- **2022, original [observation 113604519](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/113604519):** a green caterpillar sits on a broad leaf showing veins converging at a point inside the lamina, consistent with a **peltate** leaf. This is *visually compatible with* garden nasturtium (*Tropaeolum* sp., tentatively *T. majus*), **NOT independently botanically confirmed**, and there is **no confirmed feeding or development on this leaf**.
- Do not treat a larva physically on a photographed leaf as an experimentally identified larval foodplant. Photo composition can also place non-host foliage under an immobile larva. Any figure must be labeled **"candidate *Tropaeolum*-like leaf beneath larva"**, not "**confirmed feeding on *T. majus***".

Independent botanical references for identification features: [World Flora Online *T. majus*](https://worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0000459744), [Lucid weeds description](https://keyserver.lucidcentral.org/weeds/data/media/Html/tropaeolum_majus.htm), describing peltate rounded blades with radiating veins. Independent ecological support for suitability of this **known** plant host is [Renwick & Huang 1995, J. Chem. Ecol.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24234177/), documenting that *P. rapae* eggs/larvae can develop on *T. majus* in experimental conditions. This published host ability is **not evidence the photographed larva was feeding**.

### Distinguish two plants with similar English names

- **Garden nasturtium = *Tropaeolum* spp. / *Tropaeolum majus*, Tropaeolaceae** — the **candidate photo leaf**. *Tropaeolum* is listed by the [Catalina Island Conservancy's introduced flora inventory](https://catalinaconservancy.org/conservation/catalina-plants-animals/introduced-flora/), but this does NOT verify if the photographed individual was cultivated versus naturalized, or dated to 2001–2010.
- **Watercress = *Nasturtium officinale*, Brassicaceae** — a separately named introduced species in the frozen 2001–2010 Avalon-area WPI plant index. **This is not the photographed candidate genus** and the two English uses of "nasturtium" must never be fused.

The 2001–2010 5-km WPI records around Avalon (port **1012**, 211 general plant records) and Isthmus Cove (port **912**, 203 records) together yield **1 native + 4 candidate introduced catalogued *P. rapae* host taxa** in the frozen butterfly × island joined table. The broader listed flora near port 1012 includes *Sisymbrium officinale*, *S. orientale*, *Lepidium didymum*, *Nasturtium officinale* and other species, with statuses by contemporary **regional** WCVP geography. The frozen **port candidate host plant list does not index *Tropaeolum*** at those ports in 2001–2010. This is neither proof of local absence nor of a HOSTS database interaction omission.

## Scientific conclusion for the GEB paper

**Retain** this as an independently dated **historical resident, plausible garden-resource-contact case** outside the main causal or index-establishment claims, perhaps in Supplementary natural-history context. Do **not** upgrade the main GEB manuscript to say an introduced nasturtium plant enabled establishment on Catalina after 2011.

The next decisive evidence would be **verified plant identity in the iNaturalist photograph (independent botanical review)** and independently recorded larval feeding/development on that same local plant, combined with a host-population chronology in places genuinely without a preexisting butterfly population. A visually matching leaf and known host literature cannot substitute for the unobserved feeding event.
