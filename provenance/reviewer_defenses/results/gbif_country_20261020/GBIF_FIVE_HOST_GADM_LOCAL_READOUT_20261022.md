# Fine-grain host-opportunity geography exposes observational mismatch, not host-mediated recruitment

**22 October 2026.** Source-exact exploratory macroecology continuing the
independently audited 229-butterfly country panel; the **five** focal
butterfly×country cases were selected based on previous stage-label and
source-added-host classifications, not on the new plant observations.
The original GEB manuscript is frozen and unchanged.

## Result 1 — Most botanical source candidate country links lack an indexed record

Original checksum-restored HOSTS/WCVP/BCE host identities produce **eight
strictly accepted botanical species**, **20 butterfly×country×plant
candidate links** across the five source-selected added-resource
countries, and **26/48** reproduced positive added-resource country flags
in the original panel. Exactly **9/20** candidate plant links have at least
one GBIF record in the specified country/2010–2025 filters, comprising
only **38 indexed plant observations** across all 20 links. The other
**11 links have no indexed observation**: this is **NOT proof the plants
are absent** and may reflect sparse floristic effort, cultivation
outside published wild-occurrence datasets or taxon/source coverage.

All 8 GBIF plant species identities were resolved as exact accepted
binomials. All 20 source-matched GBIF botanical GADM1 facet requests
completed; even exact taxonomy plus a botanical range estimate does
not prove field availability to a larva in a particular province.

Two previously selected source-added-host cases, *Anteos clorinde*
in Cuba and *Papilio demoleus* in Cuba, have **zero juvenile-stage
co-detections** at GADM1 scale despite the original **positive
country-scale host-opportunity classification**. This is absence of
matching GBIF records in a GADM1 division, **not proven absence of
hosts or true larval–plant spatial mismatch**.

The Dominican Republic *Papilio demoleus* case accounts for **19/24**
focal immature-stage labels. It contains **13/19** juvenile-labelled
records in **5 of 10** juvenile-positive GADM1 subdivisions where at
least one candidate plant was indexed. The GADM1 intersections arise
from *Triphasia trifolia* (4 of those units) and *Ziziphus mauritiana*
(1 unit), while several other source-listed *Citrus* candidates have
zero or rare country records. This is not evidence those plants were
actually eaten.

## Result 3 — Compare juvenile labels against where all butterfly records occurred

As an explicitly **posthoc *data-location* reference**, we fixed,
separately in each butterfly×country case, the total number N of all
indexed GADM1-assigned butterfly records, H records within GADM1
units having a source-candidate plant observation, and j records
carrying source immature-stage labels. We then compared the actual
immature-label/plant-GADM overlap O with the exact conditional
hypergeometric reassignment of j labels among those N records.
This reference holds original administrative reporting geography
constant. It **does not** model independent populations, observer
sampling, probabilities of life stages, survey effort or real
host-dependent demographic outcomes.

| Posthoc source-reference comparison | Juvenile labels | Observed GADM1 co-detection | Exact count expectation | Excess over reference (percentage points) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Five preselected cases | 24 | **16** | **13.870** | **+8.873** |
| Exclude the single Dominican Republic *Papilio demoleus* case | 5 | **3** | **3.391** | **-7.821** |

Under this *toy exchangeability reference*, the upper-tail for observing
at least 16 overlapping juvenile labels is **0.2291**.
**This is not a valid biological p-value**: the individual GBIF
observations can be repeat sightings, life-stage strings can be missing
nonrandomly, and GADM1 botanical datasets have very unequal coverage.
The positive excess flips sign when one country/species case is
removed. No broadly supported juvenile-host spatial association has
been established.

## Ecological conclusion and evidence still needed

This is a **source-verified measurement result**: coarse host
geographic availability does not automatically provide
documented local co-detection, and even subcountry co-detection
cannot establish usable larval resource or butterfly production.
The five country cases are not independent samples from global
butterfly diversity. The original global country contrast still
has only **21 informative source strata** against its prospective
minimum of 50. This new selected-subset audit cannot replace the
missing broader biological contrast.

The next genuine discriminator is **host taxon × actual juvenile
record locality**, using validated host species and the provenance
of each larval sighting, plus a defensible non-detection sampling
background and dated host arrival. Point-level overlap alone also
cannot establish successful recruitment without followed larvae.
