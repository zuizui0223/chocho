**2026-10-27. This is NOT a preregistered revision of the first 13-event/15-literature-record stage screen** in PR #79. Additional original primary sources were located only after the first stage audit was finished (CI #40463837546), so the earlier **3/13** estimate is retained as **first literature pass**. This follow-up records a new, explicitly *posthoc* 6-source study batch with a distinct file and output schema. ## Source additions and stage classifications

For original 13 focal invasion events (butterfly × recipient):
- Chile / Pieris brassicae / Viña del Mar–Concón / **1971**: retrospective agricultural historical account says wild larvae actively fed on introduced Tropaeolum majus. Kew POWO introduced **Chile Central** and native Peru. Field larval feeding *reported retrospectively*; original 1971 primary notebook not retrieved. Counts toward site-resolved direct recipient larval evidence only with this source-tier caveat. Document subsequent 1972 spread into cabbage/cauliflower cultivation as **retrospective temporal contextual evidence**, not replicate observation.
- New Zealand / Pieris rapae / Pukekohe, South Auckland / **1993–1996**: original Cameron & Walker 2002 Environmental Entomology source collected P. rapae larvae on cultivated cabbage and broccoli plants in field, sampled weekly and separately reared; cabbage and broccoli normally Brassica oleracea, introduced New Zealand North Kew, but botanical species assignment from common crop names is not original voucher-based taxonomy. Count direct site-matched larval field evidence; cultivated crop ≠ proof of wild plant naturalization.
- Puerto Rico / Papilio demoleus / island field location **not specified** in accessible excerpt / original study published **2010**: Segarra-Carmona et al. 2010 and 2015 independent historical citation explicitly describes several hundred eggs/larvae collected from Citrus × aurantiifolia. Kew documents introduced Puerto Rico, cultigen E Himalaya. **Country-level recipient field evidence only**, cannot pass strict site-level definition until original sampling locations in study verified; no invented specimen collection year.
- Dominican Republic / Papilio demoleus / general source observation **site unspecified** / published by Wehling, Núñez, Glassberg in 2006: observed limeberry Triphasia trifolia as a caterpillar foodplant, Kew introduced Dominican Republic. Only **country-level host observation** until specific place/date/individual host plant documented.

Two non-invasion-event context sources, not counted as additional original event verification:
- Original 1998 Olivares & Hormazábal in Rev. Chilena de Entomología 25:5–9 reports indigenous Tatochila autodice blanchardi larvae co-occurring with introduced Pieris brassicae larvae **on the same introduced plant** Tropaeolum majus in Chile. This demonstrates potential shared resource, **NOT causal competitive displacement** (parasitoids, asymmetric oviposition also mentioned). Original specimen collections were January 1993 (not necessarily precise co-occurrence date).
- 2018 Negi & Joshi Journal of Threatened Taxa observed native-range Pieris brassicae nepalensis larvae on Tropaeolum majus in Uttarakhand, India; same host plant also introduced India, **record observed decades after 1971 Chile**, not proof prior identical host repertoire before Chile invasion.

## Exact comparison logic
- Keep first stage source readout **3/13** unchanged. Addenda unique primary IDs A01–A06 (do not duplicate first-pass literature rows). Do not double-count a source that merely re-summarizes the same paper.
- Two tiers: strict field evidence **site explicitly known in original named recipient**, and relaxed field evidence **country/territory only with demonstrated eggs/larvae but site unknown**. New strict evidence adds two original event keys (Pieris brassicae/CL and Pieris rapae/NZ); relaxed evidence adds two (Papilio demoleus/PR and /DO). Original 13-event cohort still 13, no negative inference for remaining events. Count unique events not source rows. Other ecological context (indigenous Chilean consumer co-occurrence / native-range India same host) is excluded from event numerators.
- Field feeding (retrospective), field larva on crop in contemporary study, country-level immature collection, local juvenile life-cycle and lab-only experiments are **not equivalent levels of evidence**. Report source type and inference limits.
- Plant origin from Kew modern source is never a dated introduction record, and genus-only source cannot be forced into species status. Explicitly distinguish butterfly-native versus host-plant-native geography; introducing a plant in a butterfly's native region can create host opportunities even before butterfly invasion elsewhere.
- No claim that Chile P. brassicae **newly evolved** Tropaeolum use, because India native butterfly populations use that plant and original Old World reports list it. No historical sequence or fitness estimate from spatial/temporally confounded case studies.
- Source breadth review is posthoc and can only report source availability, not ecological prevalence/probability or published finding of host switching; original first-pass total and addendum version must coexist.
