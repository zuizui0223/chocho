# Source-conditional field abundance pilot: Illinois butterfly larval-host sharing
**2026-10-25, fixed before examining any host-sharing abundance slopes.** Strictly exploratory observational modelling, separate from GEB. All original source program/year counts and species coverage inspected before this protocol; no effect chosen.

## Fixed scientific question
Can butterflies sharing known larval host plants be *positively associated in site-year adult abundance* (resource filtering) while predicting *lower next-year focal population-index change*, potentially consistent with competition? These are distinct statistical relationships and can both exist. Neither proves an underlying cause without local plant or larval stages.

## Programme choice independent of effect values
**Primary** `Illinois Butterfly Monitoring Network`: 433 sites, 2,245 site×years, 14,843 surveys, 122,272 raw species count records across 2000–2020, 50 original accepted butterflies, 99.56% source rows with positive survey-duration field, and 231 sites recorded in 3 consecutive years. Repeated Pollard transects with individual event effort and annual revisits. 
**External replication (same eligibility and estimand)** `Michigan Butterfly Network` and `Colorado Butterfly Monitoring Network`: 2011–2020/2013–2020 respectively, 47/48 matching original butterflies, ~99% positive-duration coverage and ≥300 site-years each. A non-qualifying programme does not count as a biological negative.
**Excluded** NABA/NFJ calendar-day circle count scale; MASSBfly opportunistic field trips, Shapiro (only 5 sites and no positive `duration` field), and other source-poor programs. No changes based on model sign.

## Exact fixed observation panel
1. Parse source CSV `4_res/complete-share.csv` in original ZIP; `program,site,event.id,year,doy,Reported species name,GU code,count,duration` required. Survey IDs must refer to one site, year, day and positive duration; if distinct counts repeat for the same species/event, use sum and record duplication, not double-count effort.
2. Restrict to site×year units with **≥5 unique surveys and ≥3 distinct 31-day DOY bins** plus positive complete recorded duration for every included event. Restrict sites with **≥3 consecutive eligible calendar years**.
3. Restrict to original 239 butterfly names present in each programme and with ≥1 positive source count during the eligible site's entire time series. At eligible site-years, fill an unlisted species as zero **only for site-specific species previously observed anywhere across the site's eligible time series and for all-species Pollard-transect programs**. Authors acknowledge mixed absence conventions; THIS remains a conditional assumption and may bias results.
4. Aggregate each species' annual counts per site, and measure log response `log(1+annual_species_count / annual_survey_hours)`. Survey coverage is *seasonally incomplete* in some years even after bin gate; count per hour is NOT an unbiased population density or UKBMS GAI. Report number of sites, years, actual zero records vs imputed zero and effort, host overlaps and eligible species×site×year pairs before inference.
5. Annual transition only if same site has eligible years `t` and `t+1`. Response `Δlog(1+count/hour)`; predictor focal `log(1+count/hour)` at t and (a) **average** `log(1+count/hour)` for all other eligible species at the same site sharing ≥1 accepted documented larval host (b) same mean for other eligible species at same site with **no** shared documented host. Both comparator groups required with at least one other butterfly. Species×site fixed effects (demeaning), year fixed effects, change in log annual sampling hours. Use OLS and site-cluster uncertainty to avoid treating thousands of species-years as independent geographical sites.
6. First-stage contemporaneous analysis: descriptive species-level co-fluctuation and host-sharing versus nonsharing (compute only if enough paired site-years); not a population-interaction effect. The lagged heterospecific coefficient is the **primary observational competition proxy**. An ecological claim requires a negative shared-host coefficient and corresponding shared-minus-nonshared contrast robust to site-cluster controls, matched source-quality controls, host-graph degree null, and independent programmes.
7. Prior decision gates: in any programme ≥20 host-sharing butterfly pairs, ≥300 eligible species×site one-year transitions, ≥50 independent monitored sites. If unavailable, mark `NOT_IDENTIFIABLE` or `INSUFFICIENT_SOURCE_SUPPORT`. No arbitrary fallback narrower bins/alternative exposure windows.
8. Null: degree-preserving randomized **butterfly–butterfly shared-host projection** with 199 independent 5×edge swapping replicates (seed 20261025), while retaining each butterfly's number of partners and all original counts/site-years; this tests simple degree structure only. It does **not** preserve botanical plant × butterfly bipartite degree, local host presence or phylogeny, so an excess beyond it remains exploratory and cannot identify competition.
9. A weak/null, positive, or negative regression coefficient is not proof of competition or release: time series exclude local host biomass, actual larval development and introduced-host dates. No cross-species significance mining, adjustment tuning, result-dependent programme changes. Inference is explicitly observational and report results whether positive or negative.

## Full hypothesis hierarchy
`resource present` (old GEB) → `field annual butterfly abundance` (new source) → `conditional within-site annual interaction signal` (this pilot) → `interference vs exploitative competition via host leaf biomass manipulations` (requires new experiments). Environmental filtering can yield **positive site-level cooccurrence and negative within-site heterospecific density effects simultaneously** without contradiction.


## Post-primary fragility checks — specified after seeing observational pilot, before re-estimation

The first Illinois model passed the predeclared eligibility gate (121 sites, 47 species, 9,178 site-species-year transitions) but the host-sharing lag coefficient itself was **+0.02194 ± 0.01717**, NOT a negative competition effect; the sharing minus nonsharing contrast was **−0.2835** (cluster-normal 95% −0.5132 to −0.0538). The model includes a site-year mean of focal+all peer indices, mechanically related to the same exposure variables. Moreover, **7,501** eligible site-year×species records were zero-imputed (45.6% of species-year index cells; source generally omits unobserved species), and Michigan/Colorado fail the original minimum of 50 eligible sites, so no generalizable competition conclusion is established. The first whole dataset re-estimation must never be relabeled confirmatory.

We fix two **posthoc failure-mode audits** now, *before their results*: 
1. **Omit all-species within-site-year mean log count rate** (the fifth exposure-control variable) while keeping the original 9,178 focal transitions, same effort and own baseline, site×species and calendar-year effects and site-cluster covariance. This assesses compositional/collider coupling to the nonsharing/shared exposure terms.
2. **Only positive focal counts recorded in the original source in BOTH adjacent years** (no implied-zero focal observations), retaining the original source's positive observation inclusion and same fixed effects. Peer terms can still contain imputed zeros; this is a bounded sensitivity, NOT unbiased survivor-only inference. Exclude if <300 eligible contrasts, <50 unique sites, or full-rank within-group design fails.
3. Report both choices side by side with the original coefficient, cluster-normal 95% intervals for shared coefficient and for shared−nonshared contrast, n sites/observations, and source flags. A sign-flip or interval spanning zero in any sensitivity prevents promoting the comparative difference as evidence for competition. Missing focal presence is not treated as verified absence.
4. Do not reduce year windows, season-gate thresholds, choose taxa by outcome, or reinterpret a nonnegative shared-peer beta as negative heterospecific competition. These checks challenge a pilot result; they do NOT replace the original locked protocol and host-degree network null requirement.


## Source-host-network negative control: implementation frozen before permutations

The original protocol requires a host-network degree-preserving benchmark before any niche/competition claim. We specify its code-level conservation criterion, after observing the original preliminary Illinois regression but **before generating random host networks**:

- In the original accepted `insect_host_accepted.csv`, retain all 239 butterflies and accepted plant IDs. Join each butterfly's `Family` from frozen LepTraits and each plant's `family` from accepted WCVP. If a family is missing/inconsistent, abort.
- Draw **199 independent** randomized host graphs (seed 20261025), each beginning at the same pinned network. Within each `butterfly Family × plant family` stratum, swap two (butterfly,host) edges of different plants and different butterflies only if the candidate cross-links do not already exist. Thus EXACTLY preserve each butterfly's host degree **within plant family and butterfly family**, and each plant's degree within butterfly family; preserve all taxa and adult site-year source records.
- Attempt 5×E edge swaps per replicate; minimum accepted 100, minimum changed fraction 0.10 of all **viable** stratum edges, and at least 190/199 valid complete model runs. If numerical gates fail, `NOT_EVALUABLE`; never loosen strata to obtain a favorable difference.
- Apply the same Illinois 5 surveys × 3 seasonal bins × 3 consecutive years and species-site imputed-zero rule to all 199 graphs. Recompute shared/other peer exposures and the exact species×site and year-controlled **shared-minus-nonshared lag coefficient**. Record changes in number of valid site-species transitions; reject structural-null interpretation if any complete replicate differs from the original observed 9,178 by more than 25%. Such variable-inclusion nulls still condition on the host-source graph and cannot be called a causal test.
- Compare observed −0.28350714681 with null quantile 2.5/50/97.5% and its descriptive empirical one-sided proportion. The null hypothesis is only that this **recorded network assignment** may create the coefficient without ecological interaction. Do not relabel the result a competition p value, whether it passes or fails. Code must independently reproduce original baseline coefficients to numerical precision.
