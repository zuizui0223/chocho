# Macroecological falsification: do potential introduced-host resource gains predict real butterfly adult population-index trends?

**2026-10-26; frozen before model coefficients were inspected.** This is a direct multi-species, multi-programme original-data test, not an extra literature review and **not** a test of an actual time-stamped plant introduction. The hypothesis follows the earlier 239-butterfly HOSTS/WCVP potential opportunity study. This analysis belongs on an isolated exploration branch, not `main` or GEB PR #38/#70.

## Core research question and direction

Does a species gaining more **potential larval-resource regions** due to the mapped modern native+introduced host distribution also exhibit a less negative **observed adult annual encounter-rate trend** in structured long-running monitoring programmes? Set the positive abundance-opportunity association β_gain > 0 as an ecological hypothesis to try to *falsify*, not presuming a positive effect. If no robust association appears, report **not supported by these proxies**, NOT proof host globalization had zero ecological effect.

This moves from isolated named monarch lab experiments to a macroecological analysis across many butterfly species and US monitored populations. A second readout asks whether species with the largest mapped gains can nonetheless be declining in actual repeated monitoring, but a raw count is descriptive and not evidence those introductions preceded monitoring.

## Frozen adult index panels and response Y

1. Parse the original ZIP `4_res/complete-share.csv` without treating each species-sighting row as a separate survey. Survey = unique `(program,event.id)` with one site, calendar year, DOY and a single consistent positive recording duration. Exclude events with source duration ≤0 or **>600 minutes**, as recommended in Henry et al. 2025 usage notes. Source details re-reviewed in programme audit.
2. Standardize within **each programme independently**: site×year has ≥5 unique surveyed events and ≥3 distinct `floor((doy-1)/31)` seasonal bins; site has **≥3 consecutive eligible years**. This exactly reuses the Illinois original field-eligibility logic; assert **Illinois 121 eligible sites and 851 site×years** after published >10h cap. Never pool unrelated source programme survey counts.
3. Focal butterfly species s enters a programme if it is positively recorded in **≥5 eligible sites and ≥3 different eligible calendar years**, has **≥15 site×year positive entries**, and its site×year adult index is evaluable on **≥30 repeated site×years** from sites where s was recorded positive at least once. A site×year lacking a species record is set to zero **only for source sites where s had a positive source detection at least once**; these zeros are *conditional survey nondetections*, not verified true zero local butterflies. Explicit source zeros remain included.
4. Annual adult index: `log(1+sum(source adult counts in species×site×year)/sum(monitored site-year survey duration in hours))`. Each site-year enters at equal weight (not raw insects or event rows). Species×programme annual slope β_sp uses **within-site fixed-effect time regression** `sum_{sites,years} (year−site-mean-year)·(index−site-mean-index) / sum(year−site-mean-year)^2`. Require at least 5 positive sites and 10 independent source site×year site-centered time degrees of freedom; source sites with under 3 eligible years excluded already. This is *an index trend*, not demographic population growth rate.
5. For a species in more than one qualified programme, **average its programme-specific β_sp equally** into Y_s (one species = one macro data point). Do not give species with more programme monitoring 4× statistical weight; the number of programmes is a model covariate. This is a sampled-population trend index across objectively selected states, **not** the U.S. national trend estimate and not actual reproductive recruitment.

## Primary macro statistical model

Species-level equal-weight OLS:
`Y_s = α + β_gain·X_s + β_native·log(native_resource_units_s) + β_host·log1p(resolved_host_species_s) + β_program·n_monitored_programmes_s + butterfly_Family fixed effects + ε_s`.

Family categories with ≥3 monitored species get distinct source dummy variables; smaller family categories aggregate to one `Other` (construction only based on species occurrence/eligibility, never on Y/X). If the design matrix is not full rank or has fewer than 25 independent butterflies, status `NOT_EVALUABLE`. Report adjusted β_gain as primary estimate in `log1p(adult/hour)/calendar-year` per 1 log potential range gain, not abundance percent increase.

Source-conditional uncertainty:
- **1999 genus-cluster bootstrap** replicates (seed 20261026), draw genera as whole groups with replacement, retaining all species within sampled genus, and refit identical OLS. Fail/record rank-deficient resamples; require >=1900 successful draws. Interval is descriptive among current monitored taxa, does not account full source taxonomic misclassification or within-site first-stage slope uncertainty.
- **1999 permutation nulls** (seed 20261027): shuffle species X values among distinct butterflies **within exact LepTraits butterfly Family**, holding adult Y, monitoring programme sample and other regressors fixed. Do not infer independent field randomization or plant introductions. Require ≥1900 full-rank draws. Report positive-side and two-sided empirical fractions as exploratory source-conditional baselines. A taxonomy-stratified shuffle preserves approximate clade distribution, **not** the causal date of alien hosts and not exact geography/native-range dependence.
- Descriptive unadjusted Spearman rank association with X, and positive/negative observed species β fractions by gain>0, as context only.
- **Leave-one-programme-out** sensitivity for each of the four original accepted networks with species Y re-averaged over programmes still represented. Hold all eligibility criteria and controls; do not replace dropped species by different taxonomic thresholds. If a sensitivity has <25 species or rank-deficient, mark NOT_EVALUABLE.
- **Host catalogue sensitivity**: repeat adjusted coefficient on `host_taxonomy_lower_bound_adequate==True` only if ≥25 source eligible butterflies; this is the source-confidence filter, not an outcome-selected subset.
- Quantify how many species×programmes, distinct monitored butterflies, genus groups, independent site-years, 0/positive-gain species, and observed negative/positive adult slopes the tested outcome truly covers.

**Decision:** to call a tentative multi-program cross-source association, require adjusted genus-bootstrap interval excluding 0, both within-family permuted reference tails aligned with sign, and all evaluable programme-omission coefficients same sign. Otherwise report `NO_ROBUST_HOST_GAIN_TREND_ASSOCIATION_IDENTIFIED`; do not say mathematically equal to zero. If one programme dominates or source zeros/gain exposure have no variation, report source limitations, not a novel biological law.

## Source bias, biological interpretation and stop
- The exposure is **global and time-collapsed WGSRPD3 potential host-resource gain**, while Y is a **2000–2020 sampled US adult index trend**. The predictor has no year of plant introduction and is not the number of locally available milkweed leaves, so this study is a macro **association falsification**, not before/after plant globalization or a colonization effect.
- Adult count/hour depends on survey method, detection, weather, flight phenology, zero reporting and sampling; all 19 programmes' records are NOT exchangeable. Restrict to the four valid transects with event duration. Event-season coverage across years may still change.
- Recorded hosts are sampled from the HOSTS catalogue and botanical ranges from WCVP, so source richness and regional opportunity are biased by literature/availability.
- Modern butterfly abundance decline does not imply that a specific introduced plant caused harm. Conversely no positive X association is not evidence alien hosts never feed butterflies.
- Need multi-site plant arrival dates, site vegetation/host phenology and individually monitored larval recruitment for a causal population-gain inference. Never multiply a named-monarch laboratory pupation coefficient by global host-regions.
