# Source-registered: do known butterfly larval-host portfolios predict independent field larval occurrence?

**Committed 2026-10-27 before computing any HOSTS×Caterpillars Count cross-dataset outcome.** Stack on chocho #80, do not merge into original GEB v0.3 and do not change previous native/alien HOSTS source nulls.

## Primary hypothesis and boundary

GEB chocho +54.9% documents reconstructed potential geographic larval-host resources for original 239 butterflies. The missing step is field larval realization: an accepted host relationship / its plant presence does **not** demonstrate contemporary insect larvae occupying a plant or recruiting successfully. A published independent 2026 Caterpillars Count! branch survey database provides a *field observation* outcome with standardized survey denominators. This dataset counts **all Lepidoptera caterpillars (moths and butterflies)**; not the 239 original butterfly species individually. Results are a deliberately labelled **proxy validation of the generic realized-larval filter**, not a quantitative validation of butterfly-specific establishment.

## Models frozen before results

1. Check author published reference rates only as consistency: 2026 full field-study subset ≈8.1% native vs 2.2% alien (68,392 surveys). Our Table S2 panel has only the prefiltered 100 species with >=40 surveys, so its weighted rates **will differ** and MUST NOT be presented as a new replication of the full published sample.
2. **Primary ecological opportunity-to-observation pilot:** among Table S2 plants with ≥1 exact original-239 known host link, relate branch survey caterpillar detection fraction to `log(1+#original 239 documented butterfly consumers)` and plant origin indicator. Fit binomial logistic model with original n_Surveys denominator, plus native/alien status. Across all 100 plants use a separate sensitivity where 239 source no-link count is explicitly documented **zero known links**, not a biological zero; label this a source-coverage rather than ecology model.
3. Countercheck larger original-HOSTS documented Lepidoptera consumer counts per exact field plant against observed caterpillar occurrence. This is a measurement *coverage control* and might be far more closely related to all-caterpillar field outcome; no species-specific inference. No model claiming non-native host is "available to all butterflies" without taxon link.
4. Original survey plant native status itself may predict larvae strongly (previously published, not new). Do NOT state a new native/alien field result. The new scientific element is the **previously uncombined, source-frozen host-link-count-to-field-realization cross-dataset test**.
5. Evaluate **incremental out-of-genus prediction**: leave each survey PLANT genus out, fit native-origin-only versus origin+log(1+documented HOSTS consumers) binomial logistic models on all OTHER plant genera, score held-out survey success counts using log predictive likelihood and report gain per 1,000 held-out survey events; negative means source portfolio counts do not improve prediction. No survey-event-independent significance test. This score is additional to the signed in-sample coefficient and prevents a source-association coefficient alone from being interpreted as actual forecasting skill.
6. Original sensitivity: equal-weight plant species Spearman(rank, larval%) versus survey-event-weighted estimate; both should not be described as independent inferential p values. Genus is the unit of descriptive nonparametric bootstrap (499 fixed draws, seed=20261027). Avoid naive binomial confidence intervals treating individual branch-surveys and repeated sites as independent.
7. Inference quality gate: at least **15** exact-match Table S2 plants with original-239 host links and at least **3 alien + 3 native** plants among those; at least **10 independent plant genera**. If below, report join bottleneck only, not interpretable alien×host mechanism. Do not drop criterion if it fails.
8. Model stability: design rank and native/alien overlap checks; no extrapolation to plant species/groups unobserved by original field census. Infer only an *association of original source host-list documentation* with all-Lepidoptera larval field detection, not true per-host resource quality or butterfly colonization.

## Important confounders and next conditional stage

Field observation is not larval survival, fitness or recruitment; same plant may host moths absent from 239 list. Original HOSTS sources are reporting-biased and plant taxonomy crosswalk incomplete; field survey locations are Eastern North America and primarily summer 2010–2024 woody plant branches. Source Table S2 has no latitude/site-level records and prevents independent same-site effort matching. Therefore no causal claim, no direct invasive butterfly use, no interspecific competition. Separate public 2022 Dryad source by Ferreira & Rodrigues provides two *Danaus* taxa with field versus laboratory native/exotic host performance, which can serve as complementary *fitness-stage* evidence **if its raw individual data can be independently source verified**; do not invent raw effects or outcomes from the article abstract.
