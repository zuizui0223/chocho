## Current decision

**Outcome: author-review candidate, not automatic replacement of the frozen v0.2 paper.** The alternative is coherent enough to read as a Research Article (~4,300 main words, ~250-word seven-heading structured abstract, 25 references, 3 proposed main figures), but the question of conceptual/ecological novelty still needs deliberate editorial judgment.

Working biological question: *When does an introduced larval host produce a genuinely new geographically available resource for a butterfly, rather than another potential host where a native resource already exists?*

The source scenario supplies:
- Original HOSTS panel: H=75,346 introduced host×butterfly×WGSRPD3 candidate incidences; A=46,387 already native-supported; R=14,406 extra introduced supports in new cells; U=14,553 first supports of distinct new butterfly×region cells (H=A+R+U).
- Independent Europe-based BCE/Clarke candidate evidence (81 butterflies, 1,027 additional links): H=103,349; A=67,740; R=20,642; U=14,967. Native-overlap fraction **61.6%→65.5%** and distinct-first-support fraction **19.3%→14.5%**: different incident denominators and source definitions.
- The 81 host-augmented butterflies' initial and augmented sets of introduced-added resource cells agree on only 3,225 of 6,445 union cells (**Jaccard 0.5004**), because 1,403 old-added cells are now native-supported and 1,817 other cells are newly recognised. These are **source label changes, not temporal colonisation**.
- Numerically qualified but post-hoc original-HOSTS climate-and-1,500-km matched null: the observed native overlap exceeds null median by only **271–276 host incidences, about 0.36 percentage points** of the fixed original H. This analysis was **not rerun** under BCE-augmented hosts.
- Independent occurrence check: **66 of 115** outside-native butterfly×region cells from a separately chosen 32-butterfly GBIF panel overlap added resources; continent-matched structural null median **49**, but no verified local larval host use or demographic impact.

## What is and is not novel

**Potential ecological contribution:** a fixed-link, consumer-centred geographic distinction between added host plant occurrence, already available native-host geography and newly opened butterfly regional access; linked to a transparent estimate of how incomplete host records affect that distinction.

**Not new independently:** that more plant ranges can enlarge a union, that non-native plants have ecological effects, or that plant introductions can homogenise plant and animal interaction networks. The equality H=A+R+U is **bookkeeping**, not a tested biological mechanism. The **50% host-source identity discordance** is an empirical sensitivity of one Europe-biased catalogue, not a global ecological phenomenon or causal range shift. The **0.36-point** constrained structural-null deviation is numerically reproducible but small and source-conditional.

The current strongest paper argument is therefore an **ecologically motivated inference-limit and opportunity-geography comparison**. Avoid adjectives such as 'dramatic ecological filtering', 'unexpected butterfly spread', 'universal host-network fragility', 'increased competition' or 'confirmed anthropogenic butterflies'.

## Reviewer objections to address *before* changing the frozen manuscript

1. **Mechanism:** The measured response is potential regional resource *access*, not survival or recruitment. Does the Introduction and Discussion make the implied consumer mechanism a *future field hypothesis* rather than a result? The full draft explicitly says yes.
2. **Representativeness:** Original 239 butterflies were selected by documented native resources from 339 descriptors. BCE augmentation covers 81 with European foodplant evidence; conclusions are **not probability estimates for the world's butterflies**.
3. **Source extrapolation:** Clarke/BCE evidence is regionally documented; linking each candidate host globally to WCVP accepted plant ranges assumes the link may be transferable. It does **not** certify butterfly host acceptance in every region.
4. **Metric unit mixing:** A/H and U/H partition host×butterfly×region *incidences*, whereas resource expansion counts unique butterfly×region cells; captions and legends must state both denominators.
5. **Counterfactual robustness:** The small original-HOSTS local-null residual has no BCE-aware matched counterpart; the +3.98-percentage-point source descriptive change and +0.36-percentage-point null deviation are **different estimands**.
6. **Spatial and temporal structure:** WCVP introduced vs native status does not carry introduction time; WGSRPD3 is geographically coarse; CHELSA 1981–2010 is static; raw occurrence records and botanical reference coverage are uneven.
7. **Significance:** Serial-swap MCMC worlds are correlated and the spatial protocol was decided post-hoc. Numerical mixing passed a finite gate but is neither exact inference nor a predeclared ecological hypothesis test.
8. **Journal fit:** GEB requires broad ecological/biogeographic conceptual relevance, not simply a more reliable checklist audit. The manuscript must lead with **what resource novelty means for consumers** and avoid making data-quality differences sound like observed ecological change.

## 18 October 2026: source-quality and recruitment sign boundaries — author decision

**New conceptual opportunity:** the working butterfly paper should distinguish three different mathematical or ecological objects instead of using 'host availability' for all of them: (1) the *contemporary set union* of regional host plant occurrences, monotonically expanded as plant-host records are added; (2) the *newness classification* relative to a native-resource host inventory, which can be non-monotonic when native and contemporary catalogues change together; and (3) *realized egg-to-recruit or source-population output*, whose sign can depend on oviposition diversion, independent host performance, season, parasite load and dispersing breeders. These last mechanisms require independent field data; the global 239-butterfly dataset does not observe them.

**Actually confirmed single-species new source-sensitivity:** [quality-filtered monarch result, original published appendix and plant-map hashes frozen](results/butterfly_monarch_independent_host_quality_resource_novelty_v0.1.json), qualified in [successful run 39096026037](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/39096026037). `Danaus plexippus` has 40 accepted HOSTS species, 181 native, 263 contemporary and **82 introduced-added regions**. The independent Greenstein et al. (2022) PLOS S1 classification covers 35/40 strict binomials; high H1–H3 includes 23 HOSTS plants and supplies **78/82** original added cells, while strictly high H3 includes 8 plants and supplies **37/82**. **The high-only restricted new area increases 82→101**, because the *native baseline shrinks* 181→152 versus contemporary 263→253. This does **not** mean actual monarch breeding range or recruitment grew; it is a genuine empirically sourced **resource classification reversal** from an independently documented quality classification, conditional on propagating quality status across coarse plant distribution cells. PLOS labels include two source `nonhosts` and two `unsubstantiated` within HOSTS; five HOSTS plants lack exact PLOS name matches. Do not collapse all ambiguous plants into 'poor performers' or assert the PLOS classification is valid at every WGSRPD3 region.

**Falsifiable demographic threshold (NOT fitted):** for season `t`, adult egg budget `E_t`, fraction `q_t` shifted from native to introduced plant and per-egg recruit success `w_N(t)` vs `w_I(t)`: `Delta recruits = sum_t E_t q_t [w_I(t)-w_N(t)]`. If no breeder or egg redistribution, competition feedback, loss of native source or other adverse effect, merely adding an unused bad host cannot reduce reproduction: the original native source remains, and optional non-negative extra recruitment is monotonic. If eggs are diverted and `w_I<w_N`, recruitment can decrease. If immigrants are drawn into a less productive U-region from a more productive established source, the **metapopulation** can lose even though the U-patch has locally positive reproduction. Seasonal sign switching and loss of temporal buffering are hypotheses, not numerical study results. [Code and rigorous synthetic-only tests](../../src/butterfly_specialization_ecology/host_realization_nonmonotonicity.py) document the assumptions.

**Conservation relevance by the already published A/R/U accounting:**
- **A, already native-supported resource region**: restoring or improving the locally usable native host may prevent attractive inferior introduced plants from drawing eggs away, *provided actual oviposition redistribution and poorer recruit performance are measured*. Coarse A-region membership alone is insufficient to infer a trap.
- **R, multiple introduced hosts support one introduced-added region**: resource richness is only **structural** redundancy. Conservation benefits require functionally effective, temporally complementary host availability; multiple co-failing hosts or a tempting poor-quality host do not guarantee robustness.
- **U, only newly supported region**: may provide an actual colonisation opportunity if movement, survival and reproduction are all adequate; conversely may create management-sensitive dependency or an attractive source–sink. Restoring native alternatives **before** wholesale removal of an introduced breeding host may be necessary in locally dependent populations, while invasive-plant harms to the native ecosystem must also be weighed. No global directive to retain or remove alien plants follows.

This is an **ecological theory bridge and a one-butterfly independent quality-source sensitivity**, not yet an original replicated global discovery that plant introductions reduce butterfly populations. Examples of real preference/performance traps and management-sensitive host shifts are already known (Singer & Parmesan 2018; Yoon & Read 2016; Thiemann & Cipollini 2025; Greenstein et al. 2022). The additional scientific advance would be a *multi-butterfly independently validated test of where the sign changes*: host identity and local native alternatives, female egg allocation, survival to an actual reproductive stage, and seasonal/landscape connectivity, with held-out observations compared to simple host-presence-only macro prediction.
