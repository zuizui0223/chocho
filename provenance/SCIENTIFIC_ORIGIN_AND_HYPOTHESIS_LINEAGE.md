# Scientific origin and hypothesis lineage

Status: internal provenance for the butterfly ecology paper. This document is not part of the manuscript narrative and is not intended to be cited as a scientific result.

## Independence of the paper

The present study is an independent ecological analysis of butterfly specialization, host-resource geography, anthropogenic host redistribution and climate-associated filtering.

The analysis assets originated from earlier cross-species method-development work in which larval host-resource geography had been constructed as an external ecological descriptor. In that earlier use, similarity in host-resource geography did not detectably predict the separate response for which it had been built. That earlier response is not analysed, reinterpreted or rescued in the present paper.

The scientific transition occurred when the host-resource reconstruction itself was inspected as an ecological object. It revealed structure that was biologically interesting independently of its original use: taxonomic host breadth, resource geography and host-species portfolio structure were not interchangeable.

Exact migration/source provenance is retained separately in `MIGRATION_PROVENANCE.md` and `SOURCE_SNAPSHOT.txt`.

## Discovery chronology

### Stage 0 — resource geography became available as an ecological object

A global butterfly host-resource reconstruction linked LepTraits host breadth, HOSTS larval-host interactions and WCVP plant distributions. The resulting species descriptors separated at least three quantities that are often collapsed into one specialist–generalist label:

- number of host-plant families;
- number of resolved host species;
- geographic breadth of the union of host distributions.

No genetic or other precursor response is used anywhere in the ecological analyses reported in the paper.

### Stage 1 — taxonomic and geographic specialization were found not to be interchangeable

Exploratory inspection of the exact resource reconstruction asked whether host-family breadth adequately represented geographic larval-resource breadth.

This was a descriptive ecological question rather than a prospectively directional hypothesis test.

Observed pattern:
- full resource-eligible panel: n = 239, Spearman rho = 0.276;
- conservative host-taxonomy subset: n = 215, rho = 0.398;
- 18 one-family butterflies nevertheless fell in the upper quartile of geographic resource breadth.

This established the first ecological separation: taxonomic specialization does not uniquely determine resource geography.

### Stage 2 — anthropogenic host redistribution became the central global-change question

Before opening the full native-versus-contemporary reconstruction result, the following exploratory question was frozen:

**Q1. Does anthropogenic redistribution of larval host plants disproportionately expand geographic resource opportunity for butterflies with broader taxonomic host breadth?**

Exploratory expectation:
- broader host-family diets should have more opportunities for at least one host lineage to acquire a wide introduced range;
- therefore proportional resource expansion was expected to increase with host-family breadth.

Observed outcome:
- 206/239 species expanded;
- aggregate species × region units increased from 26,530 to 41,083 (+54.9%);
- proportional expansion was almost unrelated to host-family breadth (rho = 0.008).

Thus the large anthropogenic expansion was real in the reconstruction, but the expected proportional generalist advantage was not observed.

### Stage 3 — the unexpected aggregate result generated the host-portfolio mechanism hypothesis

Only after the near-zero aggregate breadth–expansion association was known was the next exploratory mechanism hypothesis generated.

**Q2. Why can taxonomic host specialists and generalists gain similar proportional geographic resource opportunity from anthropogenic host redistribution?**

Fresh post-result exploratory hypothesis:
- added geographic opportunity may be dominated by one or a few highly redistributed host species in specialists;
- broader generalists may reach a similar total gain by accumulating smaller contributions across a larger host portfolio.

The host-level fractional-credit decomposition was then defined before opening the host-contribution result.

Observed pattern in the conservative expanded subset (n = 191):
- median effective contributor number: 1.67 in one-family butterflies versus 5.23 in 6+ family butterflies;
- median maximum single-host share: 74.7% versus 31.3%.

This became the main explanatory result of the paper: similar aggregate expansion can arise through contrasting portfolio architectures.

### Stage 4 — the portfolio result generated a within-category hierarchy question

After the host-contribution result showed that resolved host-species richness tracked portfolio architecture more strongly than family breadth, a second post-result exploratory question was frozen.

**Q3. Within a fixed level of host-family breadth, does host-species richness still predict the architecture of anthropogenic geographic resource opportunity?**

Fresh exploratory hypothesis:
- even within a single family-breadth category, richer host-species portfolios should produce more distributed anthropogenic opportunity.

Primary descriptive focus:
- expanded one-family butterflies, n = 82;
- resolved host-species richness ranged from 1 to 37.

Observed pattern:
- host richness versus effective contributor number: rho = 0.734;
- host richness versus maximum single-host share: rho = -0.707.

This motivated the hierarchical interpretation of specialization. Because the concentration metrics are structurally bounded by contributor count, these associations are treated as architecture decomposition rather than independent causal effects of richness.

### Stage 5 — an independent climate test bounded the resource-side story

A small exploratory climate pilot generated a separate ecological prediction, after which pilot species were excluded and a new panel was frozen before independent occurrence/climate responses were opened.

**Q4. Within contemporary larval host-resource opportunity, is climate-associated filtering common?**

This is reported as an independent cross-fit descriptive result.

Observed pattern:
- 24 species were climate-informative after frozen quality gates and uniform technical completion;
- median filtering score = 0.801;
- 23/24 species had scores above the neutral value 0.5.

**Q5. Does broader host-family breadth weaken climate-associated filtering within available host-resource geography?**

Prospectively frozen pilot-derived hypothesis:
- host specialists may possess geographically broad resources but remain restricted to a narrower climatic subset;
- broader host generalists were therefore predicted to show weaker climate filtering.

Independent primary outcome:
- partial Spearman = -0.166 after controlling contemporary resource breadth;
- one-sided residual-permutation p = 0.2237;
- decision: hypothesis not supported.

No replacement predictor was selected from the same independent response to rescue this result.

## Paper presentation order versus discovery order

The manuscript does **not** narrate the chronological development sequence above.

For clarity and novelty, the paper presents the evidence in the following order:

1. anthropogenic host redistribution strongly expands reconstructed resource geography;
2. comparable aggregate expansion is assembled through contrasting specialist/generalist portfolios;
3. taxonomic host breadth only partly tracks geographic resource breadth;
4. hierarchy persists within fixed family-breadth categories;
5. climate-associated filtering is common, but the predicted release with broader diets is not supported.

This presentation order is editorial. It does not alter when each question or hypothesis was generated.

## Interpretation boundary

The paper should be readable without any knowledge of the earlier method-development context.

The origin history is useful only for:
- auditing why the resource reconstruction existed before the present ecological questions;
- distinguishing pre-result questions from post-result exploratory hypotheses;
- documenting that the independent climate hypothesis was not generated from its own test panel;
- showing that no unrelated precursor response is being reinterpreted as evidence for the present ecology paper.

The manuscript itself should motivate all questions from ecology and global change, not from the history of the analysis pipeline.
