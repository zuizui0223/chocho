# EU butterfly climate tracking × host geography — historical falsification pilot

**28 October 2026; exploratory pilot, source gate frozen before retrieving any GBIF outcomes.** The original chocho GEB manuscript and `main` are unchanged; this analysis is stacked on prior draft PR #88, not the unrelated monarch climate branches. Source data and predictions from separate branches are not assumed to be the same temporal estimand.

## Scientific question

Does a source-verified *pre-existing* larval host opportunity map predict subsequently observed butterfly range-front recording **beyond** historical climate, survey effort, generic non-host vegetation and land use? Are host opportunities demonstrably maintained by humans more predictive of climate tracking than the purely wild host flora, after accounting for diet breadth, species, dispersal, habitat and original butterfly occurrence evidence?

**Avoid a tautology:** climate itself shifting northwards, hosts occurring where butterflies occur, and crop patches in future maps are insufficient evidence. Predictions must be frozen using information available in the *training* era. Any model with contemporaneous post-hoc host distributions in the training predictor, any evaluation against pseudoabsence generated by recording gaps, or any anthropogenic host classification derived solely from WCVP `introduced` is invalid for the intended mechanism.

## Crucial prior art

Rashid et al. (2026), *Diversity and Distributions* doi:10.1111/ddi.70245 studied **118 Europe-centred butterfly species** for host-constrained thermal-range margins and future static-host range constraints. Therefore, 'multi-species host-limited climate projections' is **not new**. The possible novelty is whether *empirically dated and anthropogenically maintained larval host resources* explain **independently held-out past butterfly range shifts** and outperform climate + survey-effort and ordinary vegetation controls. UKBMS data, where available, are a stronger historical occurrence/effort source than undifferentiated GBIF records (UKBMS GBIF registry doi:10.15468/gmqvmk). The raw pan-European eBMS transect detail may require access agreements; eBMS has major publicly documented monitoring effort but not open unbounded raw site histories.

## Fixed first source-eligibility pilot (no ecological result until later)

1. **39 named Europe-associated species** chosen from the already-frozen chocho 239-species panel, before historical GBIF responses, deliberately including 1-family hosts and broad-host taxa. No candidate is substituted because a query is sparse or gives an inconvenient pattern. These 39 names and their GBIF taxon keys are resolved ONLY from `data/exploratory/gbif_country_20261020/original_239_taxon_manifest.json`; never use a live taxon match that changes the frozen key. Retain preselected names with unresolved original taxon keys as **UNSCORABLE**, without rematching, replacement or a fabricated zero; reject duplicated or colliding resolved GBIF keys. The first original-only identity gate identified *Lasiommata megera* as TAXON_UNRESOLVED; this correction is documented before any historical GBIF response. The original HOSTS host breadth here is a source catalogue index, not validated host-plant performance or anthropogenic dependency.
2. **Original GBIF occurrence country/basis-of-record FACETS ONLY**, no occurrence rows, with `hasCoordinate=true`, `hasGeospatialIssue=false`, `occurrenceStatus=PRESENT`, fixed taxon key and years. Periods: **1970–1990** (historic primary), **1991–2005** (pre-stated intermediate data availability check), **2010–2024** (observed later source). Eight preselected countries: **GB, DE, NL, FR, SE, ES, IT, PL**. The GBIF public worldwide country facets are queried once per species × window (39×3=117); exact returned country and basis facets are saved as derived counts, with query hashes, full response checksums and failures. Do not silently interpret zero GBIF records as biological absence.
3. **Prospective historical feasibility information gate:** ≥25 of 39 species have an original 1970–1990 source country with ≥20 indexed point records AND ≥50 records in the SAME country in 2010–2024; such pairs collectively span ≥5 of the eight target countries. This tests only whether a long-term hindcast is worth fetching at finer grid/time and determining effort. If it fails, the historical design is not suitable for the proposed macro test at that window. 1991–2005 is a separately labeled sensitivity, never an outcome-chosen replacement primary.
4. **Survey-process diagnostic:** total GBIF records and `HUMAN_OBSERVATION`, `PRESERVED_SPECIMEN` and other basis-of-record counts by period, plus UKBMS dataset-specific source availability if possible. Explicitly report specimen/human mix by period: no automatic normalization turns opportunistic GBIF occurrences into occupancy or counts.

## Next test only IF source quality and historical effort pass

Keep a grid (e.g., 50-km equal-area) fixed for both eras; require independently verified target-group butterfly surveillance in both eras and ideally repeated-sample NBN/UKBMS transects for the UK. Model newly detected grid occupancy conditional on a **well-surveyed** grid, distinguishing first recording from biological colonization. Use preperiod species×grid climate from year-matched E-OBS/ERA5 (not a single 1970–2000 WorldClim climatology as a measure of temporal warming). Compare four alternatives, all fitted in the early data and judged on later held-out grid×year / survey effort:

- M0: lagged climate + historical species observation + site geography + effort + original habitat/land use.
- M1: M0 + dated original **wild native** larval-host resources, measured at or before prediction origin.
- M2: M1 + independently dated **wild introduced** hosts (not equated with planting or human support).
- M3: M2 + **verifiably planted/maintained larval hosts**, tied to contemporaneous cropping, forestry/ornamental/grassland management evidence at cell and decade. Unknown maintained status remains UNKNOWN; do not relabel all WCVP-introduced plants as cultivated.

Focal prediction improvement = negative held-out log-loss versus M0 and M1 after spatial and temporal blocking, plus habitat/land-use and non-host-plant placebo, host-shuffle within geography/source-effort stratification, leakage tests and observer/platform effort. Evidence must demonstrate *added predictive skill about butterfly outcomes* and nontrivial interacting warming×host exposure, not just that adding plants improves apparent niche maps. Apply original host breadth (1 family or >1) as explanatory/interaction covariate, not a proof of colonization speed. If source records only provide a contemporary species plant list or a WCVP native/introduced *current geographic label* with no dates, this M1–M3 temporal comparison is **NOT IDENTIFIABLE** and cannot be made as an ecological future forecast.

## Primary scientific red lines

GBIF dated presence ≠ occupancy/detection probability/abundance/colonization; other butterflies observed in a grid can be a useful proxy for effort but cannot automatically establish a target-species non-detection. Plant GBIF records ≠ the host is absent if not recorded. Plant cultivation history ≠ current WCVP introduction; LUH2 crop/grassland cells ≠ confirmation an accepted larval host is growing there. Invasions, land management, mowing, pesticides, butterflies' mobility and host growth also respond to climate and human activity. A historic climate-plus-host predictor comparison must not incorporate **post-hoc** future host data or fit and test in the same spatial background. Sensitivity of dates, dataset mix, species/host identification, human-introduced host quality, and genuine juvenile use need separate source gates. No future SSP result is justified until a historical host-vs-climate prediction is independently validated.
