## Main empirical climate result

Original source: 30,320 annotated high-confidence monarch larval-host observation photos in Freedman (2026), DOI `10.64898/2026.04.06.716508`; exactly 12,942 author-spatially/weekly-thinned observations and 8,256 accepted exact-source host/coordinate/rearing/annotation quality-screened rows joined in memory. Original 100-km locality sample 841 hexes, matching 836 10-minute WorldClim climate pixels. All nine climate products were evaluated on the **same 840 original source hexes** and 835 climate pixels after exclusion of one physically inconsistent future temperature pixel (details below). Larval record is a source-location anchor, NOT demonstrated winter larval development at each location. 100-km hexes can share one 10-minute climate pixel; source sites are not climate-independent biological replicates.

Historical baseline is **WorldClim v2.1 1970–2000**, not measured temperatures during 2010–2025 larval photos. Physiological thermal screen uses **11.5°C lower development threshold, 33°C cap, 323°C-days for an approximate egg-to-adult thermal input**, calculated from monthly midpoint of WorldClim tmin/tmax for Nov–Feb (120 days). This is NOT validated against daily/hourly fluctuating temperature, winter frost mortality, larval fitness or measured field cohort completion.

Under the stronger warming pathway, winter thermally qualifying localities more than double versus 1970–2000 in both GCMs, **if milkweed leaves are accessible throughout winter**. Under a specified **50% effective leaf thermal exposure** assumption, the fraction qualifying falls to 15.95–16.67%. With **no winter leaves**, conditional host-mediated development opportunity would be zero regardless of climate; this is a structural assumption, NOT observation of milkweed senescence. The host-season scenario cannot be given a field probability from the public photos. Among baseline thermally qualified hexes, required host-effective GDD fraction to reach 323 has median 0.504; under 2090 SSP5-8.5 that conditional median is 0.398–0.413. This median describes the thermally *qualifying subset* and is not an observed fraction of plants maintaining leaves.

The **summer** temperature flag rises even more, from 0.95% of source-hex sites in the baseline to **26.19–56.31% in the 2090 high-emissions scenario**, and the two GCMs disagree strongly. This is the proportion of locations whose summer **monthly average of daily Tmax** exceeds 38°C in at least one month. It is a coarse **heat-exposure warning**, not actual hours above 38°C, mortality, or net population outcome. Real herbivore damage and temperature stress depend on microclimate, food, thermal refugia, insects' life stages and activity.

For the **139 source hexes already containing November–February monarch larval photo records**, the historical 323 GDD climate screen was 56/139=40.29%; future high-emissions 2090 becomes 122/139=87.77% (MIROC6) and 124/139=89.21% (IPSL). This source subset was *selected on recorded winter larvae* and is therefore not a winter occurrence or range-expansion prediction; it is descriptive thermal exposure at selected recording locations only.

## Scientific inference / novelty boundaries

- The empirical result is that **real CMIP6 scenarios generate opposing seasonal thermal pressure gradients in geographically sampled monarch larval sites**: winter thermal opportunity grows while coarse summer heat screening expands. This is a **conditional physically grounded prediction**, NOT measured host-mediated ecological niche release, successful generation establishment or future population growth.
- A milkweed plant's **botanical origin is not an observed leaf persistence parameter**. The source annotations of cultivation and native/non-native plants do not measure an actual winter leaf calendar. Comparisons across those groups are spatially biased and cannot identify cultivation as a thermal rescue mechanism. Data on plant phenology, winter leaf biomass and eggs→adult completion are still the critical biological inputs.
- Climate predictions are limited to source observation geographies; neither host redistribution after 2026 nor new butterfly dispersal, migration, diapause, parasites, disease, precipitation and drought stress, or plant survival are modeled. **No future monarch population maps or probabilities, establishment events or conservation-benefit claim** are licensed.
- Two selected GCMs and two SSPs are a finite scenario comparison, not probabilistic climate uncertainty. 2041–2060 and 2081–2100 are 20-year climate averages, not single-year weather. Monthly averaged Tmax>38°C must not be equated to lethal temperature exposure.
- The physical-source quality audit discovered **one** December Tmin>Tmax sample under IPSL-CM6A-LR SSP5-8.5 2081–2100 among 836 source climate pixels. No temperature value was imputed or swapped. One pixel, affecting one author source hex, was **excluded from all nine model periods** before thermal scoring. Excluded fraction 0.12%; report this post-source QA deviation [explicitly](CMIP6_PHYSICAL_SOURCE_QC_ADDENDUM_20261028.md), rather than backdate the QA fix or interpret it as ecological selection.
- Freedman (2026) already reported winter non-native-host photo distributions, and biophysical monarch development thresholds are well known. The new component of this study is the *transparent combination of future climate forcing with independently varied host-resource persistence* at a fixed, previously observed host-labelled larval locality frame. **Without independent leaf persistence and larval development data it is a scenario analysis, not yet a validated new biological theory.**
