# chocho — retained positive evidence and one integrated ecological story
**Source audit date:** 2026-11-18 JST. **Status:** auditable, non-destructive evidence freeze on `main`; not a journal submission or merge of exploratory PRs.

## Decision in one paragraph
The core finding is **a disparity between redistribution of potential larval resources and biological realization**. Human-redistributed *already documented* butterfly larval-host plants expanded a fixed-HOSTS resource map by +54.9% across 239 source-selected butterflies; relative expansion was much larger on 126 island botanical regions (+118.9%) than 237 mainland regions (+40.9%), largely because island regions began with fewer native-host opportunities, **not because introductions filled a greater share of island gaps**. Current port counts correlate with entire introduced island floras, and sea geometry helps organize independent plant and indexed-butterfly compositions. Known-host geography improves an independent 155-butterfly future-record index prediction, and targeted source-verified direct caterpillar observations demonstrate at least one island endemic uses an introduced plant in its home archipelago. These are **complementary, differently sampled layers**, not an identified causal shipping→host→butterfly establishment chain. The strongest paper asks how human-assembled floras change **who could use resources where**, why islands show a disproportionate relative response, and how incomplete that opportunity-to-realization translation remains.

## Evidence tiers
- **A — main retained:** verified source-derived ecological contrasts or independent endpoint prediction, with the declared denominator and noncausal interpretation.
- **B — contextual positive:** real source observations/associations supporting an edge of the story, with limited selection, strata or predictive transfer.
- **C — source-limited or exploratory positive:** preserve original estimate and scope but do not use as a principal or causal claim.
- **D — important later null / falsification:** explicitly restrict earlier positives; do not silently erase the original data.
- **S — sample/identification limit:** source evidence about what population or biological endpoint is *not* measured.

## Three release-critical external validity and predictor audits

- **Species selection:** the 155-butterfly host-based GBIF forecast panel is **not a random or representative sample** of the 239-species potential resource panel; HOSTS catalogue breadth, geographical source support and mainland emphasis differ. Numeric forecast improvement must not be extrapolated to island endemics, in particular **0/18** listed living Macaronesian endemics appear in the latter.
- **Predictor controls:** the +4.12% model comparison is positive for known butterflies, but explicitly matched **region-identity baselines and fake-host (negative-control) maps** have not been traced to an executed frozen analysis in this audit. These are validation tasks, not reasons to delete the original score.
- **Marine model nesting:** R09's original **+5.082%** refers to an early geometry/control model. The later **candidate-host richness + pre-2011 plant-record effort** conditioned native AIS/wind comparison has a different model target/contrast (**+3.1576%**, [source](../../results/island51_pre2011_effort_control_20261107/README.md)); the separately reported **+0.71%** *uniform circuit* sensitivity still needs its exact fold table and model specification traced before it is presented as the definitive correction. Never replace one comparator with another without matching all predictors and folds.

## Logical connections — evidence vs interpretation

**Step 1 — geographic starting conditions (strong directly reconstructed contrast).**
Initial native-host-resource coverage is far lower on the *sampled botanical islands*. Because proportional increment is (new covered cells)/(native covered cells), similar fractions of remaining gaps filled generate very different relative effects. This *baseline-gap mechanism* is an **exact decomposition of the study's reconstructed opportunity metric**, not an experiment identifying natural ecological scarcity in all wild butterflies. The more interesting discovery is **asymmetric impact without greater island-specific introduction efficiency** (R02).

**Step 2 — the assembly of the plant supply pool (positive, observational).**
Whole introduced island floras associate with ports, and widespread documented plant hosts are concentrated contributors to the potential new resource map (R03/R05). The data are consistent with trade, horticulture, disturbed sites and human land use organizing where *plants* appear. They do **not** identify which plant was transported by which ship. The whole flora, not a demonstrably preferential transport of butterfly hosts, is the appropriate supply-stage target (R05/R06/R19/R20).

**Step 3 — fixed feeding repertoire converts a plant redistribution into *potential* butterfly food (strong design).**
By holding each butterfly's HOSTS-known larval plant identities fixed, the WCVP native-to-contemporary contrast isolates resource *geography* rather than claims of novel evolutionary host shifts (R01). It changes access to familiar plants. However existing HOSTS taxonomic selection excludes 18/18 independently enumerated sampled living island endemic butterflies (R15); an ecological extrapolation for island endemics needs a new source-defined estimand.

**Step 4 — actual interaction is a biological filter, not an automatic consequence (separate field observations).**
Independent endemic-caterpillar use of alien *Tropaeolum* by *P. cheiranthi* (R17) shows this possible mechanism is *real at least locally*. Contrasting native-host usage by *E. grancanariensis* and family-matched HOSTS literature controls (R14) show that exposure and documented use cannot be equated with *relative preference, host quality or demographic performance*. Across eight selected island endemics, botanical global expansion is heterogeneous and fails to equal new host presence in the original home archipelago (R16).

**Step 5 — sea geometry (not necessarily ocean currents) and observation further filter geography (complementary independent predictors).**
Uniform sea-circuit structure predicts assembled alien floras and later butterfly assemblages separately (R08/R09); the 155-butterfly temporal forecast gains from host information even after climate/land cover (R11). Yet additional marine×host interaction is not identified; global observed butterfly-assemblage beta has at most a tiny extra documented-host contribution after general plant/effort controls (R10/R13). These jointly argue **opportunity expansion is not automatically proportional realized community change**.

**Synthesis claim, safe for abstract:** Human-redistributed documented larval-host plants expand the **potential geography of resources** for sampled butterflies, disproportionately on islands with sparse native host coverage. Port-linked floristic assembly and sea geography are associated with how this potential is distributed; confirmed island-endemic alien host use supplies a field example, while actual butterfly assemblages and predictive comparisons reveal important independent biological, spatial and observational filters.

## What would be a *complete* causal story, but is NOT yet observed
1. Ship/commerce delivers source-identified *plant species* to a precisely identified island at a dated time.
2. Those introduced plants become locally established and sufficiently accessible to the butterfly's larva.
3. A butterfly reaches that exact site and a **wild** larva completes development on that plant, with a native-host comparator.
4. Independent surveys document new butterfly reproductive establishment after arrival while controlling pre-existing occupancy and observation effort.

There is **no single linked empirical data set that observes all four edges**. A full, defensible **observational biogeographic paper** is possible now; a causal colonization paper is not. Do not imply that R01, R05, R09 and R17 came from a single identified matched source population.

## One-paragraph draft of Discussion, ready to adapt
Plant introductions do not simply add the same ecological opportunity everywhere. When a butterfly's known larval hosts are re-mapped without changing its documented diet, the consequences depend on the native resource starting point: insular regions have relatively few baseline opportunities, so introductions yield much larger proportional gains despite not filling more of the available gaps. These opportunities are nonuniformly distributed among host plants and correspond to island port-associated floras and the geometry of marine access, but the empirical butterfly response is substantially filtered. Species-specific host geography improves subsequent recording predictions, whereas broad observed butterfly assemblage similarity shows only weak incremental host-specific signals after geography, background vegetation and indexing effort. Field-documented introduced-plant use by an island-endemic butterfly demonstrates a feasible interaction without establishing that the plant created a new island population. Human assembly of the flora can therefore **rewrite potential butterfly resource geography much more quickly than we can infer actual butterfly range assembly**, and islands magnify the former without guaranteeing the latter.
