## 1. Audit every independent established-alien butterfly × receiving-area record

The original 2024 Zenodo source records **18** exact original chocho-239 butterflies and **35 species × invaded-recipient POSITIVE** records across **11 source areas**. Geographic source matching is performed BEFORE classifying host-plant native/introduced status, and 35/35 original identities are conserved.

| Geographic source decision | Positive establishment records |
|---|---:|
| Exact original WCVP botanical Level3 code or source-safe country union with >=95% original mapping | **24** |

At prespecified 90% original country mapping coverage sensitivity, Chile (original area-atom guard coverage **94.966%**) passes, yielding **25** primary source-comparable, 3 Okinawa proxies and 7 continent-scale source-unscorable. No threshold has been changed to rescue a favorable ecological host outcome.

The original WCVP botanical code mapping is HAW Hawaii, OGA Ogasawara-shoto, GAL Galápagos, KOR Korea. Political Japan, New Zealand and South Africa require disjoint country×WGSRPD3 source area unions, not arbitrarily one WCVP botanical region. Okinawa is NOT identical to Nansei-shoto (NNS), even though the latter source range can help find ecological exceptions. Japan's original country-wide record may overlap Okinawa and Ogasawara source areas, so do not treat all 35 zone records as independent.

## 2. Local known-host redistribution matches alien butterfly occurrence but does not yet explain invasion success

Of the **24 original comparable positive species × recipient areas**, **all 24** have at least one original globally accepted butterfly larval HOST plant recorded by WCVP as **introduced and extant in the receiving region**. Specifically, **15** have introduced wild HOST plants only, and **9** have accepted host plants in both native and introduced WCVP range states. No strict source primary alien-positive case lacks an introduced-host wild source plant. Relaxing Chile 95% country geographic coverage to prespecified 90% adds *Pieris brassicae*, with **14 original accepted plant hosts in introduced source Chile country areas**, so that source-positive cohort becomes 25/25; it does not establish that those plants were consumed in Chile at the time of first arrival.

**Interpretation firewall:** a plant geographic occurrence AND a butterfly's accepted larval host reported SOMEWHERE does not imply in situ larval use, source founding date, successful individual reproduction or proven nursery/seed commodity movement. Original WCVP does not include all cultivated garden/urban/greenhouse host resources and is a contemporary snapshot, not introduction history.

## 4. One source-proxy exception is biologically important

In the THREE source-positive butterfly×Okinawa entries mapped only to the broader WCVP Nansei-shoto (NNS) botanical REGION, the source shows **two butterflies with introduced-plus-native potential hosts and one native-only**:

- ***Leptosia nina* / Okinawa, source first reported 1988**: 10 globally accepted larval HOST plant species in original chocho; **2 original known WCVP source NNS-native hosts**, **0 introduced wild**, and **8 globally documented hosts not recorded as WCVP extant wild in NNS**. This is a botanical PROXY and not included in the 24 source main records.
- Independent Japanese occurrence sources characterize *L. nina* as originally a wandering butterfly, reported present in Yaeyama since 1988, with later repeated local reproduction; Japan NIES [provisional alien Lepidoptera list](https://www.nies.go.jp/biodiversity/invasive/resources/listen_lepidoptera.html) includes *Leptosia nina niobe*, and [regional natural history source](https://papilio.my.coocan.jp/siro_shasin.html) describes 1988 Yonaguni and later local establishment.
- An independent [Okinawa larval-host natural-history photo archive](https://syokusou.choumusubi.com/07gyoboku.html) explicitly shows a *Leptosia nina* early-stage larva photographed **10 November 2003 in Iriomote** on ギョボク, with plant described as *Crateva religiosa* under a **Japanese local source name**. The [National Museum of Nature and Science local floristic record](https://www.kahaku.go.jp/research/activities/project/hotspot_japan/ryukyus/db/S1011.html) identifies locally native ギョボク as *Crateva adansonii* subsp. *formosensis*; modern [Kew POWO](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:1014207-1) accepts the name ***Crateva formosensis*** (native Nansei-shoto). **The photo's plant has not been independently vouchered/reidentified as that exact modern POWO accepted species**, so call it *Crateva* ギョボク native-host inference at coarse taxonomic resolution, not a definitive modern-species pair from the photo.
- The original chocho global HOSTS catalogue for *L. nina* includes several *Capparis*, *Cleome* and *Crateva* species, but the exact Okinawa modern *Crateva formosensis* host relationship is not resolved by that original accepted-pair snapshot. Do not create or merge a new HOSTS accepted species link from genus resemblance.
- This exception is consistent with an alien/expanding butterfly finding a **recipient-native larval resource**, independent of whether the original carrier was a living imported ornamental host. Natural long-distance dispersal from the south is a live alternative to human-mediated egg/larva movement, and no original carrier/first actual arrival is documented. No historical experiment confirms evolution of a new host-use ability.

This is a more scientifically distinctive *mechanistic boundary condition* than the global 24/24 intro-host coexistence: **presence of a nonnative consumer need not require its arrival commodity and the botanical host that supports local larvae to be the same introduction event**. It does not yet establish a quantitative frequency or host-mediated invasion effect.

## 5. What this result does NOT show

The 2024 registry contains original positive-only alien-established records; the remaining 221 original chocho source butterflies are not verified absent, untransported or failed species. Original time labels may be suspect (e.g. *Vanessa atalanta* Hawaii 1758), and a 2024 positive record can be historically eradicated (*Pieris brassicae* New Zealand). Source first report years do not establish when WCVP plants arrived; original 239 WCVP source range is a present-day geographic snapshot, not original planted-only urban inventories. Neither the 24 source coincidences nor the Okinawa native host use proves herbivorous insect establishment via a specific commodity.

The two-process model still requires independently **observed pre-establishment consumer propagule arrival** linked to verified exported host commodity/origin/destination/year and **subsequent wild juvenile establishment**. Existing original South African customs and published positive Zenodo sources did not produce a repeated original-239 butterfly founder-event panel; the only two exact intercepted original-239 butterflies had already been historically recorded as present in South Africa, so cannot provide founding vector timing.
