# Cultivated hosts outside naturalization maps: biological evidence gate v0.2

**Date:** 2026-10-08. **Branch:** `analysis/resource-homogenization-v01` only. **Status:** EXPLORATORY; NO INDEPENDENT DEMOGRAPHIC RESULT.

## What is now actually supported

1. **Uncaptured botanical occurrence pathway.** The fixed WCVP distribution sidecar contains no Florida distribution row for the native-Caribbean `Senna polyphylla` (reproduced against the pinned WCVP version; run 37716723493). An independently sampled iNaturalist Florida panel contains 59 photo-supported observations explicitly marked cultivated/captive in 46 approximate 5-km cells during 2010–2025. These are not plant censuses; the cultivated label is an original-observer categorization. It also contains 21 "uncaptive" photo records in 20 cells, which cannot by themselves prove naturalization. Within the grid, 41 cultivated-report cells did not overlap the 20 uncaptive-report cells; only five cells had both categories. The published 2024 South Florida rearing experiment demonstrates that larvae can emerge as adult Phoebis on cultivated `S. polyphylla`. This establishes that the WCVP geography is not an exhaustive inventory of cultivated host exposure; it is NOT proof that each of the 59 observations is used by larvae or that any butterfly population is rescued.

2. **Independent larval use is still insufficient.** Frozen GloBI pilot for 24 Fabaceae-using Pieridae recovered 58 dated, geographically placed putative larval feeding records. `Phoebis sennae` had 13 and `P. philea` had 11 records with several other Senna/Chamaecrista plants, but neither had a `S. polyphylla` link within that sampled panel. The specific 24-species feasibility gate of >=10 introduced-Fabaceae records failed (seven, across five butterflies/four regions; none in an independently verified introduced-only resource cell). No absence inference: field feeding is far less often photographed and explicitly annotated than plant presence.

3. **Cultivation and alien origin cannot be conflated.** Koptur et al. (2024), DOI 10.3390/insects15020123, sampled **both native and nonnative Senna hosts in cultivation**, not cultivated alien plants versus native wild vegetation. The source's Table 1 is internally inconsistent: 16 parasitoid emergences from native host site rows versus 17 in the printed grand-total column, with a second discrepant plant row. Stratified odds derived from site rows are descriptive, not a causal treatment estimate; species/site/time/repeated shrub sampling remain confounded. The paper documented larval development to adulthood on specific cultivated exotic hosts and parasitoids across them.

4. **Prior art rules out an inflated novelty claim.** Butterfly garden planting can attract eggs and support larval development (2015 monarch garden experimental comparison: PMID 26314013), and garden host composition affects immature survival/enemy risk (Majewska et al. 2018, DOI 10.1111/icad.12286). A 76-study meta-analysis already documented negative larval performance on many exotic hosts and ecological traps (Yoon & Read 2016, DOI 10.1007/s00442-016-3560-2). Therefore simply reporting "a cultivated alien host can support butterfly larvae" or "resource maps differ from fitness" is **not** itself new ecology.

## The ecological mechanism still untested

Two plant species present in the same urban landscape may generate different butterfly reproduction and enemy-mediated mortality, and a cultivated plant can exist outside the mapped naturalized flora. But **availability of host plants, observed oviposition, confirmed larval ingestion, survival to adults, and parasitoid attack are five different endpoints**. The next scientific experiment must compare these within independently sampled planting versus naturally established habitats, ideally across several butterfly species, repeated gardens and matched search effort.

**Stop rule:** If the original observational coverage does not provide replicated and source-audited larval ingestion for `S. polyphylla`, stop this observational branch as a mechanism paper. Keep the positive horticultural *exposure/inventory* discrepancy as a conservation-method sensitivity and return to independently measured larval survival and host quality data. Do not lower observation thresholds, infer causation from proximity, or alter the main GEB manuscript.
