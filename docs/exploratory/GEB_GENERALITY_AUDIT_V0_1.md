# GEB generality audit v0.1

Status: **exploratory post-hoc audit**. This does not alter the frozen manuscript evidence or convert exploratory results into prospective tests.

## Question

Is the main butterfly host-portfolio result broad enough for a Global Ecology and Biogeography framing, or is it being generated by one taxonomic family or one geographic subset?

The focal existing result is the host-portfolio architecture among 191 host-taxonomy-adequate butterflies that gained reconstructed opportunity from introduced host ranges:

- host-family breadth vs effective contributor number: Spearman rho = **+0.492**;
- host-family breadth vs maximum single-host share: rho = **-0.486**.

## Taxonomic breadth

The resource-eligible panel contains **239 species from six butterfly families**:

- Nymphalidae 104;
- Hesperiidae 51;
- Pieridae 43;
- Lycaenidae 26;
- Papilionidae 14;
- Riodinidae 1.

The conservative expanded subset still contains 191 species from all six families. The 82 expanded one-family specialists retain five families.

The independent occurrence/climate extension is the part that becomes visibly thinner: the frozen independent panel has 32 species across six families, while the climate-informative subset has 24 species across five families.

## Family replication of host-portfolio architecture

Among families with usable replication, the architecture has the same direction in every family.

| Butterfly family | n | host-family breadth -> effective contributors | host-family breadth -> max single-host share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Hesperiidae | 44 | +0.336 | -0.316 |
| Lycaenidae | 18 | +0.639 | -0.479 |
| Nymphalidae | 79 | +0.461 | -0.466 |
| Papilionidae | 12 | +0.783 | -0.717 |
| Pieridae | 37 | +0.642 | -0.653 |

Riodinidae has one species in this subset and is not separately interpretable.

This is not a one-family effect. Removing any entire family leaves the full-panel association in a narrow range:

- effective contributor number: rho = **+0.462 to +0.564**;
- maximum single-host share: rho = **-0.458 to -0.540**.

The within-one-family hierarchy also repeats in the major families: among butterflies using exactly one host family, greater resolved host-species richness is associated with more effective contributors and weaker dominance by the largest redistributed host in Hesperiidae, Nymphalidae, Papilionidae, Pieridae, and the small Lycaenidae subset.

## Native host-resource geography

The exact S1 native host-resource footprints were recovered from the pinned TTF artifact (run 36222306369, artifact 10898504566). The 239 butterflies collectively contain **26,530 species x WGSRPD3 native resource units**.

A typical butterfly's native host-resource footprint spans **four WGSRPD Level-1 continental units** (mean 4.51). Species with some native host-resource opportunity occur in:

- Asia-Temperate: 196 species;
- Northern America: 179;
- Asia-Tropical: 167;
- Africa: 161;
- Europe: 157;
- Southern America: 122;
- Australasia: 55;
- Pacific: 38;
- Antarctic/subantarctic WGSRPD units: 4.

These counts describe **larval host-resource geography**, not the realized occurrence range of the butterfly.

## Geographic replication of the architecture

For a conservative geography-stratified audit, each butterfly was assigned to the WGSRPD Level-1 continent containing the largest share of its native host-resource footprint. The host-portfolio result again has the same direction in every geographic group with meaningful replication:

| Dominant native resource continent | n | breadth -> effective contributors | breadth -> max single-host share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Northern America | 88 | +0.478 | -0.459 |
| Africa | 32 | +0.515 | -0.492 |
| Southern America | 25 | +0.217 | -0.256 |
| Europe | 24 | +0.730 | -0.687 |
| Asia-Temperate | 15 | +0.160 | -0.160 |
| Asia-Tropical | 6 | +0.319 | -0.319 |

The tropical-Asia cell is too small for strong subgroup inference, but its direction is consistent.

Removing any one dominant native-resource continent leaves the association at:

- effective contributors: rho = **+0.443 to +0.518**;
- maximum single-host share: rho = **-0.441 to -0.519**.

## What this changes for a GEB submission

The **main resource-side analysis is no longer the data-volume bottleneck**. Its strongest generality argument is now explicit:

> Similar aggregate anthropogenic resource gains are assembled through systematically different host portfolios, and this portfolio architecture repeats across the major butterfly families and across contrasting native host-resource geographies.

That is stronger than simply saying the study contains 239 species.

The remaining weak point is the occurrence/climate extension. A GEB expansion should prioritize:

1. **True butterfly biogeographic replication.** Use realized butterfly occurrence geography to assign or weight species across biogeographic realms. Do not relabel the WGSRPD host-resource audit as a butterfly-realm analysis.
2. **A larger independent occurrence panel.** The current 32-species panel is balanced by host-breadth stratum but is small relative to the 239-species resource analysis. A prospectively frozen extension toward roughly 60-100 species would improve geographic and taxonomic replication more than adding additional species to the already-large resource reconstruction.
3. **A larger climate-informative panel.** The current 24 species are adequate for the declared independent test but too thin to carry the manuscript's broadest generality claim alone.
4. **Formal heterogeneity analysis.** The same-direction family and geography results justify a model that estimates the overall portfolio effect while allowing family and geographic context to vary. This should be framed as an audit/sensitivity extension, not as a newly prospective hypothesis.

## Claim boundary

This audit establishes **taxonomic-family replication and native host-resource geographic replication**. It does not establish butterfly occurrence-realm replication, causal effects of host introduction, or prospective confirmation of the post-hoc portfolio mechanism.
