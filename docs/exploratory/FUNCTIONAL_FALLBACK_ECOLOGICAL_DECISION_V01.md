# Functional fallback is not mapped host redundancy — ecological mechanism decision

**Date:** 2026-10-08. **Status:** EVIDENCE-BACKED QUESTION / NO NEW CAUSAL ESTIMATE / EXPLORATORY ONLY. The GEB source analysis and PR #38 are not altered by this note.

## Distinct ecological quantities

The global chocho reconstruction measures **regional potential larval-resource supply**. For a focal butterfly and region, a mapped native or introduced plant can contribute to the resource opportunity set if the fixed HOSTS relation names that plant and WCVP maps it there. The number of plants in that set is *structural* (database-visible) redundancy.

The biologically consequential quantity after loss of a focal introduced host is **functional fallback**: the capacity of a local population to oviposit on a different locally usable host, complete development on it *at the relevant season and enemy exposure*, and sustain population replacement. Nominal native-host presence is insufficient if contemporary adult preference, larval development, phenology or management prevents use. Neither WCVP nor HOSTS measures those conditions. Do not invent a numeric scalar weighting these gates from a few unrelated rearing studies.

## Strongest independent within-species contrast

1. **Singer & Parmesan (2018), Nature 557:238–241, doi:10.1038/s41586-018-0074-6.** A Nevada *Euphydryas editha* population transitioned to complete dependence on introduced *Plantago lanceolata*. After cattle removal changed the associated grass and thermal microhabitat, it was locally lost although ancestral *Collinsia parviflora* was present. This is a published local eco-evolutionary trap, **not** an effect discovered in chocho.
2. **Haan, Bowers & Bakker (2021), Scientific Reports 11:992, doi:10.1038/s41598-020-80413-y.** A different *E. editha* population that had incorporated *Plantago* retained willingness to oviposit and larval performance on ancestral *Castilleja* hosts. This is population-level host-use variation, **not** a controlled comparative estimate of survival under Plantago removal.

The cases differ in subspecies, plant community, history, population and assay design. Their contrast demonstrates plausible heterogeneity in functional fallback, but it cannot estimate a global causal effect of host introduction or structural redundancy. *Collinsia* in the first population is not an ancestral host measured in the second; do not conflate them.

## Testable causal question rather than a tautological removal simulation

> **Conditional on the same mapped set of alternative host plants, does realized population resistance to losing a currently used introduced host depend on whether oviposition preference and larval performance on ancestral hosts have been retained?**

**Unit:** replicated butterfly population × host-removal or naturally occurring host-loss episode, tracked through matched pre/post seasons with a comparison population and measured plant access.

**Minimum observed variables:** local plant biomass/phenology and vegetation microclimate; female oviposition choice among plants at the same location; cohort survival through adult eclosion on alternatives with predators/parasitoids measured; host switching after focal-host decline; adult recruitment or population growth across at least two generations.

**Discriminating predictions:** where native hosts remain mapped but fallback behavior and performance have been lost, population growth after host loss declines more than in otherwise similar populations that retain both. Where fallback remains intact, realized dependence can be lower than mapped introduced-host reliance suggests. Time-varying host conditions can reverse either sign.

**Main competing mechanisms:** direct host physiology, altered microclimate following management, time-limited seasonal host access, reduced dispersal, genetic host-race differences, sampling and population-history effects. The Singer & Parmesan event involves grazing and thermal-microhabitat change, so cannot be rebranded as simple botanical removal.

## Exact intersection with the global butterfly panel and long-term prior art

The *E. editha* case is not merely a generic example. The frozen chocho `data/frozen/figure_sources/anthropogenic_species_metrics.csv` includes this exact butterfly among 239 focal species, with **30 resolved HOSTS species**, **two host-plant families**, **131 native resource WGSRPD3 regions**, **245 contemporary regions**, and **114 added units** (+87.0%). Its added-unit rank is **29/239**, and **6/59** within the two-host-family butterfly class. These are **global species-wide database properties**, not local plant availability in Nevada or Washington, and they do not imply that any of the 30 hosts is present at the particular trap site.

**A direct scale-mismatch anchor from the original 1980s field census:** Singer & Parmesan (2021) describe egg-placement observations at **57 *E. editha* sites**, where **43 local populations were monophagous** and the rest used **two to four host genera** (referencing Singer & Wee 2005). This is a published estimate of *locally observed host-genus use*, not a claim of 43 genetic specialists or proof of unavailable alternative plants. By contrast, chocho aggregates **30 recorded host species over the entire butterfly species' range**. Their numerators, spatial grains and taxonomic denominators differ. This is an independently documented *scale mismatch*, not a newly detected reduction in host breadth and not a valid 43/57 correction factor for the global 30. Original article: [doi:10.1111/gcb.15656](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.15656), Methods 2.1.

**Previously established local dynamics must not be sold as new:** Singer & Parmesan (2021), *Global Change Biology* 27:3505–3518, doi:10.1111/gcb.15656, studied 15 *E. editha* populations over decades. Among the 15, **eight had fewer locally used host genera at the last census, seven were unchanged and none increased**; the study also linked colonization/recolonization to transient expansion of host preferences followed by later specialization. The original 2021 paper already established this population-level temporal mechanism; it is **not a chocho discovery**. Table 1 provides the observed longitudinal pair of host-*genus* counts for each population. Species-level host-*species* counts (30) and local host-*genus* counts (1–4) have incompatible denominators and should **never be divided to estimate a percentage of unused hosts**. Likewise a local population's within-site host change cannot be interpreted as a regional change in available botanical ranges.

**Newest global comparator:** Gross, Kawahara & Daru (2026), *Nature Communications* 17:7127, doi:10.1038/s41467-026-73236-4, studied climatic and plant-richness correlates of butterfly diet specialization. In contrast, chocho **holds the observed consumer–host inventory fixed** while altering the mapped plant geography, making consumer resource-opportunity reorganization—not the drivers of evolving diet breadth—its distinct estimand. The two research programs are complementary, but this distinction is substantive only if the manuscript avoids claiming butterfly occupancy or evolutionary shifts from a static opportunity model.

**New question with a testable time dimension, not supported yet by these sources:** When globally introduced host supply grows, does population-level host-use breadth expand during colonization and subsequently narrow with residence time **even while mapped resource opportunity remains high**? A publishable multi-species test would need independent dated local host-use histories and verified host-establishment histories, preserving botanical opportunity within matched spatial/effort strata; the original 15 populations alone are a one-species prior-art benchmark rather than replication across butterflies. Do not use the historical GCB data to resurrect the invalid capped-GBIF colonization hindcast or to label first records as establishment.

The original-data source gate for Haan et al. was frozen in `EUPHYDRYAS_LOCAL_HOST_FUNCTION_SOURCE_GATE_V01.json` and executed by `scripts/audit_euphydryas_local_host_function_source.py`. The [successful GitHub source audit (run 37766314573)](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/37766314573) verified exact MD5s and source schemas for **642 larval-mass rows**, **145 oviposition rows**, **22 neonate-choice rows** and **112 chemical-uptake rows**. The chemical table contains two empty-name trailing columns and requires column-position-aware treatment; no host-response coefficients or adult survival were fitted. **A source audit is not new evidence for a host-fallback effect.**

## Stage-specific resource interruption as a third independent mechanism

A third primary published *E. editha taylori* example isolates an ecological dimension that neither whole-region botanical presence nor the number of hosts represents: **larval stage × season × host condition**. Severns & Guzman-Martinez (2021), *Insects* 12:246, [doi:10.3390/insects12030246](https://doi.org/10.3390/insects12030246), described spread of the non-native plant pathogen *Pyrenopeziza plantaginis*, which causes winter foliar necrosis of the introduced larval host *Plantago lanceolata*. Their phenological reconstruction found an approximately six-week period of reduced appropriate food for post-diapause larvae in plantain-dependent populations. They argued that seasonally usable *Collinsia parviflora*, rather than the mere presence of perennial *Castilleja*, could provide functional dietary continuity; even Collinsia availability depends on local germination timing. This is an **existing, source-reported mechanism**, not an effect measured or established by chocho.

**Separate the three geographical/temporal examples:**
- Singer & Parmesan 2018: Nevada, evolved oviposition dependence + abrupt grazing/microclimate change + documented local extinction.
- Haan et al. 2021: Pacific Northwest Taylor's checkerspot, retention of ancestral-host oviposition and larval performance; plant tissue (Castilleja bract vs leaf) modifies larval growth.
- Severns & Guzman-Martinez 2021: Pacific Northwest Taylor's checkerspot, pathogen-driven winter host quality/timing interruption; risk assessment, *not proof that the pathogen caused population extinction*.

Together these demonstrate why **nominal functional redundancy is stage-conditional**. An ancestral host may be acceptable during oviposition but fail to provision post-diapause larvae during an infected six-week window. Conversely, an introduced host can support a complete diet in pathogen-free periods. The same mapped host count can therefore correspond to qualitatively different temporal continuity of resources without any change in recorded species identities.

**Distinct falsifiable prediction requiring independent data:** After controlling actual host biomass and female oviposition, plantain-dependent populations with greater pathogen exposure during their post-diapause window will show lower larval survival/recruitment *only when phenologically aligned alternate hosts are unavailable*. The interaction should attenuate where functional, stage-matched native hosts are available. Sampling only annual plant presence or only eggs would fail to distinguish it. Required measurements: pathogen intensity, plant tissue biomass by season, larval feeding stage, individual fate through adult eclosion, predators, management, and matched locality histories. No such cross-population test was run in chocho.

## Concrete same-species measurement sensitivity: four original local hosts

A new source-frozen [exact HOSTS/WCVP crosswalk run 37767325222](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/37767325222) reproduced the *E. editha* species-wide **30 accepted host species**, **131 native**, **245 contemporary** and **114 introduced-added** WGSRPD3 resource regions. Of four *preselected*, originally published local hosts, *Plantago lanceolata* and *Collinsia parviflora* were exact accepted species links in the original frozen HOSTS sidecar, but *Castilleja hispida* and *C. levisecta* were absent as **E. editha species-pair links**. This shows concrete source incompleteness **within one selectively chosen example**, NOT a 50% global missing-link rate. *Plantago* uniquely supports 52/114 = 45.6% of this butterfly's *reconstructed introduced-added region units* under a static remove-only-its-introduced-botanical-range perturbation. This cannot estimate local physiological dependence, plant-removal response or extinction.

Full reproducible decision and constraints: `EUPHYDRYAS_FOUR_HOST_CROSSWALK_DECISION_V01.md`. The augmented two-Castilleja sensitivity was prespecified **after** the above crosswalk and is a one-species robustness check. It must not be interpreted as an independent test of a novel local eco-evolutionary trap mechanism.

## Completed missing-host perturbation: geographic robustness is not functional robustness

The [completed WCVP-pinned two-Castilleja audit (run 37770418250)](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/37770418250) independently matched *Castilleja hispida* (accepted ID 2705047, native/contemporary 6/6 botanical regions) and *C. levisecta* (accepted ID 2705113, 3/3). Adding these two documented local hosts expanded the recorded E. editha list **30 → 32 plant species**, but **did not change** its 131 native regions, 245 contemporary regions, 114 added resource regions, or 52 regions uniquely dependent on introduced *Plantago* under the frozen database. Both missing plant species' botanical regions fell within the already-covered global union. The preselected geographic-completeness sensitivity therefore had a **zero** difference.

This supports only **area-level redundancy of documented plant ranges**, not resource substitutability within a host-rich cell. In particular, the original experiment found strong within-*Castilleja* bract/leaf performance differences, and direct field phenology/microclimate can remove functional availability despite unchanged botanical presence (Haan et al. 2021). The new result is a source-verified null sensitivity for **one handpicked butterfly**, not a demonstration of globally complete HOSTS inventories. Exact receipt: `EUPHYDRYAS_MISSING_CASTILLEJA_AUGMENTATION_DECISION_V01.md`.

**Consequence for novelty:** This particular one-species repair does **not** yield a new spatial finding, nor does it establish a biological mechanism. It closes an inference loophole: apparent robustness of aggregate resource-envelopes to two missing host links can coexist with severe uncertainty in stage-specific local host quality. Further static host augmentations should not be used to chase a nonzero area result.

## Current evidentiary decision

The chocho global indicators (239 butterflies, 14,553 added butterfly × region cells, 58.9% of added cells supported by one introduced plant in the database) are valid **supply-network descriptors**. The spatial stress test does not identify evolved fallback capacity or actual demographic dependence. This independent mechanism is not estimable from presently frozen HOSTS/WCVP/GBIF occurrences and published source audits.

**Keep the primary GEB article focused on potential resource expansion, regional homogenization and potential co-use.** In its conservation interpretation, distinguish mapped alternatives from usable alternatives and cite the two contrasting *E. editha* precedents, but claim no new local population-fate inference. Stop further post-hoc static network rerankings unless independent local biological outcomes become available.
