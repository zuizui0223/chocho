# Ecological results gate: how plant globalization climatically sorts butterfly larval resources
**2026-10-19 | Global 239-butterfly HOSTS/WCVP/CHELSA macroecology | Exploratory, not butterfly demography.**

## Current supported macro result — a gradient of structural explanations
A 239-species × 365 WGSRPD3 source-frozen analysis identifies **nonrandom climate sorting of potential larval-host resource geography**, with the biological and structural mechanism progressively resolved by stricter nulls. All climate similarity concerns the original geography of recorded **native HOST PLANTS**, not a measured butterfly climatic niche, oviposition or fitness. The baseline climate thresholds were 5–95% and 10–90% for CHELSA BIO1 and BIO12. Five butterfly species did not have ten climate-supported native host regions; 234 species (14,481 of all 14,553 added butterfly×region cells) were evaluable. There is no new literature-level insect experiment in this work.

### Qualification of MCMC structural nulls
- [Source-verified butterfly×region null](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/39211273830): two independent chains, 199 draws each, both q=0.05 and q=0.10 ESS≥30, agreement/movement/spacing gates passed; original 2×99 chains did not pass ESS and were not used.
- [Source-verified two-mode HOST PLANT network and plant-climate MCMC](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/39212635930): two independent seeds and 199 draws per chain for **BOTH** Family×Level1 and Family×Level1×BIO1/BIO12 2×2 nulls; all modes and both analogue thresholds PASS numeric source/margin/movement/ESS gates. The strictly climate-matched mode is separately reproduced by [run 39212611953](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/39212611953). Source total 30,373 introduced host-plant×WGSRPD3 edges fixed, viable swaps 28,670 in Family×Level1 and 27,886 in Family×Level1×climate. The null median evaluable added butterfly×region counts rise to ~15,315–15,317 in continent-only and ~14,905–14,907 in climate-conditioned samples, versus observed 14,481; comparisons therefore MUST use `aligned/newly added` within each draw, NOT raw aligned counts. For both modes and q thresholds, ESS≥30. Nominal serial add-one tails are 0.005 per chain, **not** independent permutation p values, ecological p values or causal treatment inference.

### Nontrivial ecological interpretation
1. **Potential larval-resource climate sorting is not solely a union-set tautology**: repeated climate-analog alignment exceeds geographic fixed margins; the direction remains positive using two predeclared climate-analogue widths.
2. **Most of the initial apparent ecological signal is plant-side**: family×continent source-range constraints reduce the wide-analogue +8.28 pp headline to **+3.57–3.58 pp**; additionally holding plant climatic occupancy in coarse within-continent 2×2 BIO1/BIO12 classes reduces it to **+0.86–0.90 pp** (about +1.39–1.42 under the narrower analogue). This comparison isolates the important ROLE of plant distribution and climate strata under specified null algorithms, but the nulls differ in their butterfly-added opportunity denominators and cannot be treated as an additive causal variance partition.
3. **A small residual remains after retaining the fixed butterfly–host network**: this is evidence compatible with finer-scale spatial organization of recorded resource opportunities within coarse climate bins; it does *not* isolate evolutionary host-choice selection from residual plant habitat geography. It may disappear under finer matching, better host-catalogue completeness or locality/area bias corrections. The plant-family-climate matched null and butterfly×region fixed-margin null differ in what they preserve; their residuals are not directly additive causal components.
4. Regional asymmetry matters: simple Level1 expectation excess in Africa ~+12.16 pp, Northern America +10.13, Southern America +9.93, Europe +7.52, Tropical Asia +0.88. This concerns introduced-host WGSRPD3 resource cells, NOT observed butterfly populations.
5. Disturbance-affinity filtering concerns **how many previously unsupported climate-analogue resource regions are filled**, not stronger climate targeting per introduced added opportunity. With native-resource continent and climate controlled, strong disturbance-affiliated vs strongly avoidant species (n=179) show corrected fill-logit coefficient **+1.379**, genus-cluster ~95% [+0.630,+2.129]; adequate-HOSTS subset n=160: +1.080 [+.423,+1.738]. Disturbance-affinity effect on climate enrichment **over the geographic expectation** is **−0.0168**, genus interval [−0.0914,+0.0578] (n=157), so **no positive differential climate targeting is established**. Native resource regions and taxonomic groups are nonrepresentative: avoidant taxa are strongly underincluded (33/68 trait candidates vs 150/193 disturbance-affiliated) and absent from the strongly-avoidant comparator in several bioregions.

## Catalogue-source sensitivity: independent computational check in progress
The previously archived **BCE/Clarke Europe-biased partial host list** adds 1,027 accepted-host pairs across 81 butterflies and changes the newly supported region IDs of that subset substantially. A full 239-species reconstruction with the SAME original climate input and Level1-uniform expectation was recomputed locally: original 5–95% excess **+8.280 pp** vs augmented **+8.240 pp** (9,904 / 14,901 evaluated added cells; E ≈8,676.19); original 10–90% **+7.688 pp** vs augmented **+8.329 pp** (7,337/14,901; E ≈6,095.91). These are catalogue comparisons, **not** independent globally representative source replication. **Do not promote these newly computed aggregate numbers to independently CI-verified status until [source-verified new workflow](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/workflows/host-catalogue-macroclimate-sensitivity.yml) has run successfully.** The already-qualified HOSTS-only plant-network MCMC residual has **not** been rerun on BCE-augmented host edges.

## Publication decision
This body of evidence offers an ecology-focused macroecological finding **at the layer of potential consumer resources**: global plant redistribution follows broad climatic geography that carries many existing butterfly–host links into analogous environments; additional alignment beyond host-network and plant-family coarse-climatic structure is detectable but quantitatively modest. For a stronger paper, characterize this *dominance of plant-side geography* rather than claiming that globalization selectively increases butterfly fitness, that host specialists benefit more or less, or that invasive plants should be retained.

The original frozen [GEB PR #38](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/pull/38) is unchanged. These new original macro analyses remain in exploratory draft [PR #46](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/pull/46); author review is needed before choosing manuscript integration or an independent follow-up. Input licences and double-anonymous package privacy are not approved for release.

**Explicitly untested:** insect geographic colonisation, oviposition choice, adult recruitment, butterfly range expansion through time, evolutionary climate adaptation, equal WGSRPD3 physical area, finer geographic plant niche conservation, a fully independent globally representative host inventory, and community-level species co-occurrence changes. No novel management intervention is derived.
