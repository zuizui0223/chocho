# Invasive butterflies: verified local larval host evidence is not the same as host range opportunity

**27 October 2026 — source-reviewed exploratory case-study audit.** [Source-gated reproducible CI #40463837546](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/40463837546) passed **7 synthetic tests** across the exact original 13 butterfly×invasion-area events (eight butterflies: five original-239, three independently curated off-panel candidates). All 15 candidate study×host entries have URL, highest identifiable plant taxonomic rank, observation region, year where supported, host origin source, and source evidence stage. Field versus lab controls and original event identities are exact, not cherry-picked after stage scoring.

## 1. Full 13-event cohort and direct field source evidence

**Three** of the original **13** species×recipient invasion events have a curated source describing local early stages on a host; only **one** original-239 butterfly species (*Thymelicus lineola*) has such direct source evidence in this small fixed invasion panel. The other ten are **NOT biological host-use absences**. No inference of global frequency or establishment is permissible.

## 2. Important companion outcome outside the original 13 events

**Direct local development on cultivated/ornamental hosts: *Cacyreus marshalli*, Greece**. In Kifissia, Greece, two Pelargonium spp. plants containing larvae were collected in July/September **2010**, with butterflies reared to adult emergence in the laboratory (Martinou et al. 2011, [original Hellenic Plant Protection Journal](https://www.bpi.gr/files/journal/2011/july/Hellenic%20Plant%20Protection%20Journal%20-%20Martinou%20%28July%202011%29.indd.pdf)). This confirms a **field-origin host observation in Greece**, not the original Mallorca, Spain invasion event. The plant is documented to **genus only** and is horticultural, so the exact plant species WCVP naturalization label is not established from this record.

**Laboratory host capacity without wild use**: Quacchia et al. (2008, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10531-008-9350-3)) found controlled development through adult emergence on native European *Geranium pratense* and *G. sylvaticum*. The same paper reported no oviposition on *Geranium* under mixed-choice conditions when *Pelargonium* was present. Kew confirms [*G. pratense* is native in Italy](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:322493-2) and [*G. sylvaticum* is native in Italy](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/322492-2). **This is a useful empirical contrast between attainable laboratory host breadth and field-realized host choices**, NOT proof that a new European wild *Geranium* host has been adopted.

Europe-wide evidence (Martelli et al. 2022, [Biological Invasions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10530-022-02895-3)) describes ornamental *Pelargonium zonale* among *C. marshalli* larval hosts, and [Kew records *P. zonale* native to South Africa and introduced in Spain](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:377229-1). However the review is **not** a separately documented larva-host observation on Mallorca during first establishment.

For other fixed candidate events, New Zealand *Pieris rapae* and *Danaus plexippus*, Chile *Pieris brassicae*, and Caribbean *Papilio demoleus*, the located sources often offer legitimate regional pest/life-cycle information or adult records but insufficiently localized juvenile×identified-host field data to satisfy this strict gate. For example first Dominican *P. demoleus* specimens were caught in an area where citrus was planted; this alone does not establish a larval feeding interaction there. [Guerrero et al. 2004](https://bvearmb.do/handle/123456789/2202).

## 3. Historical date conflicts preserved

- *Papilio demoleus*, Dominican Republic: peer-reviewed specimens [Guerrero et al. 2004](https://bvearmb.do/handle/123456789/2202) document collection on **29 March 2004**; an [EPPO distribution page](https://gd.eppo.int/taxon/PAPIDE/distribution/DO) retrospectively lists first observation in **March 2001**. The true first year remains **unresolved**, not silently overwritten by either source; 2004 remains independently dated specimen documentation.
- *Pieris rapae*, New Zealand: the [NZ butterfly organization](https://www.nzbutterflies.org.nz/species-info/cabbage-white/) describes a first record in Napier in **1929**; historical [Te Ara](https://teara.govt.nz/en/1966/butterfly-white) places establishment/arrival in **1930**. First encounter and sustained establishment may differ; treat 1929–1930 as distinct, unresolved reporting fields.

## 4. Scientific inference

The two mechanistic possibilities **are genuinely documented in case studies**: interaction with a new island-native host (*Chilades pandava* on *Cycas micronesica*) versus use of geographic redistribution of an alien grass (*Thymelicus lineola* on introduced timothy) and ornamental hosts (field *Cacyreus marshalli* in Greek gardens). But **these sources do NOT quantify how frequently either route occurs**, and independently found research may also underpin citations later aggregated into HOSTS; the original HOSTS CSV lacks per-record study-citation IDs, so statistical source independence is not identified.

A strengthened conceptual distinction emerges:

1. **Potential known-host resource geography:** original GEB 54.9% reconstructed increase, known butterfly–plant accepted edges fixed.
2. **Reported use at receiving localities:** 179 WCVP-introduced-location HOSTS interaction triples over 239 butterflies, source-native documentation largely missing.
3. **Field-verified host realization:** this selected 13-event study can strictly verify three original recipient events, only one in the original-239 cohort; other events are **unknown**, not zero.
4. **Controlled host capability vs realized field interaction:** European *Geranium* development in controlled assays vs Europe-wide ornamental *Pelargonium* host reports is a warning against interpreting physiological feasibility as actual wild host adoption.
5. **Butterfly native climate vs invaded climate:** not assessed by this stage panel; no thermal niche or competitive-displacement effect inferred.

All outputs are limited to original-event summaries and short source citations. Full curated study/citation ledger remains in the branch without complete copyrighted paper text. This exploratory work should NOT be added to the frozen GEB manuscript.
