# Cover letter — Global Ecology and Biogeography

**Working draft v0.2**

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Anthropogenic host redistribution expands butterfly resource geography beyond matched-host expectations,”** for publication in *Global Ecology and Biogeography*.

Global change redistributes interacting species as well as climate, yet consumer biogeography is often interpreted against resource breadth or native resource distributions as if the geography of those resources were fixed. We ask how human redistribution of larval host plants changes butterfly resource opportunity, whether the resulting geography aligns with contemporary butterfly occurrences, and how much of that expanded opportunity remains filtered by climate-associated constraints.

Across 239 butterflies, introduced host distributions expanded reconstructed larval-resource opportunity for 206 species and increased aggregate species × WGSRPD3 coverage from 26,530 to 41,083 units (+54.9%). The key advance is not simply this large descriptive increase. In a conservative 215-species subset, we randomized host identity while preserving each butterfly's exact resolved host-species count and exact plant-family composition. Actual host portfolios produced 13,529 introduced-added units compared with a matched-null median of 8,046; mean and median proportional expansion likewise exceeded null expectations (all p = 0.0005). Yet the specialist–generalist gradient itself was ordinary under the same null: host-family breadth versus proportional expansion was rho = 0.015 against a null median of 0.004 (p = 0.7865). Thus the non-random signal lies in which plant species butterflies use, not in broad family-level generalism.

We independently evaluated whether the added resource geography is relevant to contemporary butterfly distributions. In a separately assembled 32-species occurrence panel, introduced host ranges recovered 66 of 115 butterfly species × region observations lying outside native host-resource envelopes. This recovery exceeded both a global envelope-size null and a stricter null preserving the regional allocation of added host geography; under the latter, expected recovery had a median of 49 units (95% interval 43–56; p = 5 × 10^-6). We interpret this as occurrence-side validation of contemporary resource opportunity, not as proof of local larval use or causal range expansion.

Finally, an independently specified 24-species climate analysis showed that host-resource opportunity remains only partly realized. Climate-associated filtering persisted after within-region and geographic-distance matching, whereas the pre-specified prediction that broad host-family diets weaken filtering was not supported (partial rho = -0.166, one-sided p = 0.2237). We report the negative test with its limited precision rather than retuning the hypothesis.

We believe this manuscript fits *Global Ecology and Biogeography* because it identifies a broad-scale consequence of anthropogenic redistribution that is not recoverable from consumer range size or taxonomic diet breadth alone. Human movement of resource species changes the geographic opportunity landscape itself. The matched-host and occurrence-overlap nulls show that the result is not merely arithmetic expansion of larger portfolios or larger maps, while the climate analysis demonstrates a second boundary between potential resource geography and realized consumer geography.

The manuscript also reports an important robustness correction. An initially striking specialist–generalist contrast in host-contribution concentration largely disappears after exact host-species richness is held constant, so we explicitly demote that pattern from mechanism to structural diagnostic.

The analyses use public trait, interaction, plant-distribution, occurrence and climate data. Analysis code, fixed source identities, robustness analyses and result records are versioned for reproducibility; archival public metadata and the anonymized reviewer-access package will be finalized for submission.

This manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors will approve the submitted version and agree to be accountable for the work.
