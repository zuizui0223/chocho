## One-sentence manuscript claim

**Potential butterfly larval-host resource opportunities expanded disproportionately on insular botanical regions, while modern port counts were associated with broad introduced plant assembly rather than an additional host-specific excess.**

This is **not** proof that a plant arrived by ship, that a butterfly colonized a new island, or that sewage or agricultural nitrogen made a host plant reproduce.

## Submission boundary

GEB's global +54.9% resource opportunity reconstruction and the fixed 239-butterfly host network are shared inputs. Their reuse requires explicit overlap disclosure; the global +54.9% must not be advertised as two separate novel findings. JBI-oriented manuscript currently derives its unique narrative from island–mainland heterogeneity and port-linked whole-flora versus host-taxon comparison.

**Author/editor requirements before submission:** references checked against original sources, visual figures rendered and audited, complete methods on data licensing and taxonomy, source-data availability checked, coauthor approval, and a publication-overlap decision. The manuscript is an **initial full draft**, not a declaration of journal submission readiness.
