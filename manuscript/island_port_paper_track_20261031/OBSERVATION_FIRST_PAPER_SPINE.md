# Island–port butterfly paper | Observation-first spine

2026-10-31. **Draft concept, not a submitted or accepted manuscript.** Prioritize the *observed island-level host/butterfly association*, not an unobserved ship or colonization mechanism. The original independent GEB v0.2 manuscript is unchanged.

## One question

**Do similarities in the introduced larval-host plants indexed near island ports align with butterfly assemblage turnover, beyond baseline geographic and historical community structure?**

A stronger, unmeasured specificity question determines whether this becomes a standalone *Journal of Biogeography* Research Article:

**Is the concordance specific to known butterfly hosts, rather than being the same island-wide pattern visible in all recorded plants?**

## Working title

*Port-indexed larval-host assemblages covary with butterfly composition across islands*

Here “port-indexed” identifies the plant **data collection geography**, not proven port transport or a port-specific effect.

## Narrative order and currently defensible findings

1. **Geography and sampling (Fig. 1).** Complete single-landmass port plant sources cover 103 islands; historical butterfly recordings and alien-candidate plants retain **51 islands**, not a random global island sample. Candidate pool 225 butterflies; 2011–2022 newly indexed 116 butterfly species and 430 butterfly×island units. All **1,275 island pairs reuse those 51 islands**.
2. **The observed coupled turnover (Fig. 2).** Introduced candidate hosts and newly indexed butterfly assemblages have Jaccard-rank **ρ=+0.300**, vs **+0.418** with historically recorded butterfly assemblages. Distance + prior butterfly composition adjusted rank association **+0.215**.
3. **Structure controls (Fig. 3).** Positive association is not restricted to jointly zero Jaccard pairs: host-positive pairs (n=711) **ρ=+0.285**; both-positive pairs (n=394) **ρ=+0.326**. Four global-distance strata retain positive rho **0.183–0.298**. Island-node row/column residual permutations, including a 2,000km geographic complete-link restriction, give **exploratory two-sided tail fraction 0.0282**, against an already-positive geographic null average +0.157. This is **not a pre-registered calibrated population p-value**.
4. **Are these larval hosts rather than just regional flora? (Fig. 4, NOT YET MEASURED)** Reconstruct same-port same-year lists for general plants / non-host species and effort, then compare M0 (geography + prior butterflies + all-plant composition + effort) to M1 (+ compatible introduced host composition). Species identities and host network held fixed; evaluate island-level dependence and holdout geography. No claim of host-specificity until this comparison exists.
5. **Historical/port mechanism checks (Fig. 5, bounds).** Current plant atlas spans 2001–2024, overlapping and postdating the new-butterfly record years. In a small historical 17-island sample, first-record host overlap loses its excess when conditioning on plant geography. Existing matched historical port vs nonport botanical comparisons (PR #116) do not establish port-specific introduced-host enrichment. Geographic coincidence is not proof of transport, butterfly breeding, or inter-island stepping stones.

## Connection to completed GEB results, without double-reporting

- **GEB:** 239 butterflies, introduced known-host range information adds **+54.9%** potential butterfly×region resource opportunities (206/239 species). This is an opportunity geography result, **not realized butterflies**.
- **Independent forecast panel:** in **155 species** and **208,482** time-out species×grid evaluations, adding host geography improves prediction MSE **4.12%** (spatial block **3.00%**). This is butterfly *indexing probability*, not proof of colonization or port mediation.
- **Island association:** a separate ecological scale where host-index and butterfly-composition patterns covary. Do not infer that island/port movements realized any fixed fraction of +54.9%, or that the island correlation explains the forecast improvement.
- If one integrated paper uses the +54.9% as a Results centerpiece, do not publish the same result as a separate new GEB contribution. If the GEB-first paper is submitted, the island paper must contain genuinely new evidence (general-plant matched specificity and/or independent port/nonport material), cite and disclose the prior paper.

## Claim ladder and stop condition

**Supported now:** port-indexed introduced-host candidate similarity covaries with both old and newly indexed butterfly composition among **selected 51 islands**. The direction survives several post-hoc descriptive restrictions and exploratory node-label controls.

**Not supported/identified now:** host-specific interaction rather than general plant geography, port-only flora enrichment, actual larval feeding or local reproduction, true butterfly arrival, ship introduction, island relay or causal port effect.

**Next sole target:** a *same-site source-matched all-plant vs documented larval-host contrast* that asks whether the association contains information **specific to butterfly resources**, rather than generic turnover. Match plant observation effort and island/region membership before looking at the response. Preserve all 51 island eligibility identities and publish attrition without selecting favorable islands.

**Decision:** If the host-specific increment cannot survive geographical prevalence and botanical-effort controls, retain the positive observational correlation as a limited descriptive result, not a standalone host-mediated colonization claim. Standalone JBI submission stays on hold until independent ecological specificity is demonstrated.
