# Candidate manuscript insert — resource gain and known-host support architecture
**Exploratory, source-locked addendum v0.1, 24 October 2026.** This is an optional discussion/Supplementary Results element for a future revision of the *single* butterfly resource-geography GEB paper. The frozen v0.2 paper and PR #38 are **not modified**.

## Candidate Results text (English; insert only after review of hypotheses and figure placement)

Beyond the aggregate expansion of potential larval-resource geography, newly accessible regions varied in the number of documented introduced hosts supporting each butterfly. Among the 206 butterflies with additional resource regions, 8,574 of 14,553 added butterfly × region combinations (58.9%) were supported by exactly one recorded introduced host. Nevertheless, species with greater proportional resource gain tended to have a **lower** single-host-supported fraction of their newly accessible geography (raw species-level Spearman rho = −0.200). After controlling for the number of native resource regions, resolved larval hosts and host families, the coefficient of log contemporary/native resource-region gain was −0.221 (5,000-genus-bootstrap 95% percentile interval −0.298 to −0.165; 206 species, 124 genera). The negative association persisted in the prespecified 191-species host-taxonomy-adequate subset (coefficient −0.271, interval −0.369 to −0.197) and for the fractional contribution of the single most important recorded host (coefficient −0.150, interval −0.215 to −0.093). These coefficients summarize the **same reconstructed host-resource network** and cannot be interpreted as independent measures of larval survival or ecological resilience.

An archived BCE/Clarke supplement added 1,027 candidate host links to 81 butterflies, allowing a separate, paired 70-species comparison of the *mean number of introduced-host incidence supports per newly accessible region* (not the same response as the single-host fraction). In this catalogue sensitivity, the adjusted association between log proportional geographic gain and log mean support intensity was positive under both original HOSTS (coefficient +0.297, interval +0.177 to +0.572) and augmented HOSTS+BCE data (+0.577, interval +0.295 to +0.976; 53 genera). Median candidate supports per added region rose from 1.44 to 2.21 within the paired source panel. Within-species changes in geographic gain and mean support intensity across catalogue definitions were uncorrelated (r = −0.019), making a causal mechanism of host-source enrichment unsupported.

## Candidate Discussion paragraph (English)

Resource globalization can jointly modify the extent and the geographic redundancy of consumers' *potential* host opportunities. Although a majority of added butterfly × region cells in this reconstruction have only a single documented introduced host, the butterflies with larger proportional gains generally have more multiply supported added cells after accounting for recorded host-list breadth. This pattern contradicts an intuitively simple gain–fragility trade-off in the **recorded resource network**. It does not establish that globally expanding butterflies are ecologically buffered: both gain and redundancy follow algebraically from the same host-range unions, and local plant abundance, temporal availability, host acceptance and survival to adulthood were not measured. Moreover, expanding an evidence-based host catalogue can substantially change the resource baseline and apparent redundancy. Hence regional conservation assessments require verified local host use and suitable alternative resources rather than relying on global extent or diet breadth alone.

## Source and inference caveats
- Non-experimental posthoc exploratory analysis; direction of the initial gain–fragility hypothesis was fixed immediately before source regression and contradicted by results. The sign of the detected negative slope is source conditional.
- Host inventory adequacy sensitivity passes but cannot rule out family-specific incomplete records or correlated botanical introductions.
- Genus-cluster resampling is not a full butterfly phylogeny or independent cross-data validation.
- BCE source enriches European butterflies non-randomly; its `(H−A)/U` response is average support incidence, **not** the fraction with multiple hosts.
- The original global paper contains a stronger, independent claim of widespread **potential** resource geography change across the specialization spectrum; do not promote the addendum into a second manuscript without external ecological data.
