# Main figure legends | Integrated island-port-butterfly paper v0.2

These five figures serve ONE Research Article, *Islands and ports reveal the geography of introduced host plants and butterfly records*. Each panel carries its own population, endpoint and period. Figures 1–5 are newly rendered from public source-locked results, not evidence of actual host shipping, butterfly flight trajectories, larval host use or biological colonisation.

## Figure 1 | Islands and ports as windows onto butterfly resource geography

**Conceptual study design and units.** Known butterfly–larval-host identities are held fixed while plant ranges change from native-only to native plus introduced World Checklist of Vascular Plants (WCVP) statuses. The **239-butterfly** resource reconstruction generates potential host-resource geography; **126 insular and 237 mainland WGSRPD3 regions** provide the main contrast. Independent port-associated flora observations (1,596 GBIF-query-complete port buffers across 142 country codes; 98 effort-covered insular botanical regions), butterfly record forecasting (155 known species) and 225 butterfly candidate species across 51 selected original GSHHG landmass groups assess distinct observational links. **Solid** arrows identify the deterministic host-resource reconstruction. **Dashed** arrows indicate retrospective associations/predictors, **not identified causal relationships**. The mapless concept deliberately does not show a proven shipborne plant trajectory, larval feeding event, butterfly colonisation path or island transit relay.

## Figure 2 | Islands amplify the potential larval-host resources of butterflies

**A:** Summed candidate butterfly×WGSRPD3 region opportunity under native-only and contemporary known larval-host distributions: **4,829→10,570** in strict insular regions (**+118.9%**) and **21,215→29,901** in mainland regions (**+40.9%**). These are not butterfly abundance or range-size measurements. **B:** All **15** Level-1 biogeographic×log-area overlapping strata; each point is the insular minus mainland fraction of contemporary resource opportunities attributable to introduced plant geography in percentage points (point size reflects the source weighting). The weighted insular excess is **+10.7 percentage points**, with a descriptive within-stratum permutation p=0.0002. **C:** Fraction of the independently reconstructed 239 butterflies gaining at least one resource region across classes of larval host-family breadth: **82/97** one-family, **47/59** two-family, **44/50** three-to-five-family and **33/33** six-or-more-family. Overall opportunity increased from 26,530 to 41,083 (+54.9%); this resource gain is not a measured increase in butterfly occupancy. Fixed HOSTS interactions and WCVP native/introduced geography.

## Figure 3 | Port-rich islands contain introduced floras that include butterfly hosts

**A:** Coverage of the global WPI/GSHHG-anchored GBIF plant source: **3,630** registered port candidates, **1,596** plant-query-complete buffers across **142 country codes**, and **1,150** with indexed candidate introduced larval-host taxa. The source contains 687 distinct candidate introduced host plant species and 25,742 plant×port recorded combinations, not 25,742 distinct sampled seedlings or historical shipments. **B:** Independent regression estimates for **98** botanical-effort-covered insular WGSRPD3 regions: the association between modern WPI indexed port count (per log₂(1+ports) unit) and log introduced flora richness (**β=+0.2445**); distinct introduced larval-host richness (**β=+0.1639**); and host richness conditional on entire introduced flora (**β=−0.0415**). Different outcomes and controls preclude interpreting these three coefficients as a causal decomposition; confidence intervals were not invented. **C:** The ten plants with the largest credit to *global potential* butterfly resource gain, with the number of original **38** sampled port buffers across **17** landmass groups in which each accepted species name was GBIF-indexed in **2001–2010**. Eight of the ten had at least one record, jointly representing 20.7% of *global potential resource-credit contribution*. A zero bar indicates no index in this small search support, **not biological absence**. No evidence of local historical alien status, cargo transport, or use by the larvae of a particular indexed butterfly follows from the plant records.

## Figure 4 | Butterfly records and compatible introduced-host geography are associated

**A:** The original **225 butterfly candidate species**, each plotted by the number of potential added resource regions globally (239-butterfly parent source) and the count of 51-study-landmass port buffers with an indexed compatible introduced-host candidate. The raw species Spearman association is **ρ=+0.652** and descriptive partial rank adjusting host count, native resource extent and earlier indexed butterfly landmass breadth is **+0.550**. These variables share HOSTS/WCVP host identities and are not causally independent. **B:** Candidate port-host Jaccard similarity versus later-indexed butterfly community Jaccard similarity across **51 selected landmass groups, 1,275 dependent pairs**: later Spearman **ρ=+0.300**, earlier-butterfly association **+0.418**, distance-and-prior-butterfly adjusted descriptive partial rank **+0.215**. A logarithmic density scale shows frequency of the non-independent island pairs; no pair-independent p-value is implied. **C:** Of **1,163** combinations where a butterfly species was indexed later on both landmasses, **208 (17.9%)** had an introduced compatible-host candidate indexed at both landmass port buffers. A plant-family and butterfly/host network-degree-preserving null averaged **11.9%** over 199 swaps; the null does **not** preserve plant distribution geography and the plant indices pooled over **2001–2024** can postdate a butterfly. Consequently, the comparison is a **catalogue compatibility** result, not observed caterpillar feeding, independently dated host movement or a confirmed establishment mechanism. **D:** The separately sourced **155-butterfly, 208,482-evaluation-row** record model: adding host geography to climate and land cover reduces species-equal Brier/MSE loss by **4.12%** in a held-later-record period and **3.00%** in held spatial blocks. Contemporary plant geography was used; dates of actual local host introductions are not demonstrated.

## Figure 5 | Butterfly indexing supports geographic association, not a demonstrated island-to-island mechanism

**A:** In the 17-landmass, 38-port historical subset, 2001–2010 compatible host indexing preceded **34/113 (30.1%)** 2011–2017 butterfly first-index events, versus **25.2%** under a plant-family/host-degree null but **30.26%** under a plant-geography-preserving null. Host non-indexing is not absence. **B:** Five butterflies from an independent **225-species × 51-landmass** GBIF panel; points represent 2018–2022 first-index counts and median distance to the nearest earlier indexed conspecific *within sampled landmasses*, not known origins or flight distances. **C:** Among the **199-trait-matched** candidates (**83** late first-index cells), species-, island- and region-constrained nulls show source proximity (**p=0.00033**) and distance-adjusted marine access (**p=0.00233**). Grey intervals are 95% null references, not dispersal-rate confidence intervals. **D:** Adding uniform marine resistance beyond distance **worsened region-held-out Brier error by 6.48%**. In an indirect, positive-only migration-candidate subset, ocean-residual p changed from **0.047** (51 groups) to **0.229** (49) after excluding two port-rich landmasses from *both targets and candidate prior sources*. The **114 unlisted species** have unknown migration status. **No panel documents butterfly colonisation, larval feeding, ship transport or island relays.**
