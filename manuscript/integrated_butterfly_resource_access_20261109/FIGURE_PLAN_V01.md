# Single integrated GEB butterfly paper — five-figure editorial specification
**2026-11-09 | companion to INTEGRATED_RESEARCH_ARTICLE_V01.md | no new figure falsely claimed as rendered**

## Central claim and intended reader takeaway

**Human redistribution of known larval hosts changes which geographic regions are potentially accessible as resources to butterflies, without butterflies changing their known diets. The opportunity gain is particularly strong in insular regions, while actual indexed butterfly records retain source geography and accessibility structure that cannot yet be assigned causally to specific ships, host introductions or colonisation.**

A butterfly must appear explicitly as a taxon or measured response in Figures 2, 4 and 5, and the butterfly–host opportunity must be visible in Figure 3. Ports, island geometry and Circuitscape should be explanatory lenses, not the biological response. No abstract icons implying confirmed live butterfly shipping.

This is ONE paper. Five main display pieces, not five independent papers. In general, each figure shows one question, one denominator with a visible population label, one positive result and any contradictory diagnostic on the same outcome. Avoid comparing numerically unmatched coefficients and slopes as if they were the same unit.

## Figure 1 — Question and what the three samples can actually establish

**Title:** How globalization changes a butterfly's resource geography.

- A: Fixed host repertoire for two explicitly **illustrative, not observational** butterflies (one-family versus several families), contrasted with native-only and native-plus-introduced host geography.
- B: A resource/access/recording diagram: host species geography → potential larval resource opportunity (solid computational link); opportunity ↔ butterfly recorded geography (dashed association); prior indexed conspecific geography and modelled marine access → retrospective record prediction (dashed). The port → plant-shipment → larval host use → reproduction pathway is *unobserved*, displayed as a grey question rather than a positive causal edge.
- C: data-grain legend: 239 butterflies×WGSRPD3 resource regions; 155 butterfly recording predictions; 98 plant-index-effort insular regions; 225 butterflies×51 GSHHG/WPI landmass groups (199 species trait-complete, 111 indirect migration positive-list).
- Note: all graph connections in this figure are explanatory; no synthetic case data should be presented as real observations.

**Necessary source:** frozen GEB HOSTS/WCVP region data, new public51 source provenance, claims R1/O2/P1/A3. This may be a precisely authored diagram; it is not yet rendered in this branch.

## Figure 2 — Which butterflies benefited from host redistribution?

**Question:** Does taxonomic dietary specialization predict change in geographic resource opportunity?

- A: paired aggregate native-only **26,530** versus contemporary **41,083** butterfly×WGSRPD3 resource combinations; annotate +14,553 (+54.9%) without presenting that as butterfly range growth.
- B: by host family breadth: 82/97 (one-family), 47/59 (two), 44/50 (three-to-five), 33/33 (six-plus) butterflies gained. A continuous plot retains full rho **0.008** and 95% interval **[−0.111,+0.128]**, and posthoc equivalence test p=.078 as a caveat rather than a proven zero.
- C: butterfly-facing plant contributions: 670 host plants contribute; **38 account for half** of added butterfly–region credit, top10 25.1%, annotated examples (*Medicago sativa*, *Poa pratensis*, *Oxalis corniculata*, *Cynodon dactylon*, *Dactylis glomerata*). Use a cumulative concentration curve; not a plant-import flow map.
- D: one line or inset: FAO crop exclusion still **46–47%** increase, so association not wholly a crop accounting artifact.

**Provenance:** frozen global GEB v0.2 full data and its original numerical receipts. **Design priority 1** because this is the generalized conceptual novelty.

## Figure 4 — Do the added resources help explain where butterflies are recorded?

**Question:** Is the resource envelope relevant beyond adding cells to a map?

- A: **155 butterfly species, 208,482** prediction rows: +4.12% time-held species-equal MSE reduction and +3.00% spatial-held when host information is added to climate plus land cover. Mark that the predictor host geography is **contemporary**, so *only butterfly records* are held out by period.
- B: **225 candidate butterflies×51 landmass groups**, first GBIF indexes **221** in 2011–2017 and **96** in 2018–2022 (new public 2026 reindex). For 199 species with trait descriptors and prior other-island same-species indexes, **187** and **83** cases are used in the later access test. Show a *true species-based visualization*, e.g. event count distribution by butterfly and source distance with selected named butterflies such as *Zizula hylax*, *Nymphalis antiopa*, and *Vanessa cardui*. Do not imply pairwise source is biological source.
- C: 51-port-landmass assemblage observational coupling: host similarity vs later butterfly rho +.300, earlier butterfly +.418 and distance/prior adjusted later +.215. Annotate 1,275 *dependent* pairs and use a density scatter, not 1,275 independent-case hypothesis test.
- D: Optional 32-species occurrence overlap 66/115 remains Supplementary, not a false population realization rate (separate 13/920 archival island record overlap also SI only).

**Provenance:** original 155 forecast fixed source and OOF scores; new 225×51 recorded butterfly source and 270 events, source-pinned PR #151 host pair similarity.

## Figure 5 — Which butterflies have source/access-aligned recorded geography, and is it sea-specific?

**Question:** How much of butterfly recording geography transfers beyond ordinary distance and local sampling, and does it vary consistently by butterfly traits?

- A: one species-focused observed record-source distance distribution in the **187 early / 83 late** trait-complete prior-conspecific-source risk: source distance is the nearest sampled earlier indexed conspecific landmass, not movement route. Restrict to original 51 landmasses.
- B: constrained null for late butterfly first-index layout within original geographic group: geodesic proximity **p=.00033**, distance-residual ocean access **p=.00233** (exploratory), with the 51-landmass sample stated.
- C: independent **geographic region-held-out** test of the same binary first-index model: adding uniform sea resistance beyond source distance **worsens late Brier by 6.48%**. This juxtaposition demonstrates why a conditional association is not the same as transferable prediction or a causal effect.
- D: trait identifiability: wing-size × distance gains +0.349% island / +1.861% region / +0.134% species are modest; within-region wing-size × distance p=.227 and wing-size × sea residual p=.645. The subset of **111** indirectly author-listed migratory butterfly candidates is positive-only; its remaining 114 species are UNKNOWN, not sedentary. Heavy-port source landmass sensitivity ID9/15 removes late within-region residual-sea signal (p=.0473→.229) while geography remains (p=.00067→.00267).
- E: show **no arrows tracing island-to-island biological movement**. Current-density Circuitscape map, 100/199 local AIS placebos, full historical index negative and direct host/ship pathway tests go in SI as mechanisms **not identified**.

**Provenance:** exact public51 source, OOF scores, centered 2x2 constrained null, author-selected migration proxy and 49-landmass influence tests.

## What is intentionally cut from the main paper

- A standalone huge marine-current map is NOT the concluding main figure: the map measures assumed electrical demand and resistance, not butterfly paths.
- Original JBI Figure 5 transport-specific AIS / liner transport is now **SI negative specificity**, because it neither isolates ship effect nor changes the core resource–access discovery.
- The historical 17-island small-outcome pilot and four identified original-year positive species are **SI provenance**; the 225×51 independent source supersedes them as the main butterfly record example. Original private Parquet remains unavailable.
- Wastewater, nutrient deposition, colonial-era specific voyages, Cirsium/Campanula analogies and speculative stepping-stone coalescence are outside this manuscript.
- Do not duplicate the global +54.9% result as if a separate JBI paper replicated it.
