## Scientific story and statements supported by original sources

**Main question:** With fixed documented larval-host identities, how does redistributing plant native and introduced regional distributions change the mapped availability of butterfly food, and is this opportunity response spatially and taxonomically unequal?

- **Island baseline:** 239 butterflies × 369 botanical regions, +14,553 documented host-coverage region combinations (+54.9%). Islands +118.9% from native coverage 16%, mainland +40.9% from native 37.5%; island gap fill 22.7% versus mainland 24.5%, so larger relative gains do not prove superior island efficacy. Introduced-only sources newly supplied 54.3% of currently covered island source combinations.
- **Partner-side inequality:** 38 of 670 contributing host plant taxa account for half the added source units (contributor Gini .777); geographically widespread original native-host resources among 239 butterflies predict more *absolute* added island units (rho +.579; broadest 59 species 39.6% versus narrowest 60 11.2%); 82/97 one-family source butterflies also gain new source regions. These are not observed butterfly winners or competition effects.
- **Flora, ports and sea:** Introduced botanical flora port coefficient +.2854 for 126 island regions, direct alien-minus-native contrast uncertain; alien whole-flora median island-pair overlap ~10.3× native. Undirected ocean circuit original held-island introduced flora prediction +4.239%, geographically strict +2.735% with fivefold interval spanning zero; neither identifies actual ocean currents or seed transports.
- **Independent consumers:** Distinct 155-butterfly later indexed GBIF model gains +4.12% source MSE, spatial +3.00%; 224-species recorded butterfly community source H-after-O gain +.091% in 169 primary regions, but strict 133-region eligible cohort changes sign to −.0764%, and across-South-America communities predict worse after H. Named endemic Pieris cheiranthi larvae are documented feeding on introduced Tropaeolum majus; not general island endemic population dependence.

## Nearby published work and exact novelty boundary

The 2026 GEB Couto et al. study (DOI 10.1111/geb.70292) already measures 969 *established* non-native moths/butterflies across 258 regions. Hatfield et al. 2026 (DOI 10.1111/geb.70195) analyze actual species contribution to assemblage homogenization/differentiation, and the 2026 GEB Mediterranean 105-butterfly genetics paper (DOI 10.1111/geb.70263) analyzes genuine insular genetic separation. Braga, Halsch and Rashid already demonstrate exotic host use and host geographic constraints in other butterfly contexts. The novel source estimand here is the global, fixed-diet *plant-resource redistribution opportunity*, its uneven island/native baseline and asymmetric plant/butterfly partner supply, accompanied but not conflated with independent recorded consumers.

The published examples also show the journal's normal evidence standard: actual monitored butterfly populations are not equivalent to occurrence-indexing; independently verified alien butterfly establishment is not equivalent to WCVP documented host plant presence; and measured botanical similarity is not actual biological butterfly homogenization.
