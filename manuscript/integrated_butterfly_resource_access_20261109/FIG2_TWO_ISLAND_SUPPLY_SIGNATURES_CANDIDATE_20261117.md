# Fig. 2 alternative: Two observed supply-context signatures, not two identified transport routes

**Date:** 17 November 2026. **Status:** Candidate only for one integrated butterfly GEB manuscript; retain the original Figure 2 ("global contributors at ports") and all original accepted butterfly-resource and recording results until the authors explicitly choose final figure hierarchy. This note is based on executed source-locked validation; it is not a new paper, submission or causal identification.

## Proposed English main Results paragraph (only if this candidate figure is selected)

At the island-regional scale, the geographic association of registered ports with introduced plant richness and the dispersal-trait composition of native versus introduced floras suggest two *different, nonexclusive candidate supply contexts*. Across 126 botanical island regions, the relationship between the contemporary World Port Index count and the number of accepted introduced vascular plant species was positive after adjustment for island area, native plant richness and broad botanical region (slope per log2[1+indexed port count] = 0.221). This observational association does not identify historical plant transport by ships. In a separate European-biased EuDiS trait-matched subset of 22 island regions, native plants exhibited a higher prevalence of morphological thalassochory than introduced plants within the same botanical region (median within-region difference = 2.95 percentage points; descriptive region bootstrap interval = 2.04–4.53, with 17 positive, four negative and one tied region). A thalassochorous morphology is not evidence that a given species arrived by ocean current, and these 22 regions are not a random global sample of native flora. The two patterns illustrate plausible natural and human-mediated supply pathways without demonstrating their causal separation.

## Why this is not evidence of two separate realized delivery routes

1. **Global taxon-level cross-check**: The original source accepted WCVP v13 126-island matrices contain 184,997 native and 39,024 introduced species×region records, respectively. Jaccard similarity medians across the same 7,875 island pairs are 0.004487 (native) and 0.046030 (introduced), but these marginals are differently sized and documented; a ratio of medians is not a ship-transport effect.
2. **Sea geometry vs ports**: On the original 104 GSHHG-anchorable regions and 5,355 complete native/alien pairs, island-held-out prediction from *undirected* GSHHG shortest-water detour adds MSE improvement +2.234% for native and +1.775% for introduced flora, while current WPI port-count product adds −0.077% and +0.043%. On strict two-unseen-island folds (1,089 unique pairs), GSHHG's positive increments (+1.735%/+1.858%) have intervals crossing zero; port proxy no longer improves either. This supports neither native-only sea-current delivery nor alien-only ship delivery. Original electric Circuitscape resistance and shortest-water distance are different quantities.
3. **Actual verified ship OD is sparse**: Only 7/7,875 pairs in the original 126-island botanical universe have any name+geography-verified WPI port-to-port 2020 AIS episode. Missing 2020 triplet matches cannot be converted to no ship routes, and a 2020 voyage cannot cause pre-2020 WCVP historical origin.
4. **Real directional NOAA is now piloted but cannot identify species transport**: Public *legacy* NOAA OSCAR 1/3-degree ~5-day 15-m u/v, **decimated** to 1 degree, was integrated at 280 three-date coastal starts on 94/126 original regions for up to 90 days in 2005. Among 7,313 source-evaluable ordered botanical region pairs, only 12 show at least one modeled trajectory within 50 km of another TDWG3 shoreline and four have a GSHHG raster contact within another exact TDWG3 region. Ten of 12 modeled positive pairs include WCVP accepted taxa currently native at source and alien at recipient, and **zero of those pairs** overlap the seven strict 2020 AIS pairs. Yet taxonomic co-listing is not dated seed transfer; model proximity is not landfall or viable plant establishment.
5. **Same-origin negative controls do not resolve specificity**: Of the 12 modeled current-positive pairs, only two have at least three distance/richness matched nonapproach controls from the same source; the observed two native-to-alien co-listed positives equal the expected two from these controls. No unique current→alien species-transmission association can be inferred. The 10/12 source co-listing must not be cited as specific current-mediated colonization.
