# One GEB butterfly paper: evidence-to-story bridge, November 18, 2026

**One paper:** Human-redistributed host plants disproportionately expand potential larval resources in island regions. This document lays out why five source-supported ecological results belong in one manuscript rather than in separate port, host, and island papers.

## Narrative order (five scientific transitions)

1. **Initial island resource deficit** — In the original 239 named butterflies, native hosts cover 16.0% of island species-region cells versus 37.5% mainland. Host range redistribution adds +118.9% potential cells on islands and +40.9% mainland. Unfilled-cell fractions recovered by introduced plants are similar (22.7% versus 24.5%), so the primary island amplification is due to the baseline, not uniquely effective island plant introductions.

2. **Human-assembled plant supply** — Port counts covary with the full introduced flora (98 botanically observed island regions beta +0.2445) and known larval host candidate taxa (+0.1639). The excess port specificity of hosts after all introduced plants are controlled is not robust. The top 10 host plants contribute 25.1% of global added cells; this documents concentration in source portfolios, not observed port transport of the same propagules.

3. **From plant pool to actual larvae** — Location-identified HOSTS literature shows an island excess of reported alien hosts (42.4% versus 9.1% locality medians; crop-excluded 20.9% vs 8.1%), but the same source data combined with WCVP family-matched botanical abundance give no robust extra preference (67 shared butterflies, family-adjusted excess −3.87pp [−15.85,+8.13]). Independently observed *Pieris cheiranthi* larvae feed on regionally introduced *Tropaeolum majus* on Tenerife (wild photo) and La Palma (garden photo); separate wild-endemic larvae use native *Hirschfeldia*. Direct local use is possible, but not uniform resource replacement or population growth.

4. **Actual butterfly records carry host information** — In 155 known butterflies, host geography improves later GBIF-record index MSE by 4.12% (spatial blocks 3.00%). The 224-butterfly global beta model yields at most a tiny host-specific additional effect (+0.157%). These are different outcomes and samples; the resource gain cannot be read as an observed +54.9% range expansion.

5. **Geographic access filters the expression** — On 51 indexed island groups, uniform-ocean effective resistance adds 5.08% held-island butterfly-community prediction, and AIS/wind marine descriptors provide 6.49% MSE gain beyond host composition in a separate model. The host×marine interaction and geographically double-unseen transfer are not confirmatory. No observed vessel-delivered seed-to-butterfly colonization chain.

## Independent external-validity correction

The original 239 sampled butterflies include none of the **18 independently classified extant Macaronesian endemics**. Eight endemics had exact host-species identities identified in a nonuniformly sourced subsample, four had positive *global plant-region* additions, none acquired newly host-present home-archipelago cells. This is a source-ascertainment boundary, not refutation of the original source-selected 239 result or proof of no resource use.
