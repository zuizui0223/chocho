# One GEB butterfly paper: island–port–marine evidence bridge (18 November 2026)

**Working title:** *Island ports and marine geography accompany unequal butterfly resource gains from plant redistribution*. **Status:** one source-qualified, unsubmitted, coauthor-review Draft paper in PR #179; not an identified causal transport mechanism.

## The five Results transitions (different estimands, one ecological question)

1. **Native starting geography magnifies island opportunities.** Original fixed HOSTS×WCVP 239 butterflies: potential resource +54.9% globally (206 species), island +118.9% versus mainland +40.9%. Native host region coverage 16.0% island / 37.5% mainland, contemporary 35.1% / 52.8%; **mainland–island coverage gap 21.4→17.7 points**. Gap-filling fraction **22.7% island versus 24.5% mainland**: island advantage is baseline amplification, *not* more efficient plant introduction. Among 5,741 added island opportunity pairs, **2,271 (39.6%)** belong to the 59 butterflies with greatest *native* island-region host coverage, a resource-geographic-breadth rather than flight-distance classification.

2. **Ports track the introduced whole-flora supply pool.** WPI port richness associates with WCVP **whole alien flora** under distinct island/regression controls: **β+0.221** (126; native host-resource adjusted), **β+0.233** (123; native whole-flora adjusted), **β+0.2445** (98; botanical effort adjusted). Known host candidate β+0.1639 on the 98, but **no positive independent host-specific port surplus conditional on whole alien flora** (β−0.0415; p=.093). This is port-associated vegetation, not observed vessel-delivered seeds. Native-flora conditional *pairwise* port-proxy effects near zero do not establish a causal native-versus-alien contrast.

3. **Natural and human-linked distribution proxies must be cross-checked.** In 22 sufficiently trait-covered botanical islands, native minus introduced **EuDiS seawater-dispersal morphology +2.95 pp** (descriptive CI +2.04 to +4.53). For 104 botanical regions the archived uniform-ocean *circuit* predictor has an introduced-flora +4.239% versus native −0.110% held-out MSE gain. A different 104-island **shortest-water-path** model improves **both** native +2.234% and alien +1.775% on the same 5,355 pairs; two-endpoints-unseen intervals encompass zero. These do not prove *ocean currents moved the natives / vessels moved the aliens*. The coarse 2005 OSCAR trajectory pilot and extremely limited 2020 AIS route matches remain sensitivity evidence.

4. **Butterflies sometimes use the newly available food.** Independent Macaronesian fauna 41 living butterflies, **18 endemics, 0/18** included in the original 239. Eight source-selected endemic known-host species are not a representative replacement. Original photographed *Pieris cheiranthi* larvae on regionally introduced *Tropaeolum majus* (Tenerife wild; La Palma garden) show direct use in a named endemic, but no new host-present home-archipelago region, successful adult emergence attributable to alien hosts or population benefit. HOSTS island-locality alien-host use (42.4% versus 9.1% mainland) loses evidence for *extra preference* when plant-family supply is matched (−3.87 pp; interval crosses zero).

5. **Indexed butterfly geography has independent filters.** In a distinct **155-butterfly** recording-prediction panel, host geography improves later-index MSE **4.12%**, held spatial blocks **3.00%**; the independent 224-butterfly global assemblage-beta increment is very small (**+0.157%**). In the sampled 51-island butterfly network the uniform-circuit +5.082% and AIS/wind beyond-host +6.488% are positive **retrospective source fits**, but stricter 990-pair both-endpoints-unseen host/geography/effort conditions give only **+0.6776% for AIS alone**, and **−4.6986% for AIS+wind**. Different predictor baselines mean this is **not a direct numerical correction** from +5.08 to +0.68. The source-overlapping species-level port/resource ρ=.652 belongs in SI. None of these measures butterfly colonization.

## 18 November matched-origin and tropical source qualification (PR #179)

- **One matched port design:** original 126 WCVP islands, identical `log2(1+ports) + log(area) + TDWG-Level1` controls; native β **+0.0954** (descriptive HC3 95% −0.0774 to +0.2683), alien β **+0.2854** (+0.0622 to +0.5087), and directly paired alien-minus-native β **+0.1900** (−0.0187 to +0.3987). Alien-associated signal is positive, but native zero effect and a proven origin-specific difference are **not established**. The 123 source-eligible sensitivity has the same pattern. [Source and independently rerunnable zero-dependency verifier](../../scripts/verify_island_port_native_alien_matched_20261118.py).
- **Different marine predictors:** archived 104-region uniform-ocean **circuit** +4.239% alien versus −0.110% native, whereas separately reconstructed 104-region GSHHG **shortest water path** +2.234% native and +1.775% alien. Do not describe either as measured ocean-current direction or actual shipping.
- **Tropical caution, not a global trait result:** Tonga 2011 original *broad water dispersal* n=364 native (137 positives) / n=309 post-European introduced (137) gives native-minus-introduced −6.70 pp; its multi-guild water category is **not EuDiS seawater thalassochory**. A 2020 Tonga 1,020-species source exists, but its ZIP and WCVP species-level crosswalk are not yet audited. [Tropical source ledger](../../results/tropical_dispersal_source_bridge_20261118/README.md).
- **Four main figures and word target:** The new Figure 4 is a four-panel source-verified SVG comparing port slopes, EuDiS traits, distinct marine plant predictors and strict butterfly community transfer; old 51-island positive plots remain in source appendix. The main manuscript before references has **approximately 4,905 words** under a simple whitespace-plus-Markdown token count; full removed source panels were retained in Supplement S.F instead of deleted. No new ecological model was fitted on already-seen outcomes to secure a favorable conclusion.

**Geographic robustness added to source audit.** Native/introduced common-126 port associations were refit across nine leave-one-Level1-out populations and the 91 port-positive botanical islands. All nine introduced βs remained positive (+.202 to +.380), and the 91-island introduced coefficient was +.311 (HC3 +.042 to +.580). The *direct* origin-class difference remained uncertain (91-island HC3 −.012 to +.465); no exclusive port mechanism can be inferred. [Source-locked summary](../../results/port_native_alien_geographic_robustness_20261118/README.md).

**New stricter circuit result (18 Nov, separate from shortest-path).** With all source pairs, original ridge alpha, geographic baseline, ports, and five source-hashed folds frozen, authentic 104-botanical-island *uniform ocean electrical resistance* was tested on 1,089 dyads with both endpoints absent from training. Native gain **−0.044%** (descriptive fivefold CI **−0.984 to +0.431%**); introduced **+2.735%** (**−1.649 to +5.508%**); source-paired relative-gain difference **+2.779 points** (**−1.693 to +5.400**). Introduced source positive direction persists nominally from original +4.239%, but the strict CI and contrast cross zero; hence neither a confirmed transferable flora-specific maritime effect nor evidence for historical currents/shipping. [Original code/CI/source](../../results/WCVP126_circuit_strict_geographic_transfer_20261118/README.md). Keep source original results in main, no replacement of R08, and do not interpret the plant test as butterfly immigration.

**Zero-floor/denominator outcome audit (executed 18 Nov).** On the original 5,355 paired WCVP flora dyads, exact-zero Jaccard is **16.51% native** versus **9.79% introduced**, and among nonzero native/alien dyads the medians remain **0.00679 versus 0.05837**. Across 1,089 strict source-held dyads the incremental circuit gain for any-shared-species Brier was **+0.418% native versus +0.331% alien**, for Sørensen **+0.057% versus +3.017%**, and on 1,043 pairwise Simpson-defined dyads **+1.586% versus +0.898%**, reversing the relative point direction. Thus the Jaccard positive introduced-circuit signal is **an overlap-magnitude- and index-dependent spatial diagnostic**, not a stable exclusively alien circuit mechanism. None of the matched paired-difference intervals for full-source Jaccard, binary, Sørensen or Simpson excludes zero under fivefold resampling. Positive post hoc double-positive subgroup findings are explicitly labeled as response-selected. [Full source CI](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/44293674389) and [report](../../results/WCVP126_circuit_floor_diagnostic_20261118/README_JA.md). Keep the original plant/port and butterfly positive results unchanged.

**Source-verified island resource-assemblage convergence (18 November).** We went beyond the +118.9% island count to the 239-butterfly potential-resource *composition*. Original PR #118 native/current bit matrix (369 regions, 26,530→41,083 resource units, 355 native-supported regions) gives Jaccard Δ **+0.2367 among 6,328 island–island pairs** versus **+0.1504 among 27,730 mainland–mainland pairs**, raw gap +0.0862. The old Level1 constrained exact species/region gain-margin null explains most of the gap (residual **+0.00669**); the new Level1×island/mainland constrained null reduces it to **+0.00119**, and within-Level1 island versus mainland residual is **−0.00011**. This is a strong observed **potential** compositional reallocation but only weak evidence for additional *island-specific sorting* after conditioning on which butterfly/resource combinations were newly available. [Immutable source run #44299353236](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/44299353236) and [numeric report](../../results/island_mainland_butterfly_resource_beta_20261118/README_JA.md). No migration, carrying-capacity increase or true GBIF butterfly homogenization is implied. Source-only exploratory and not independent of fixed HOSTS/WCVP network.

**Resulting safe working claim:** *Native and introduced island floras form contrasting contemporary spatial layers. Ports are associated with richer introduced floras; undirected marine geography and different dispersal traits add source-dependent information. Relocated known larval hosts enlarge potential butterfly food geography, sometimes yielding documented larval use, but their links to historical plant dispersal and newly established butterfly populations remain unidentified.*

## 19 November: independent butterfly observation effort audit — evidence qualified, positive source preserved

**New source-only diagnostics archived to main** without merging or submitting this Draft GEB manuscript: [independent observed 224-species regional uncertainty](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/blob/main/results/gbif224_region_uncertainty_20261119/README_JA.md) and [original year-by-region GBIF effort-thinning sensitivity](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/blob/main/results/gbif224_annual_effort_thinning_20261119/README_JA.md).

- The previous independent global **224-butterfly / 169-WGSRPD3** observational host-after-other-alien MSE increment **+0.091%** reproduces exactly; descriptive 4,999 region-resampling 95% range **−0.0915 to +0.2621%**. Original eligibility variation changes the cohort: relaxed 194 regions **+0.3325%**; stringent 133 **−0.0764%**. Region-held-out scores share training/test pairs, so the intervals are NOT iid population confidence intervals, and the eligibility contrast is not effort causation.
- Recovered original 224-species source annual aggregates at pinned commit `dfd49cfcf9fb01a3db47cc611aae8cdd9c53cda0`, SHA checked **91,882 species×region×year cells** and **5,390 region×year effort cells** from the original 14,637,152 GBIF records. The 169-region raw indexed butterfly-community Jaccard increases **0.114213 (2001–2010) → 0.167880 (2018–2024)**, +0.053667.
- On the **same 14,196 original region pairs**, 499 randomized record thinnings to within-region period-minimum recording-intensity proxies reduce the raw Jaccard difference to **+0.015064** (datasetKey×calendar-day standardisation; 71.9% smaller) or **+0.002914** (indexed record-count standardisation; 94.6% smaller). These are strong **assumption-bound recording-intensity sensitivities**, *not* a fitted detection model, 95% biological CI, or an estimate of the causal fraction explained by observers. Prior and late cover 10 vs 7 years; source GBIF records are opportunistic.
- Thus neither the pooled GBIF butterfly Jaccard rise nor the weak host-specific improvement licenses a claim that introduced hosts caused real butterfly community homogenisation, establishment, recruitment, or abundance increase. The original potential-host resource +54.9% globally, +118.9% islands, independent *Pieris cheiranthi* larval feeding, port–alien flora β +0.2854 and the separately qualified sea-geometry results **remain intact**. Do not subtract numeric potential-resource gains from observed butterfly prediction gains: units, taxa sets, time bases and geography differ.

**Draft manuscript decision:** keep the contemporary plant supply / potential-resource island-port GEB ecological narrative in Main, keep independent observed butterfly results as evidence that potential availability is not equivalent to realized community composition, and report effort sensitivity in Supplement S.F or observational limitations. No historical seed delivery or butterfly colonisation mechanism is newly identified. Main-source archival PRs #183 and #184 are merged; [draft article PR #179](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/pull/179) still requires coauthor and submission-gate review.

## 19 November follow-up: known-host information in effort-matched independent butterfly records

**New source-verified result, archived to main via PR #185**: original independent GBIF butterfly community cohort **224 indexed species × 169 WGSRPD3 regions** (14,196 pairwise comparisons), source original H-after-other-alien MSE improvement **+0.0912908%**. After source-only stochastic observation intensity matching, with each draw re-simulating **both** prior butterfly Jaccard covariate and later observed butterfly Jaccard response, original **M2→M3** retrospective Ridge comparison remains positive in both pre-specified proxies: **datasetKey×calendar-day median +0.214956%** (conditional 29-draw Monte Carlo central 95% range +0.065888 to +0.470555%; 29/29 positive) and **indexed record count median +0.437833%** (+0.154318 to +0.736163%; 29/29 positive). Source original full four-model/169-region heldout errors and every pair's pre/post Jaccard were exactly reproduced before these simulations. No simulated both-empty butterfly dyads.

This is the first retrospective **observed community** positive H-after-O source result explicitly checked under two region-period recording-intensity assumptions. Its **magnitude stays below one percent of held-region MSE**, unlike the much larger *potential resource* gains computed with common HOSTS/WCVP. The 29 simulations are not independent geographical replications or population confidence intervals. The original prior/later observation effort and record-count controls beyond the thinned prior Jaccard feature remain source-original rather than simulated. Contemporary GloNAF/HOSTS plants do not identify historical host arrival and GBIF does not measure true occupancy, field feeding, successful recruitment, colonisation, competition or abundance.

It is now accurate to say **catalogued larval host geography provides a small, repeatedly positive incremental geographic predictor of independently recorded butterflies under two explicit proxy-thinning assumptions**. It remains inaccurate to claim ports/ships caused the host spread or that potentially available hosts caused a real butterfly community homogenisation. In the original raw 169-region record community, 2001–2010 to 2018–2024 pairwise Jaccard rise +0.053667 shrank to +0.015064/+0.002914 under source-proxy rarefaction; that effort sensitivity is not a causal measured bias fraction.
