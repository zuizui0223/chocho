# ONE GEB paper: what is the discovery? (editorial claim lock, 2026-11-17)

**Scope:** Source-verified narrative selection, NOT additional models or evidence. Primary manuscript in [PR #157](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/pull/157), kept as one draft. Source numbers are pinned in [the canonical machine ledger](../../results/port_weed_current_review_20261117/ONE_PAPER_CIRCUIT_RESULTS_LEDGER_20261117.json). Previous source PRs remain archival.

## Single ecological question

**When familiar larval-host plants are redistributed by people, which island butterflies acquire new *potential* resources, and how does that newly assembled resource geography relate to ports and marine connectivity?**

The answer is stronger than either "more introduced plants means more butterflies" or "ships move butterflies." It is a **resource-coverage effect with a geographic network signature**, with an explicit boundary between **potential resources** and **observed establishment**.

## Positive evidence, ONE ordered storyline

1. **The surprising island amplification is a starting-state effect, not a greater fraction of gaps filled.** Native hosts initially cover **16.0%** of butterfly × island-region combinations versus **37.5%** on mainland; introduced hosts fill **22.7% vs 24.5%** of remaining gaps. Island resources therefore increase **+118.9%**, mainland **+40.9%**. Across 239 butterflies globally, fixed known hosts add **14,553 butterfly × region opportunities (+54.9%)**, with **206/239** gaining; crop removal retains ~46–47% expansion. This is a **consumer-specific geographic availability** result, not plant richness or observed butterfly abundance.

   

   **Independent observed GBIF recording layer now source-verified:** The original public **225 butterflies × 51 landmasses** have cumulative indexed-community Jaccard **0.121 (2001–2010) → 0.162 (through 2017) → 0.178 (through 2022)** across the same 1,275 pairs. **945 pairs (74.1%)** became more similar after 317 first-indexed butterfly×island cells. Keeping each butterfly's added-island index count, each island's newly indexed butterfly count and five-group geography fixed, four source-verified Markov chains (796 configurations) predict mean Jaccard increase **+0.04961** versus observed **+0.05681** (excess **+0.00720**; chain means stable). Among the matched **225 butterflies**, the broadest native HOSTS-footprint quarter contributed **163/317 (51.4%)** of later first-indexed cells versus 23/317 in the narrowest; still, species-specific *new host resource additions* give no independent positive partial-rank link to later butterfly indices after native host footprint, known host count and earlier indexed butterfly breadth (**−0.068**). Source panels differ in geography and observational process. **GBIF index convergence is not biological butterfly colonization or proven plant-caused homogenization**. This is the third panel of main Figure 3, with former host-use uncertainty moved to Supplementary Figure S14.

**New butterfly-first core result (executed and source-verified, not merely an editorial suggestion):** The broadest quarter of *native known-host resource footprints* received **2,271/5,741 (39.6%)** new island resource opportunities, versus **644/5,741 (11.2%)** for the narrowest (ρ native-host geography versus island additions **+0.579**). Across **7,797** comparable island pairs, reconstructed *potential butterfly-consumer* Jaccard similarity rose **0.140→0.368**; **92.3%** of pairs became more similar. However, a 199-step-report geographic species×island fixed-margin null expected **+0.224** of the observed **+0.227** increase; additional composition-specific excess is only **~0.0038** Jaccard. The candidate consumers and similarity are derived from HOSTS/WCVP plant resource geographies, not from new butterfly occupancy; the separate **225-species historic 51-island GBIF index breadth** association with the same island resource gains is only **ρ=+0.198**. This makes butterfly beneficiaries and *potential* shared food a direct result, but observed butterfly homogenization remains untested.

2. **Ports identify one human-associated setting of the new resource-bearing botanical layer.** Island port number and *all introduced vascular plant* richness co-vary (126 regions, adjusted **β=+0.221**). In 98 botanical-effort-covered islands, the host-candidate port correlation is positive but has no independent host-specific surplus once all alien flora is controlled (**β=−0.0415**, p=0.093). The important inference is a broadened **plant supply pool**, not active transport of particular HOSTS taxa. A separate 1,596 completed-port search finds **1,150** with a candidate known introduced larval host, **687** candidate taxa, and **25,742** plant×port combinations. Of the **10 largest resource-contributing plants**, **8** are indexed during 2001–2010 in 38 historical study-port buffers; this is taxonomic and temporal overlap, not a shipping event.

3. **Native and introduced botanical layers bear distinct but nonexclusive sea-related signatures.** In 22 EuDiS-covered regions the **native floras** have **+2.95 percentage points** more morphology consistent with thalassochory than introduced floras (17 positive, 4 negative, one tie); this is dispersal-*potential* morphology, not documented current-carried arrivals. Across the same 104 coastal WCVP regions/5,355 pairs, uniform undirected **electrical marine resistance** improves **introduced-flora Jaccard predictions +4.239%** but does not improve native-flora prediction (**−0.110%**); the matched difference is **+4.349 points**. However, *shortest-water distance* improves **both** groups (+2.234% native, +1.775% introduced), so native floras are not "unrelated to the sea." Strict 1,089 two-unseen-island circuit transfer retains the alien-positive direction (+2.735%) with 5fold interval crossing 0. Nothing identifies true ocean-current direction or separates ship from other water-associated histories.

4. **Butterfly resources and indexed butterfly communities are also spatially organized, not proven plant-mediated arrivals.** On a **different original 51-landmass/1,275-pair** butterfly panel, marine circuit topology adds **+5.082%** held-island MSE improvement beyond declared geographic/history controls. Candidate introduced-host Jaccard and later butterfly assemblage similarity associate (**ρ=+0.300**; distance-and-earlier-adjusted descriptive rank r **+0.215**). In a separate **155-butterfly recording prediction** task, adding HOSTS geography to climate/land cover reduces later-period error **+4.12%**, spatial-block heldout **+3.00%**, and ≥100 km **+2.74%**. These support *conditional correspondence/predictive value*, NOT an observed ship→host→butterfly chain. Butterfly and WCVP botanical pair panels are different samples, endpoints and estimands.

5. **Named consumer connections establish relevance, not an extra island preference.** 1,928 butterfly–host–locality reports, 67 butterflies shared across the island/mainland host-report contrast; 29 butterfly–plant pairs occur in both contexts, with **13** plants native on mainland but regionally introduced on islands. The raw same-butterfly origin contrast is **+11.32 pp** island-minus-mainland, but the plant-family composition expectation is **+15.19 pp** (residual −3.87 pp, wide interval). We therefore report the actual named *recorded use/association* rather than excess preference or increased larval fitness.

## What is a limitation, not the discovery?

- **Do not use "stepping stones", "human-current channel", "shipborne seed delivery", "marine currents make alien floras similar", or "reproduction on introduced hosts caused butterfly expansion" as established results.** Both electrical circuit and historical particle paths are not biological dispersal observations. The 2005 OSCAR pilot has 12 model shoreline approach directions out of 7,313 eligible and only four other-region coarse-mask contacts; its recipient comparison had no excess over matched expectation. Move to Supplement/Limitations, do not leave as a headline Results section.
- **Independence is fragile:** 51-island uniform circuit +5.08% is correct under its original baseline; adding source-verified marginal candidate-host richness and early general plant-recording effort attenuates uniform circuit *after hosts* to **+0.712% LOIO** (95% [−0.665,+2.009]%) and **−0.522% both-unseen**, thus no robust independent sea effect on butterfly recording. Retain adverse results in Supplement S.C and main Limitations. This does not invalidate the different 104-region whole-alien plant result.
- **Time-correct species-specific HOSTS test is especially important:** 218 butterflies, 51 islands, 10,431 at-risk species-island cells, 306 late initial GBIF indices. Pre-2011 compatible host GBIF indexing has crude later butterfly-index rates **4.93% (53/1,075)** vs **2.70% (253/9,356)**. Controlling species/island fixed effects and *nearest prior indexed conspecific geographic distance* eliminates the positive association (**−1.103 pp** with interval spanning 0); adding sea resistance **−0.890 pp**. Source-aware island/region heldout Brier performance is *worse* with historical host indicator (−0.271% / −0.382%). These are in Supplement S.C and Limitations. Early HOSTS taxa cannot be individually certified as historically *alien* on the island. This rules out simple claims of an independently identified host-driven establishment effect, NOT the potential-resource discovery.
- **Potential host-resource homogenization and independently observed GBIF recording-index convergence are different measurements, not plant-mediated butterfly homogenization.** The 239-butterfly derived islands share plant-defined resource access; native HOSTS footprint is not the butterflies' actual native geographic range, endemicity or occupancy. Broad resource categories are enriched for larger known-host catalogues (24.4 vs 4.5 host taxa mean), the limited true GBIF breadth signal is weak, and fixed-marginal counterfactual explains most of the Jaccard increase. **Never title the finding "widespread butterflies have colonized" or "plants made actual insect communities homogeneous."**
- **The new 225×51 cumulative GBIF record index adds a genuine but incomplete observational layer:** the same historical species×island cells, not 51 independent repeated ecological censuses. Geographic/species/island margin-null gain +0.0072 after four chains may still reflect observer effort, taxonomy and missing early detection. The 225×51 and 239×126 panels are NOT co-located, so do not promote their separate positive directions into causal insect community change.
- **No double-counting across source populations** (239, 126/237 WCVP regions, 98 effort-covered, 22 trait-covered, 104 circuit-qualified, 51 butterfly landmasses, 155 predictive butterflies, 67 locality-use butterflies). Distinct denominator, background and uncertainty for every analysis.
- The 51-landmass target is **GBIF first *index***, not colonization. Plant GBIF indexing is not true plant arrival/absence. Region-level HOSTS opportunities omit performance, seasonality, competition and replacement effects.
- GBIF independent derived data DOI / provider attribution, restricted HOSTS rights and source-data long-term reproduction, Kew/WPI source permissions, coauthor agreement, final typesetting remain **open publication gates**. Do not hide these in supplementary if they materially qualify the main claim.

- **Main Results 3.5**: 155-butterfly climate/landcover-out-of-period record-prediction gain.
