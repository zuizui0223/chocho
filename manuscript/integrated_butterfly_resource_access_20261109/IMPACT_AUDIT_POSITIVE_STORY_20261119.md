## Decision
**The paper already contains many positive numbers but does not yet present its most interesting joint ecological finding as a single discovery.** The paper currently reads like a sequence of robust associations plus limitations. The conceptual center should be: **the same plant redistribution amplifies relative known-host opportunity where native coverage is sparse, yet its absolute additions are concentrated among butterfly species whose existing native host-resource geography is already broad.** These are *different denominators*, not a contradictory biological mechanism. Both responses are reconstructed from fixed HOSTS×WCVP source records; they are not evidence of increased butterfly abundance, competition or invasion.

## Overlooked higher-novelty hypothesis (already source-executed, NOT confirmatory)

[Source report](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/blob/main/provenance/reviewer_defenses/results/butterfly_host_plant_prominence_expansion_v0.1.json) and [script](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/blob/main/scripts/analyze_host_plant_prominence_expansion.py): 8,909 eligible plant hosts from an ORIGINAL *Lepidoptera*-wide network, 278 families (NOT the same as final 239 butterfly panel). Plants recorded as hosts of 1 Lepidoptera species gained WCVP introduced regions in **26.2%** of cases, whereas plants recorded as hosts of ≥6 species gained regions in **65.9%**. Source within-family × native-geographic-breadth-quintile rank association between recorded HOSTS plant consumer degree and introduced-range expansion **rho +0.295**, 4,999 stratified source permutations gave 0 exceeding absolute null.

**Interpretation gate:** This suggests source-network-prominent plants may be disproportionately redistributed, giving a promising nontrivial partner-selection axis. But **HOSTS recorded interaction degree combines ecological host use with sampling/reporting effort**; Poaceae and crop hosts may account for part of the effect. Without independent field/reporting-effort and crop/grass sensitivity, do NOT call it targeted movement of "ecological hubs" or an identified biological mechanism. This is a strong *next-test candidate*, not a result to promote uncritically into the lead abstract.

## A coherent, high-impact single-paper hook

**Source-supported ecological headline:**

> *Reallocating plants changes more than local plant richness: it changes which butterflies could find a familiar larval host. Islands show the largest proportional changes where documented native hosts were scarce, although absolute new opportunities concentrate in butterfly species with geographically widespread existing host resources. Ports and marine geography correspond to the plant supply layer, while independently recorded butterflies and named caterpillar observations reveal a smaller, spatially conditional biological response.*

**Use three predictions rather than six parallel claims:** (1) low native-host coverage amplifies proportional opportunity, not the fraction of gaps filled; (2) assembled introduced floras are spatially structured and concentrate source-known host identities; (3) potential host geography and recorded consumer geography share some information but the association need not transfer to every biogeographic division.

**Suggested title if ecological insight is prioritized:** *Native-host scarcity amplifies the redistribution of butterfly resource opportunities on islands*.  
**Suggested title if keeping ports and the sea visible:** *Between ports and the sea: unequal butterfly resource opportunities in two island floras*. This treats marine geometry as geographic context, not as proven a causal agent delivering plants.

**Suggested Results sequence (4 figures):**  
3.1 Native host baseline and the island resource paradox → Fig.1.  
3.2 Port-associated whole-alien supply and conditional sea geography → Fig.2.  
3.3 Concentration across host plants *and* butterflies with pre-existing resource breadth → revised Fig.3.  
3.4 Small, independent GBIF observational host information and a direct endemic larval-use example → revised Fig.4, with adverse cross-world case explicitly preserved.

**Suggested abstract opening in English (editorial, no new claims):**  
"Human redistribution of plants can alter where consumers encounter familiar resources without changes in diet. We show that potential larval-host opportunities expand particularly strongly on islands because documented native hosts cover fewer initial butterfly–region combinations, even though island introductions do not fill a greater share of missing host opportunities. The increase is highly unequal among contributing plants and butterfly species. Port-associated introduced floras and source-defined sea geography help describe the assembled plant supply, while independently recorded butterflies show smaller and geographically conditional correspondence."

Avoid "global butterfly food webs were rewired", "ports/sea caused new butterfly colonies", "specialists benefited more than generalists", "marine circuit confirms ocean currents", and "most new food is unused" — none are identified by these sources.
