# Supplementary Endemic Butterfly Methods and Results — one GEB working manuscript

## Target-population coverage and limits on transporting host-resource geography

To evaluate whether the geographic host-resource increases estimated for the original butterfly panel might be generalized to island-endemic butterflies, we defined a separate target population from independently assembled Macaronesian checklists, rather than conditioning species inclusion on NHM HOSTS. The checklist contained 42 distinct named butterfly taxa, including one historically extinct Madeiran taxon. Among the remaining 41 living taxa, 18 were independently classified as island endemics (13 Canary Islands, three Madeira and two Azores), and 23 were nonendemics.

We reconciled the checklist with the original LepTraits source, the frozen 339-species source panel and the final 239-species GEB panel, preserving documented name equivalences and excluding species-concept conflicts. None of the 18 extant endemic species occurred in either the 339- or 239-species panels, compared with 15/23 and 13/23 extant nonendemic species, respectively. Although 10/18 endemic species had a corresponding name in the original LepTraits data, none had all seven audited major trait attributes. Thus, selection into the original resource analysis had no observed overlap with the independently classified endemic target population. Inverse-probability reweighting of the original 239-species results cannot identify a mean effect in these 18 endemics.

In a complementary **source-ascertained sensitivity analysis**, we identified exact plant-species-level host records for eight of the 18 endemics (13 butterfly–plant links spanning 11 accepted plant species). Using the same version of the World Checklist of Vascular Plants (WCVP v13) and WGSRPD3 regions as the original reconstruction, four butterflies had additional contemporary host-plant regions and four had none **under their currently documented host lists**. This subset was selected for the availability of named host species and is not representative. More importantly, the number of additional resource regions, defined as the difference between the unions of contemporary and native host distributions, is not monotonic when previously unreported hosts are added: a newly documented native host can cover a region previously counted as introduced-only, whereas a newly documented introduced host can add a region. The observed four positives therefore do not furnish a mathematically valid nonzero lower bound on the full-diet response among all 18 endemics. Assuming, counterfactually, that the eight known host lists are complete yields a scenario range of four to 14 positives among 18 species; without that assumption, the target-population positive fraction remains unidentified.

This audit does not change the original aggregate **+54.9%** global resource-opportunity estimate or the **+118.9%** estimate for the original island sample. Both describe the original source-selected, known-host resource reconstruction. The independent endemic analysis establishes the need for comparable taxonomic, species-level larval-host and island-scale plant distribution evidence before any quantitative extrapolation to endemic butterflies. Field observations of alien-host use by *Pieris cheiranthi* on *Tropaeolum majus* on two Canary islands are reported as illustrative host-use evidence, **not** evidence of butterfly colonization or demographic benefit.

### Source selection, scope and incomplete foodplant lists

The original 239-species reconstruction contains 0/18 independently classified Macaronesian living endemics, even though 13/23 living nonendemics are represented. Exactly documented host-plant species were recoverable for 8/18 endemics under targeted literature search: *Pieris cheiranthi* (2→80 global source-plant regions), *Euchloe grancanariensis* (30→77), *E. hesperidum* (60→111; captive-only host evidence for one plant), and *Pararge xiphioides* (92→101; regional literature only) showed positive known-list botanical-region additions; the other four had zero on their **currently known host lists**. All eight had a documented native host in the original archipelago, and **zero** newly host-present home-archipelago regions were created. The four additional *global* plant-region counts are not biological range expansions of those four butterfly species.

This subset cannot identify the mean effect or even the positive-fraction lower bound among the 18 independently defined endemics: adding new documented native foodplants may reduce the introduced-only region increment and introducing previously missing alien host identities may increase it. The 4–14/18 scenario calculated under the *counterfactual complete-known-list* assumption is an illustrative sensitivity, **not** a frequentist confidence interval and not a valid unqualified biological proportion bound.
