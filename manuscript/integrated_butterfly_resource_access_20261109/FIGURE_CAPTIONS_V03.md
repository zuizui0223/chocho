# Main figure legends

## Figure 1 | Why island host coverage increased more

Native hosts initially covered fewer butterfly–region pairs on islands. A shows native coverage among all pairs; B shows how many pairs lacking native hosts gained an introduced host; C divides those new pairs by the number already covered by native hosts. The vertical scale changes in C. The comparison covers 239 butterfly species, 126 island regions and 237 mainland regions. Standardization weights the 15 shared area and biogeographic strata. [Regional counts and denominators](../../results/island_resource_gap_filling/pooled.csv).

## Figure 2 | Globally influential larval hosts in island port floras

The ten leading global contributors, ranked by their share of the 14,553 added butterfly–region pairs, and the number of island ports with a record of each plant in 2001–2010. Each row matches the same accepted plant name. Local introduced status, host abundance and larval feeding were not assessed. The dated inventory covers 38 port buffers of 5 km on 17 landmasses. Zero denotes a search without an indexed record. These 38 ports form a separate sample from the broad inventory of 1,596 ports. [Plant contributions and dated port counts](../../provenance/reviewer_defenses/results/geb_port_island_realization_20261031/GEB_top10_pre2011_port_plant_overlap.json).

## Figure 3 | Marine network resistance predicts introduced plant floras and later butterfly records

**A.** Percentage reduction in leave-one-island-out mean squared prediction error (MSE) after adding *undirected uniform-ocean effective resistance* to response-specific geographic baselines. WCVP v13 accepted native and introduced floras use exactly the same **104 botanical island regions / 5,355 unique pairs**, the same GSHHG finite-volume uniform-ocean circuit (nonport coastal terminals), fixed ridge penalty and corresponding richness/area/geographic controls; botanical circuit changes are **native −0.110%** (descriptive island-resampling 95% **−0.391 to +0.165%**) and **introduced +4.239%** (**+2.511 to +6.029%**). Their *within-island paired* relative-gain difference is **+4.349 percentage points** (descriptive 95% **+2.711 to +6.087**). The separate **51-landmass / 1,275-pair GBIF butterfly** analysis uses its own port-weighted circuit terminals, OLS baseline including earlier butterfly assemblage similarity and later first-indexed records; its gain is **+5.082%** (descriptive held-island resampling approximately **+1.36 to +8.30%**). Do not treat these three independently normalized effect sizes as matched organism-by-organism associations, three independent replications or plant-to-butterfly mediation.

**B.** On the matched 104-region **botanical-only** cohort, the alternative GSHHG **shortest-water path** predictor improves native/introduced Jaccard predictions by **+2.234% / +1.775%**, respectively, while contemporary WPI **port-count product** adds **−0.077% / +0.043%**. The circuit result differs from a single shortest path because effective resistance incorporates multiple connected water routes; neither measure is a historical current direction, propagule survival, a verified ship voyage or an introduction event. Native floras remain geographically structured despite the nonpositive additional *circuit* result. Multiple predictors and outcomes were inspected on the same observational data, so resampling intervals are descriptive, not preregistered confirmatory tests.
