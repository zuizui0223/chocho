# Main figure legends

## Figure 1 | Resource coverage in native and contemporary floras

Native hosts initially covered fewer butterfly–region pairs on islands. A shows native coverage among all pairs; B shows how many pairs lacking native hosts gained an introduced host; C divides those new pairs by the number already covered by native hosts. The vertical scale changes in C.

## Figure 2 | Port floras and sea-dispersal morphology

Port number and introduced richness across 126 island regions; paired prevalence of sea-dispersal morphology in native and introduced floras across 22 trait-covered regions.

## Figure 3 | Host reports and floristic expectations

Each contrast compares the same butterfly across geographic classes. Points show equal-species means and bars descriptive 95% species-bootstrap intervals.

## Figure 4 | Sea geometry and assemblage similarity

Relative reduction in island-held-out mean squared error from adding uniform ocean resistance. Whiskers give descriptive island-bootstrap 95% intervals. Native and introduced floras share 104 regions and 5,355 pairs; butterflies use a separate 51-landmass, 1,275-pair panel. The displayed botanical results are **one-island-held-out** comparisons. A separate, stricter **both-endpoints-unseen** test on 1,089 pairs retains +2.735% for introduced floras (fivefold descriptive interval −1.649 to +5.738%) and −0.044% for native floras (−0.898 to +0.433%); neither strict interval excludes zero. Undirected resistance does not measure a directional sea current or transport mechanism.
