# Main figure legends

## Figure 1 | Native host gaps amplify the proportional gain from introduced plants

Native hosts initially covered fewer butterfly–region pairs on islands (16.0%) than on mainlands (37.5%). A shows the native coverage baseline; B shows what fraction of initially uncovered combinations became covered by introduced known hosts (islands 22.7%; mainland 24.5%); C shows the much larger *relative* island resource gain (+118.9% versus +40.9%) because the starting coverage was low. The axis scale changes in C. These are potential host-covered combinations, not butterfly abundance or colonization.

## Figure 2 | Introduced floras covary with ports, while native plants retain sea-dispersal morphology

Contemporary registered-port abundance covaries with whole introduced vascular-plant richness across 126 island botanical regions, conditional on original area, native richness and broad biogeography (β=+0.221). A separate 22-region EuDiS-covered subset shows a median +2.95-percentage-point native-minus-introduced difference in plant morphologies consistent with sea dispersal. Different panels represent different islands. Port counts do not establish shipping origin, and morphology is not an observed current-driven seed arrival.

## Figure 3 | Who gains potential host resources, and do islands share butterfly consumer pools?

**A:** 239 butterflies are grouped into quartiles by the number of botanical regions with at least one of their **native known larval hosts**, NOT by the butterflies' observed native ranges. Among 5,741 new island potential host-covered combinations, the narrowest quarter received 644 (11.2%) and broadest quarter 2,271 (39.6%); the other two quarters received 1,077 (18.8%) and 1,749 (30.5%). The reference 25% line denotes equal allocation across approximately equally sized species groups. **B:** Across the same 7,797 island pairs with a defined native potential-consumer overlap, mean Jaccard similarity of butterflies with at least one regional known host rose from 0.140 under native-only hosts to 0.368 after including introduced hosts. These are **potential consumers**, not observed butterfly community homogenization. A constrained species-by-island margin null generates mean similarity increment 0.216 compared with observed 0.227; additionally retaining each butterfly's gains within Level-1 regions raises its expected increase to 0.224. The nulls are exploratory and dependent Markov-chain samples. The former host-report comparison is retained as **Supplementary Figure S14**, rather than reported as a conclusive island introduced-host preference.

## Figure 4 | Undirected marine connectivity predicts introduced floras and recorded butterfly communities

Relative reduction in island-held-out mean squared error from adding uniform ocean resistance. Whiskers give descriptive island-bootstrap 95% intervals. Native and introduced floras share 104 regions and 5,355 pairs; butterflies use a separate 51-landmass, 1,275-pair panel. The displayed botanical results are **one-island-held-out** comparisons. A separate, stricter **both-endpoints-unseen** test on 1,089 pairs retains +2.735% for introduced floras (fivefold descriptive interval −1.649 to +5.738%) and −0.044% for native floras (−0.898 to +0.433%); neither strict interval excludes zero. Undirected resistance does not measure a directional sea current or transport mechanism.
