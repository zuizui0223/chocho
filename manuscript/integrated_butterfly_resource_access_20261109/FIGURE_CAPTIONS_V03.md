## Figure 1 | Native-host scarcity, rather than exceptional gap filling, amplifies island resource opportunity

The source-verified three-panel [Figure 1 SVG](../../figures/integrated_island_port_butterfly_v03/Fig1_resource_geography_scales_v07.svg) contrasts **126 island** and **237 mainland WGSRPD3 botanical regions** for the fixed **239 butterfly species**. Panel A shows the share of all evaluated butterfly–region combinations with at least one **native** documented larval host (**16.0% islands** versus **37.5% mainlands**). Panel B displays the fraction of combinations originally unsupplied by native hosts which gained at least one introduced documented host (**22.7% versus 24.5%**). Panel C expresses the same introduced host additions relative to the original number of native-covered combinations (**+118.9% versus +40.9%**). The panels intentionally use different denominators; the Figure 1 percentage contrast must not be read as stronger island gap filling. The added bottom annotation shows that introduced-only hosts supply **54.3% (5,741/10,570)** of mapped contemporary island opportunities and **29.0% (8,686/29,901)** of mainland opportunities. The island–mainland absolute coverage gap falls from **21.4** to **17.7 percentage points**. This is source-defined **potential food geography**, not observed butterfly dispersal, successful feeding or population size.

## Figure 2 | Ports, island floras and mapped butterfly host opportunities

[Source-rendered Figure 2 SVG](../../figures/integrated_island_port_butterfly_v03/Fig2_ports_native_alien_floras_readable_v07.svg) contains six panels using **separate source populations** where indicated.

**A. Same-island port and botanical flora associations.** For the same **126 botanical island regions**, estimated WPI port count coefficients (per log2(1+ports)) for log1p accepted native or introduced WCVP whole-flora richness, adjusted for island area and TDWG Level1: introduced **+0.2854** (descriptive HC3 95% interval **+0.0622,+0.5087**), native **+0.0954** (**−0.0774,+0.2683**). The directly estimated introduced-minus-native slope is **+0.1900** (**−0.0187,+0.3987**); its interval crosses zero. These are source-observational botanical responses, not ship cargo or origin-selective introduction effects.

**B. Same-island native and introduced floristic similarity.** Among **7,875 region pairs** formed by the same 126 WCVP island regions, median accepted-species Jaccard was **0.004487 native** and **0.046030 introduced** (approximately **10.26 times** the native median). This is a contemporary composition comparison of different plant species pools, **not a tenfold time change**, a butterfly community effect or a ratio of shipping to natural dispersal.

**C. Source-original marine-circuit prediction plus geographically strict uncertainty.** A **separate cohort of 104 eligible island botanical regions** had **5,355 source-defined native/introduced flora pairs**. One-island-heldout source electrical-resistance model provided **+4.239% introduced-flora** versus **−0.110% native-flora** relative MSE improvement beyond its original geographical/richness baseline; descriptive bootstrap intervals were approximately **+2.51,+6.03%** introduced and **−0.39,+0.17%** native. The source-locked stronger **both-islands-unseen** test evaluated **1,089 test pairs in five fixed held-island folds**, obtaining introduced **+2.735%** (descriptive fivefold bootstrap **−1.649,+5.508%**) and native **−0.044%** (**−0.984,+0.431%**), with **3/5** folds positive for each origin class. In the stricter source test the introduced-minus-native gain interval crossed zero; the undirected **uniform-ocean electrical resistance** is not observed ocean currents or historical ship/seed routes. A distinct shortest-water-path model helped both origins under separate source constraints. Source: [strict 104-island marine receipt](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/blob/main/results/WCVP126_circuit_strict_geographic_transfer_20261118/VERIFIED_STRICT_CIRCUIT_SCORE.json).

**D. Nearest-mainland distance and potential known-host opportunities.** This panel uses the *same* 126 WGSRPD3 botanical islands but a different mainland-proximity model than the 104-island sea circuit. For fixed **239** butterflies, the x-axis is approximate mainland boundary distance in km, displayed as log(1 + distance), and fitted y-values count species with at least one **mapped** native or contemporary larval host, **not** observed butterflies. The native-host curve counts species with at least one native known host; the contemporary curve includes any native or introduced known host, so the two counts are nested. Native-host distance slope **β=−0.1676** (HC3 p=0.000095; nine-TDWG-Level1 clustered t8 p=0.014), contemporary slope **β=+0.0192** (HC3 p=0.751). The paired standardized slope shift **β=+0.1868** (HC3 p=0.0011) is uncertain under nine-region clustering (p=0.0677; t8 95% interval −0.0172,+0.3907). A **separate introduced-only coverage share log-odds model** for 123 resource-active islands yielded distance β=+0.4251 (nine-cluster p=0.014); equal-island adjusted shares at 100/1,000/4,000 km were **55%/66%/72%**, not ratios of the count curves. Controls were port count, area, absolute latitude and TDWG Level1. Source: [independent fixed239 reconstruction](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/actions/runs/44672145927) and `results/isolation_port_resource_reanalysis_20261120/SOURCE239_PAPER_RECEIPT_V01.json`. Approximate distance, nine clusters, and log ports versus log area correlation (r=0.807) limit causal interpretation. Original 125-island/2,517-species scratchpad files were unavailable and are not claimed as independently reproduced.

**Spatial-dependence sensitivity for panel D (Appendix S3.3).** The original 126-island regressions showed positive within-Level1 residual Moran's I in the native-host and introduced-only-share models (p=0.001 and p=0.003 under 2,000-km proximity). A separate ten-eigenvector MEM filter retained negative native-host mainland-distance β=−0.133 (HC3 p=0.020) and positive introduced-only share distance β=+0.395 (p<0.001). The more conservative nine-Level1 cluster t8 test of the paired native/current slope shift remains uncertain (p=0.068). Spatial filters are not independent biological validation or confirmed mitigation of all spatial autocorrelation.

**E. Native host taxonomic resolution.** For the same 126 regions and 239 butterflies, standardized distance slopes are shown separately for family, genus and documented host-species coverage: −0.0126, −0.0928 and −0.1676. Solid teal points are base-model slopes; open orange points add MEM10, with nine-Level1 clustered t8 intervals in both cases. The direct species-minus-family contrast is −0.155 (p = 0.008; MEM10 + cluster p = 0.059). Uncertain family or genus slopes do not establish absence of their isolation gradients. A higher-rank presence is a taxonomic match, not demonstrated suitability. Source: Appendix S3.6.

**F. Independent morphological context, not another measured circuit component.** In **22 eligible botanically matched islands**, the EuDiS source-native flora had **+2.9515 percentage points** higher **coded seawater-dispersal morphology** than introduced flora (descriptive region resampling +2.0395 to +4.5323; 17 positive, four negative, one tie). This trait measure is biased toward European plants and does not directly establish ocean transport. Full source [EuDiS record](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/blob/main/results/eudis_thalasso_wcvp_gate_20261116/README_JA.md).

## Figure 3 | New food opportunities are concentrated in a few plants and unequally distributed across butterflies

[New source-verified Figure 3 SVG](../../figures/integrated_island_port_butterfly_v03/Fig3_global_host_contributors_at_ports_v07.svg).

**A. Host species contributions.** Among **670** accepted introduced documented larval-host plant contributors to the 239-butterfly source **14,553 added butterfly–botanical-region combinations**, the smallest group supplying half of added units included **38 plant species (5.7%)**. Source contribution credits are split equally among all contributing known-host taxa for each newly covered butterfly–region unit; thus the source attribution does not double-count the original 14,553. The weighted plant-contributor Gini was **0.7774**, and the top ten accounted for **25.10%**. The cumulative-share plot displays **only exact source-verified top-k anchor summaries**, connected by visual guides on a logarithmic k-axis; it does not imply new measurement of intermediate plant ranks. Original source: [HOSTS contribution concentration receipt](https://github.com/zuizui0223/chocho/blob/main/provenance/reviewer_defenses/results/butterfly_host_contribution_concentration_v0.1.json).

**B. Original all-species native-resource geography versus newly supplied island resource combinations.** Every point corresponds to a documented butterfly species in the **original n=239** source panel. Its x-coordinate is the number of **global botanical regions with native documented hosts** (the original species source file); y-coordinate is the number of **newly covered island botanical regions** among the original 126. Their tied-rank **Spearman ρ=+0.578896**; the top **59** species by native-resource geography contributed **2,271/5,741=39.56%** of newly supplied island combinations, and the bottom **60** contributed **644/5,741=11.22%**. The global native partner range is not a butterfly's dispersal ability, and absolute opportunities should not be compared with relative resource gain or with observed butterfly abundance.

**C. Broad reach across originally recorded host-family specialization.** **82 of 97** butterflies catalogued as having exactly one larval-host plant family gained at least one new global potential host region (**84.5%**). The global total was **206/239 butterflies (86.2%)**, so the result demonstrates broad opportunity gain including source-defined one-family butterflies, **not a greater benefit than in broader-diet butterflies**. Removing FAO crop hosts retained approximately **+46–47%** source-global potential resource expansion. Species names and exact source denominators are tied to the original fixed [239-butterfly metrics CSV](../../data/frozen/figure_sources/anthropogenic_species_metrics.csv) and [original butterfly island additions](../../results/island_resource_gap_filling/by_species.csv).

**D. Pooled island gap filling across butterfly native-resource breadth.** The original 239 butterflies were grouped by global native-resource extent (60/60/60/59; ties resolved alphabetically). Added units divided by initially empty island units yield 644/7,221 (8.9%), 1,077/6,850 (15.7%), 1,749/6,322 (27.7%) and 2,271/4,892 (46.4%). This is descriptive gap filling, not a comparison of feeding success, dispersal or establishment; the upper boundary tie sensitivity is retained in Appendix S2.

**Archived historical port overlap plot retained as supplemental source.** The original [ten-leading-hosts versus early 38 port-buffer source plot](../../figures/integrated_island_port_butterfly_v03/Fig2_global_contributors_at_ports.svg) remains unchanged and linked to the pre-restructured manuscript. Eight of the ten top plants had at least one 2001–2010 observation near one of the original sampled ports (17 landmass groups), but the 38-buffer historical sample does not represent the separate 1,596 queried ports and neither establishes ship delivery nor field larval use.

## Figure 4 | Independent recorded butterfly signals and regional limits

Newly composed [Figure 4 SVG](../../figures/integrated_island_port_butterfly_v03/Fig4_recorded_butterfly_isolation_monarch_v07.svg). Panels A and B show observational correspondence with mapped host opportunities; panels C–F show relative reductions in held-out MSE, with distinct populations and model baselines. Positive MSE reduction indicates improvement; negative indicates worse predictions.

**A. Independent recorded butterfly–region combinations and mapped introduced-only host shares.** Each orange dot represents a raw recorded introduced-only share among combinations with mapped hosts in the same 84 record-active island regions (224 shared butterflies, 2001–2024); dot size scales with its recorded denominator. The orange solid and teal dashed curves are back-transformed fitted log odds at mean standardized covariates and mean division composition, from the source-equivalent equal-region models for observed and potential shares on those same 84 regions. These fits use the continuity-corrected log odds log((introduced-only + 0.5)/(native-host + 0.5)), standardized log1p mainland distance, port count, area, absolute latitude, Level1 effects and recording activity. The corresponding standardized distance coefficients are +0.439 (eight-division clustered p = 0.0396) and +0.257 (p = 0.0368). These curves are not the separate 239-species count and share models of Figure 2D. Raw pooled nearest/farthest quartile shares are 70/612 (11.4%) and 27/44 (61.4%); 32/32 and 12/32 regions contribute, respectively. Effort-restricted models are uncertain; the observed-minus-potential contrast is also uncertain (Appendix S5.5). No butterfly presence or feeding event is inferred from the host map.

**B. Monarch and endemic examples.** The 42 botanical regions in which *Danaus plexippus* has only mapped introduced hosts are shown at source geography representative points, not occurrence coordinates or individual islands. Filled symbols identify the 20 with an indexed butterfly record (18 with records in multiple years); open symbols indicate no indexed record in this archive, not absence. Symbols may overlap, and Hawaii is highlighted as the historical example (Scudder, 1875). The schematic GSHHG land outlines are simplified cartographic context (Basemap-data 2.0.0), not an analytic marine connectivity surface; source attribution and map reuse clearance remain required. The text box separately reports independently documented *Pieris cheiranthi* larval feeding on introduced *Tropaeolum majus* and the literature-reported native *Crambe strigosa* host. No third-party photographs, transport routes or colonization chronology are shown. See Appendices S5.5 and S6.

**C. Independent later GBIF record indexing, 155 butterflies.** Source-verified species-equal MSE reduction for M5 (known-host geography and host-family composition) relative to M3 (prior/indexing/environment background) on **208,482 butterfly×cell test cases**: **+4.12%** on temporal holdout, **+3.00%** on spatial-block holdout and **+2.74%** for tested cells at least 100 km from training. The distant evaluation is a subset of the spatial validation, not a third independent fit. Predictor plant maps are contemporary rather than independently frozen before butterfly evaluation periods.

**D. Separate independently recorded community composition, 224 butterflies / 169 WGSRPD3 regions / 14,196 region pairs.** Relative MSE reduction when M3 adds known-HOSTS larval-host alien flora Jaccard **H** to M2, which already contains unlisted introduced alien plant composition **O** plus broad source geography, flora, climate, past butterfly similarity and recorded observation-effort covariates. Source-original regional-heldout increment **+0.0913%**. After within-region period-wide **datasetKey×calendar-day** or **indexed-record-count** observation-intensity proxy thinning of **both previous and later** GBIF recorded communities, the median 29-draw increment is **+0.215%** and **+0.438%**. These small positive results do not measure abundance, larval use, true occupancy or established immigration.

**E. Entirely unseen broad biogeographic divisions.** With each full TDWG Level1 held out from source training (eight world divisions), the *world-equal mean-MSE* H-after-O source increment is **+0.097%** unthinned and median **+0.695% / +0.779%** after the two proxy-thinning designs. The horizontal whiskers for the thinned source medians depict **2.5–97.5% conditional Monte Carlo quantiles across 29 simulations**, **not ecological population confidence intervals**. Only **5–7 of eight** divisions improve within a simulated source draw, so the average does **not** show universal transfer.

**F. South America: compact cross-biogeographic transfer counterexample.** The inset intentionally shows only the **2,980 South-America-to-other-world dyads** from a 20-region entirely unseen South American world (original complete test 3,170 dyads). Median H-after-O MSE gains in the original 29-draw effort proxy runs are **−4.036%** (datasetKey×calendar-day, descriptive conditional Monte Carlo 95% −7.268 to −1.473%) and **−2.697%** (record-count proxy, −8.043 to −0.137%). A textual inset note retains within-South-America (190 dyads) medians **−0.496%** and **−0.126%**; the full original four-subgroup figure, including all uncertainty bars and all four scores, is **preserved as [Supplementary Figure S.G-D](../../figures/integrated_island_port_butterfly_v03/Fig4_independent_butterfly_record_predictability_full_south_america_v04.svg)**. The negative cross-world result indicates a specific documented prediction-calibration failure, not a general inability of South American butterflies to feed on introduced plants. Whiskers depict the original conditional Monte Carlo quantiles, including negative prediction outcomes.

**Scope guard:** The source GBIF recorded community observations were derived from one opportunistic occurrence archive (DOI **10.15468/dl.umqmsf**), not standardized repeat surveys. Both simulated observation-effort proxies depend on the same indexed observations, the source-original later observation-effort/count covariates are held fixed and all Monte Carlo simulations overlap in observations. These figures cannot identify historical plant deliveries by ships, novel butterfly colonization, improved recruitment, biological homogenization or causal benefit from alien hosts.
