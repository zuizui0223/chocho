# Main figure legends

## Figure 1 | Why island host coverage increased more

Native hosts initially covered fewer butterfly–region pairs on islands. A shows native coverage among all pairs; B shows how many pairs lacking native hosts gained an introduced host; C divides those new pairs by the number already covered by native hosts. The vertical scale changes in C. The comparison covers 239 butterfly species, 126 island regions and 237 mainland regions. Standardization weights the 15 shared area and biogeographic strata. [Regional counts and denominators](../../results/island_resource_gap_filling/pooled.csv).

## Figure 2 | Globally influential larval hosts in island port floras

The ten leading global contributors, ranked by their share of the 14,553 added butterfly–region pairs, and the number of island ports with a record of each plant in 2001–2010. Each row matches the same accepted plant name. Local introduced status, host abundance and larval feeding were not assessed. The dated inventory covers 38 port buffers of 5 km on 17 landmasses. Zero denotes a search without an indexed record. These 38 ports form a separate sample from the broad inventory of 1,596 ports. [Plant contributions and dated port counts](../../provenance/reviewer_defenses/results/geb_port_island_realization_20261031/GEB_top10_pre2011_port_plant_overlap.json).

## Figure 3 | Known-host geography improves prediction of butterfly records for known species

Species-equally weighted changes in the mean squared error (MSE; left) and mean absolute error (MAE; right) of a GBIF-recording-probability model after adding known larval-host resource and host-family information. Negative changes indicate reduced error (improvement). The comparison is **M5 host resources and family composition minus M3 background, climate and land cover**, based on 155 butterflies and 208,482 test species–cell combinations in the main evaluation. Points show source-archived differences; horizontal lines are descriptive 95% fixed-prediction intervals from 2,000 crossed butterfly-species × spatial-block resamples. The time-held comparison reduced MSE by 4.12%, the held-spatial comparison by 3.00%, and the nested ≥100-km spatial subset by 2.74%. The model did not demonstrably improve for butterflies withheld from model fitting (MSE difference interval crosses zero). Four quality/source sensitivity comparisons reuse the source cohort. Contemporary host geography may postdate butterfly records, so the figure depicts **retrospective recording prediction**, not historical host introduction, actual abundance, feeding, occupancy, movement or island establishment. ## Figure 4 | Marine geography informs recorded island assemblages, but strict geographic transfer is limited

Both panels show **relative MSE reductions** in butterfly-community predictions from source-locked retrospective comparisons; positive values mean lower error, negative values mean worse predictions. Panel A: across **51 held-island models** fitted to butterfly-community Jaccard similarities over **1,275 dependent island pairs**, including uniform-ocean Circuitscape resistance in a distance-based baseline reduced MSE by **5.08%** (33/51 islands improved). In a separate joint source analysis, adding contemporary larval-host assemblage information beyond AIS and wind proxies improved MSE by **1.73%**, while adding AIS and wind beyond host assemblage data improved MSE by **6.49%**. Their denominators and reference predictors differ, so the three percentages **must not be summed or interpreted as independent causal components**. Panel B: under the stricter cross-community model where **both endpoints of 990 island pairs were geographically held out**, adding AIS to a retrospectively host-adjusted later butterfly assemblage predictor improved MSE by just **0.68%**, whereas adding AIS **and** wind worsened it by **4.70%**. Source islands, sampling support and train/test geometry differ from Panel A, so these are not matched replications or formal effect-size contrasts. Modern AIS and wind variables are conditional geographic predictors, **not actual migration, shipping trajectories, historical plant delivery or butterfly recruitment**. Neither panel establishes arrival or successful host use.
