# Main figure legends

## Figure 1 | Why island host coverage increased more

Native hosts initially covered fewer butterfly–region pairs on islands. A shows native coverage among all pairs; B shows how many pairs lacking native hosts gained an introduced host; C divides those new pairs by the number already covered by native hosts. The vertical scale changes in C. The comparison covers 239 butterfly species, 126 island regions and 237 mainland regions. Standardization weights the 15 shared area and biogeographic strata. [Regional counts and denominators](../../results/island_resource_gap_filling/pooled.csv).

## Figure 2 | Globally influential larval hosts in island port floras

The ten leading global contributors, ranked by their share of the 14,553 added butterfly–region pairs, and the number of island ports with a record of each plant in 2001–2010. Each row matches the same accepted plant name. Local introduced status, host abundance and larval feeding were not assessed. The dated inventory covers 38 port buffers of 5 km on 17 landmasses. Zero denotes a search without an indexed record. These 38 ports form a separate sample from the broad inventory of 1,596 ports. [Plant contributions and dated port counts](../../provenance/reviewer_defenses/results/geb_port_island_realization_20261031/GEB_top10_pre2011_port_plant_overlap.json).
