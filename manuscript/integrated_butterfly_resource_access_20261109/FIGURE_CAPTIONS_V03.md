# Main figure legends

## Figure 1 | Why island host coverage increased more

Native hosts initially covered fewer butterfly–region pairs on islands. A shows native coverage among all pairs; B shows how many pairs lacking native hosts gained an introduced host; C divides those new pairs by the number already covered by native hosts. The vertical scale changes in C. The comparison covers 239 butterfly species, 126 island regions and 237 mainland regions. Standardization weights the 15 shared area and biogeographic strata. [Regional counts and denominators](../../results/island_resource_gap_filling/pooled.csv).

## Figure 2 | Globally influential larval hosts in island port floras

The ten leading global contributors, ranked by their share of the 14,553 added butterfly–region pairs, and the number of island ports with a record of each plant in 2001–2010. Each row matches the same accepted plant name. Local introduced status, host abundance and larval feeding were not assessed. The dated inventory covers 38 port buffers of 5 km on 17 landmasses. Zero denotes a search without an indexed record. These 38 ports form a separate sample from the broad inventory of 1,596 ports. [Plant contributions and dated port counts](../../provenance/reviewer_defenses/results/geb_port_island_realization_20261031/GEB_top10_pre2011_port_plant_overlap.json).

## Figure 3 | Known-host geography improves prediction of butterfly records for known species

Species-equally weighted changes in the mean squared error (MSE; left) and mean absolute error (MAE; right) of a GBIF-recording-probability model after adding known larval-host resource and host-family information. Negative changes indicate reduced error (improvement). The comparison is **M5 host resources and family composition minus M3 background, climate and land cover**, based on 155 butterflies and 208,482 test species–cell combinations in the main evaluation. Points show source-archived differences; horizontal lines are descriptive 95% fixed-prediction intervals from 2,000 crossed butterfly-species × spatial-block resamples. The time-held comparison reduced MSE by 4.12%, the held-spatial comparison by 3.00%, and the nested ≥100-km spatial subset by 2.74%. The model did not demonstrably improve for butterflies withheld from model fitting (MSE difference interval crosses zero). Four quality/source sensitivity comparisons reuse the source cohort. Contemporary host geography may postdate butterfly records, so the figure depicts **retrospective recording prediction**, not historical host introduction, actual abundance, feeding, occupancy, movement or island establishment. [Original source figure (file frozen and digest-validated)](../../provenance/reviewer_defenses/results/host_forecast_validation_20261029/forecast_validation_transfer.png); [original quantitative comparison](../../provenance/reviewer_defenses/results/host_forecast_validation_20261029/forecast_paired_comparisons.csv).

## Figure 4 | Two island floristic layers, matched port associations and conditional marine geometry

**Four panels, intentionally different estimands and source populations.** (A) **Port–plant richness associations.** On the same **126 strict WGSRPD3 island botanical regions**, the coefficients for log2(1 + WPI ports) adjusted for region area and TDWG Level-1 strata are **β = +0.0954** for log(1 + native accepted plant species) (HC3 descriptive 95% interval **−0.0774 to +0.2683**) and **β = +0.2854** for introduced flora (**+0.0622 to +0.5087**). The paired difference is **+0.1900** (**−0.0187 to +0.3987**) and is **not a proven between-origin interaction**. The separate **98-region**, botanical-effort-adjusted introduced-flora coefficient is **+0.2445**, while extra known host-taxon richness beyond all alien flora is **−0.0415**. These contrasts are regional associations with differently conditioned predictors, **not plant importation or historical shipping data**. [Same-design 126/123-region source verifier](../../scripts/verify_island_port_native_alien_matched_20261118.py).

(B) **Morphological seawater-dispersal traits, not actual arrival.** In **22** same-region native/introduced source-eligible EuDiS comparisons, native minus alien thalassochorous morphology median **+2.95 percentage points** (descriptive source 95% interval +2.04 to +4.53); 17 native-higher, four alien-higher, one tie. EuDiS primarily records European species; this is **not** a global flora estimate. Independently published Tonga 2011 broad **water-dispersal** proportions have a different trait definition, source population and sign; they cannot be overplotted as thalassochory. [EuDiS source result](../../results/eudis_thalasso_wcvp_gate_20261116/README_JA.md); [Tonga external-validity audit](../../results/tropical_dispersal_source_bridge_20261118/README.md).

(C) **Native and alien floristic similarity: two different marine predictors.** Archived **104-botanical-region** uniform-ocean **Circuitscape effective resistance** improves held-region pairwise *introduced*-flora Jaccard prediction by **+4.239% MSE**, with a **−0.110%** native-flora change under its source baseline. A separately reconstructed **GSHHG shortest-water-path** comparison on **5,355 identical native/introduced source pairs** improves **both native +2.234% and introduced +1.775%**. The latter gains are smaller and uncertain in stricter both-endpoints-unseen tests. A water-path distance is **not** an electrical-circuit model and neither is a direction-resolved ocean current or actual ship route. **New source-locked geographically stricter check of the original electrical descriptor:** both endpoints absent from training across 1,089 fivefold test pairs, **introduced +2.735%** / **native −0.044%** MSE change, with descriptive fivefold intervals introduced **[−1.649,+5.508%]**, native **[−0.984,+0.431%]**, and the source-paired introduced-minus-native gain **+2.779 points [−1.693,+5.400]**. All strict intervals cross zero. Panel C displays the **initial one-island-out** source figures; these strict external-geography scores are numerical sensitivity results, not changes to plotted data. Source-locked *zero-floor/overlap-definition* checks found similar small circuit gains for binary shared-species presence (native +0.418%, alien +0.331%), but Jaccard/Sørensen favor introduced flora and source-defined Simpson overlap does not (native +1.586%, alien +0.898%, only 1,043 strict pairs). All are numerical, post-result metric sensitivities based on the **same WCVP species inventories**, not separate confirmations. [Original-source floor audit](../../results/WCVP126_circuit_floor_diagnostic_20261118/README_JA.md). (D) **Recorded butterfly community, more demanding transfer.** On **990 geographically both-endpoints-unseen source-eligible pairs**, adding an AIS marine predictor to the source-specific geography, host and botanical-recording baseline improves retrospective later butterfly-composition Jaccard prediction by **+0.6776%** MSE, while adding AIS and wind jointly **worsens it −4.6986%**. The originally positive selected 51-island held-island **uniform-ocean circuit +5.082%** and a separate conditional **AIS/wind +6.488%** result remain retained in Supplement S.F.
